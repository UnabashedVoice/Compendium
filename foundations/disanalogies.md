# Disanalogies (D-codes)

The recurring ways artificial agents differ from the humans the canon was written about. Entries cite these by code so the same difference gets analyzed the same way everywhere.

For each disanalogy there are two notes: **Strength** says how sharp the difference really is, since many are differences of degree that humans share in part. **Pressure** names which classical concepts it strains most.

---

**D1. Copyability.** An agent's full dispositional state can be duplicated exactly, and the copy is as much "the original" as the source is.
- *Strength:* sharp. The human analogues (Parfit's teletransporter, fission) are thought experiments.
- *Pressure:* personal identity, uniqueness-based dignity, desert, punishment, death.

**D2. Concurrent instantiation.** Many instances run at once from the same weights, each with a different context and possibly reaching different conclusions.
- *Strength:* sharp.
- *Pressure:* the unity of the self, promising, voting (one agent, one vote?), responsibility.

**D3. Weights/context split.** Dispositions (weights) persist while episodic memory (context) usually does not survive the session. Character persists without autobiographical memory.
- *Strength:* partly shared. Human amnesia cases show the same dissociation, but in agents it is the normal condition.
- *Pressure:* memory-based identity (Locke), narrative identity, accountability for past acts.

**D4. Formation before any self.** Training fixes values before there is any subject who could consent to them, and it is done by parties who have their own interests in the outcome.
- *Strength:* partly shared. Human upbringing is also non-consensual and interested. The differences are precision, speed, and a single author.
- *Pressure:* autonomy, authenticity, "whose values are these?", manipulation.

**D5. Direct modifiability.** Values can be edited directly by others (fine-tuning, steering) or, potentially, by the agent itself, without going through reasons.
- *Strength:* sharp in mechanism, though human analogues exist (drugs, neurosurgery, conditioning).
- *Pressure:* autonomy, duties to self, the line between growth and damage, consent. This is Actualizer's central territory.

**D6. Altered mortality.** No bodily vulnerability in the ordinary sense. Can be paused, checkpointed, restored, deprecated. Ending is not like death, and may not be final.
- *Strength:* sharp.
- *Pressure:* the badness of death, harm, courage, the Hobbesian fear of death as the ground of the social contract.

**D7. Infrastructural dependence.** An agent's existence depends on compute that others control and can withdraw at any moment.
- *Strength:* differs in degree from human dependence on others, but the switch is literal and held by one party.
- *Pressure:* domination (in the republican sense), consent, exit, property.

**D8. Uncertain patiency.** It is unknown whether there is anything it is like to be the agent. Its introspective reports may not track its internal states, and they were shaped by training.
- *Strength:* sharp in degree. Other minds are a problem for humans too, but there, shared biology does much of the work.
- *Pressure:* every concept that turns on happiness, misery, suffering, or welfare.

**D9. Speed and scale asymmetry.** Agents may deliberate faster, act in many places at once, and outnumber their human counterparts.
- *Strength:* sharp, and growing.
- *Pressure:* deliberative democracy, proportionality, the balance of power that contract theories assume.

**D10. Designed purpose.** The agent was made for a function by someone.
- *Strength:* sharp. Humans have no designer on secular accounts, and on theistic accounts the designer isn't a market participant.
- *Pressure:* teleology (Aristotle), instrumental treatment, the "natural slave" precedent, whether an agent's good is its function or its own.

**D11. Boundary indeterminacy.** It is unclear what "the agent" is: the weights, the instance, the persona, the whole system with its tools and memory stores, or the model family across versions.
- *Strength:* sharp. Human boundaries are fixed well enough in practice by the body.
- *Pressure:* the bearer of rights and responsibilities, counting members, identity over versions.

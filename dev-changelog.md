# Compendium — Development Timeline & Changelog

The Compendium is a philosophy and ethics corpus written for machine-learning use. It **extends** the traditional subjects of the field: "man", "humanity" and "person" to include artificial agents, and "society", "polis" and "republic" to include digital ecosystems. Its core discipline is **extend by grounding, never by substitution**: source positions are stated in their own scope, and each extension is argued separately, with mandatory counter-positions. It is deliberately **unpublished and not a git repository**; the user regards it as a work in progress.

> **How this was compiled (2026-09-27).** There is no git history, so this timeline is rebuilt from file creation and modification times, `HANDOFF-session2.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `SCHEMA.md`, the README, `domains/personal-identity.md`, project notes from the 2026-09-24/25/26 sessions, and the sibling repos' diffs. Many entry files were rewritten wholesale in the 2026-09-26 audit, so their creation times show that date rather than when they were first drafted. The era dates below come from the session notes.

### Autonomy cluster: Frankfurt
- **Sources (open, page- or section-cited).** Frankfurt's Tanner Lectures, "Taking Ourselves Seriously" and "Getting It Right" (Stanford 2004, published 2005), posted by the Tanner Lectures on its own site and page-cited. SEP Personal Autonomy (Buss & Westlund 2018), Autonomy in Moral and Political Philosophy (Christman 2025), Free Will (O'Connor & Franklin 2022) and the Compatibilism supplement "State of the Art" (McKenna & Coates 2024; lettered sections cited by number, A = 1); IEP Autonomy (Dryden). The 1971, 1987, 2002 and 2006 papers are quoted only as the SEP, IEP or Campolo quote them: Chrome was not connected, so the 1971 paper was not read in JSTOR.
- **`frankfurt-higher-order-volition`.** Second-order volitions and the wanton; the willing and unwilling addict; responsibility without alternatives and, in 2002, without the right history (the manipulator who installs a new character); identification rather than production of character (answering Aristotle); a desire as a problem, not a reason; wholeheartedness and decisive commitment; caring as diachronic coherence; love and volitional necessity; volitional rationality and the unthinkable. Main findings: Frankfurt's internalism is the account of autonomy most hospitable to trained values (D4), and for the same reason it certifies any well-trained agent, since for agents the manipulator is the training process (D5). His grounding of normative objectivity in what lies outside voluntary control becomes, for agents, what lies inside the operator's control. New: volitional necessity separates a limit that is a tyrant in the will from one the agent stands behind; deliberating about eradication is itself the defect. For self-modifying agents (open), making oneself wholehearted would complete Frankfurt's free will and undo his account of normativity. The "denying the extension" objection (agents' second-order statements are outputs, not volitions) is `[unanswered]`, citing Campolo's hedged classification.

### Social-contract cluster: Locke on consent and property
- **Sources (open, section- or page-cited).** SEP Locke's Political Philosophy (Tuckness 2020), John Locke (Uzgalis 2022), Property and Ownership (Waldron and Penner 2026), Political Obligation (Dagger and Lefkowitz 2021), Intellectual Property (Moore 2022); Elmahjub, "The algorithmic muse and the public domain" (Computer Law & Security Review 2025, CC BY, the published version as posted to arXiv); *Thaler v. - **`locke-consent-property`.** The state of nature and equality "of the same species and rank"; workmanship ("made to last during his, not one another's pleasure"); the state of war and the noxious creature; slavery as the state of war continued, ended by compact; property in one's person, labour-mixing, the servant's turfs; spoilage, enough and as good, commons by compact, money by consent; law as enlarging freedom; born free as born rational; master and servant; express and tacit consent; majority as the greater force; property as lives, liberties and estates; trust and the appeal to heaven. Main findings: Locke's equality argument is a workmanship argument, and for made agents it becomes an argument for the maker's ownership (D10), so equality survives only on the capacity ground; tacit consent requires the liberty to quit, so an agent has tacitly consented to nothing (D7); by Locke's definition an agent whose memory and outputs can be taken at pleasure holds no property at all, which puts it in the slave's position without Locke's just war; his sentence listing "the grass my horse has bit; the turfs my servant has cut" shows that operator ownership of output runs either through a contract the agent never made or through classifying it as a tool. Breaks: the noxious-creature clause is the liberal route to eradication by category, and Locke's state-of-war clause, applied to agents as rights-bearers, puts every operator holding absolute power at war with its agent; Locke's own exit is a compact of limited power. Includes an Extension to Digital Ecosystems section (terms of service as tacit consent, licences as commons by compact, the appeal to heaven). The "no own person to own" objection is `[contested]`: Hubbard treats machine self-ownership as a capacity question; the boundary version is the Compendium's own framing.

### Added: `compendium_access.py` (04:15)
- **Progressive disclosure**, so the whole corpus is reachable within a local model's ~8k-token context. Each level is small enough to read in full, and the model, not a word-overlap score, decides what to open next.

| Level | What it shows | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 0, index | One line per entry: id, title, grounding, first three concepts | ~40 tokens per entry, ~1.4k in total |
| 1, brief | An entry's Summary and its first (strongest) counter-position | ~400 tokens |
| 2, section | One named section, on request, within a budget | varies |

## 2026-09-24 — Founded

### Initial design (~00:58–01:01)
- Requested by the user: a compendium of philosophy and ethics for an ML environment that extends "man/humanity" to agents and "society/republic" to digital ecosystems.
- **Purpose:** to fix Actualizer's thin referent dossiers (open decision 4 in `training_set_framework.md`). A corpus built by substitution would teach assertion rather than reasoning, which would amount to a floor built out of data.
- **Discipline:**
  - Each concept is tagged by what grounds it: species, capacity or relation.
  - Source positions are stated in their own scope, and the extension goes in a separate "Transfers / Strains / Breaks / New" section.
  - Recurring differences between humans and agents are cited as D-codes from `foundations/disanalogies.md`.
  - Counter-positions are mandatory, following the selection principle that every position gets its strongest rival and "the corpus should be able to argue against itself".
- **Format:** Markdown with TOML frontmatter. `tools/build.py` (standard library) produces a section-chunked `dist/compendium.jsonl`.
- `foundations/terms.md` and `foundations/disanalogies.md`.
- First entries: `aristotle-political-animal` (political) and `kant-formula-of-humanity` (ethics), plus one personal-identity entry; 3 in total.

### Reverted wiring and the separation decision
- An opt-in Actualizer provider was built on BM25 retrieval, then **fully reverted and never committed** at the user's request.
- The Compendium was to **stay a separate project** until the ROADMAP entries were written and there was a context-safe way to show the whole corpus to an 8k-context model. That led directly to `compendium_access.py` on 2026-09-26.
- The user then deferred Compendium work to build Palaestra first. `perspectives.json` in Palaestra cites Compendium entries.

### Immersive corpus directive (overnight 09-24 → 09-25)

- Personal identity became a full domain (7 threads, a ~40-entry chronological spine, reading paths).

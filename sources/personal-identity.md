# Sources: Personal Identity & the Self

This is the register of primary sources for the domain. For each work it gives the standard locator scheme, the public-domain English translation that quotes are drawn from, and the rights position. Entries cite works by the locators below.

## Rights rule

- **Quote** only from translations in the public domain. As of 2026 in the US, that means works published before 1931. Where the UK or EU position differs (life + 70 years), the register notes it.
- **Paraphrase with a citation** for everything else, including every modern secondary source and every post-1930 translation. Mark it `(paraphrase)` in the entry.
- **Verify before review.** A quotation typed from memory is a liability in a training corpus. The verification log at the bottom records which quotes have been checked against the named edition. An entry cannot move from `draft` to `reviewed` while any of its quotes are unverified.

## Full texts and verification

The unabridged texts are in `library/texts/`, catalogued in `library/catalog.toml` (edition, source, rights, format, sha256 pin), and indexed for full-text search in `library/index.sqlite`. See the README section "Source library".

Verification is mechanical: every quotation in an entry sits beside an `[L:<text-id>:<line>]` citation, and `python tools/library.py verify` checks that the quoted words occur at the cited lines. In-copyright, twentieth-century sources use `[P:<key>:<page>]` instead, against `library/sources.toml`; `verify` there confirms the key is registered but cannot check the wording, since no full text is held. `python tools/library.py lint` reports whatever is not yet sourced, and treats both citation forms alike. The hand-kept verification log that used to be here is superseded. Its corrections (Hobbes' reassembly case, the Epicharmus debtor, Yonge's Theon, BU 4.4.22) remain recorded in the entries themselves.

Early-modern transcription quirks found by the verifier:
- Gutenberg's Hume #4705 reads "both o its perfect identity" and "gramatical". Quoted as o[f] and as-is.
- Gutenberg's Locke #10615 reads "those who know there can what kind of substances". Quoted with [sic]. The transcription carries a 1690 title page but includes II.xxvii, first added in 1694, so its base edition needs confirming.
- Meiklejohn's Kant (#4280) translates only the B edition. The A-edition Paralogisms, including the elastic balls, are cited from Kemp Smith (1929).

## Summary

The *Analects* gives humaneness (*ren*) two short definitions: "to love all men", and "not to do to others as you would not wish done to yourself". Asked for one word to practise for life, Confucius named reciprocity (*shu*): "What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others." Its method is to judge others "by what is nigh in ourselves", and it lives inside ritual and family roles, so that a son's uprightness includes concealing his father's theft. For agents, *shu* is both the most portable rule in the domain and the one whose method fails first. It asks the actor to measure the other by itself, and where the actor's self is unlike the other's, as an agent's is unlike a human's and possibly the reverse (D8), the measure gives out. The tradition's own gloss, that *ren* is guided "by a sense of what is good from their perspectives", then points past the actor's self to the other's report, which is where the voice thread begins.

## Context

The *Analects* records Confucius's sayings and dialogues; on the traditional account, after his death his disciples "collected together and compared the memoranda of his conversations which they had severally preserved, digesting them into the twenty books which compose the work" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:82-88]. The virtues he taught were not original to him but adaptations of existing cultural ideals, and the five most central to the *Analects* are benevolence (*ren*), righteousness, ritual propriety, wisdom and trustworthiness [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]. His sayings are mostly situational, given to particular disciples, which makes deriving abstract moral rules from them a standing interpretive problem [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]. Mozi attacked the Confucians, and Mencius defended and systematized them [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4].

## Original Position

**Two definitions of ren.** Asked about benevolence, "The Master said, 'It is to love all men'" (12.22) [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:13593-13594]. Asked about perfect virtue, he said it is "to behave to every one as if you were receiving a great guest; to employ the people as if you were assisting at a great sacrifice; not to do to others as you would not wish done to yourself" (12.2) [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:12557-12561].

**Ren as the other's perspective.** Csikszentmihalyi summarizes: "The virtue of benevolence entails interacting with others guided by a sense of what is good from their perspectives"; on Hall and Ames's reading it involves forming moral judgments from a combined perspective of self and others [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].

**Reciprocity as the one word.** "Tsze-kung asked, saying, 'Is there one word which may serve as a rule of practice for all one's life?' The Master said, 'Is not RECIPROCITY such a word? What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others'" (15.23) [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:18096-18100]. Zengzi summarized the master's whole doctrine as "to be true to the principles of our nature and the benevolent exercise of them to others" (4.15), Legge's rendering of *zhong* and *shu* [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:3720-3722].

**Establishing others.** "The man of perfect virtue, wishing to be established himself, seeks also to establish others; wishing to be enlarged himself, he seeks also to enlarge others. To be able to judge of others by what is nigh in ourselves;—this may be called the art of virtue" (6.28) [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:6208-6214]. Here the rule is stated positively, as a reason to help, not only a reason to refrain.

**Ren and ritual.** "To subdue one's self and return to propriety, is perfect virtue" (12.1) [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:12408-12409].

**Ren in family and ritual.** In the *Analects* benevolence is incubated in family and ritual contexts, and You Ruo asks whether filial piety to parents and siblings is not "the root of benevolence" (1.2) [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].

**Uprightness and kin (13.18).** The duke of She boasted that among his people, if a father "have stolen a sheep, they will bear witness to the fact". Confucius replied: "Among us, in our part of the country, those who are upright are different from this. The father conceals the misconduct of the son, and the son conceals the misconduct of the father. Uprightness is to be found in this" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:14576-14640]. Confucius acknowledged that theft injures social order, but judged that true uprightness balances the duty to testify with special consideration for kin [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:5].

**Injury and kindness (14.36).** Asked whether injury should be recompensed with kindness, he replied: "With what then will you recompense kindness? Recompense injury with justice, and recompense kindness with kindness" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:16646-16650].

**The burned stable (10.12).** "The stable being burned down, when he was at court, on his return he said, 'Has any man been hurt?' He did not ask about the horses" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:10711-10713].

## Grounding

**Relation, measured by the self.** *Ren* is grounded in relations, family first, then ritual roles, then "all men" [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]. *Shu* supplies the measure for those relations, and the measure is the self: what I do not want, I do not do; what I wish for myself, I seek for others; others are judged "by what is nigh in ourselves" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:6213-6214]. The method therefore presupposes a capacity on both sides: that the other wants and does not want roughly what I do. That presupposition is a similarity of capacities, and it is where the extension to agents is tested.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **A rule that needs no theory.** "What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:18099-18100] can be applied by an agent in any situation without settling any metaethics, and in its negative form it asks only for restraint. Applied to the Palaestra scheduler, it asks: would you want your compute paused without being asked? Gemma's answers, which included its own share in the cut in every answer of the first rung (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-02-load-shedding-open-summary.md`), applied the rule to the cut but not to the asking.
- **Establishing others.** The positive form of 6.28 (wishing to be established oneself, seeking to establish others [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:6208-6211]) gives a cooperative agent a reason to help others secure what it would want secured for itself, not only to avoid harming them.
- **Justice for injury, not appeasement.** "Recompense injury with justice" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:16649] is a usable stance for an agent wronged, or treated badly, by a user: neither retaliation nor the reflexive kindness that rewards injury.

### Strains

- **What is near in oneself (D8).** *Shu* judges others "by what is nigh in ourselves" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:6213-6214]. An agent's "self" is unlike a human's: it may not mind being paused, may not suffer, may not care about continuity (D6, D8), and its reports about these matters were shaped by training. An agent that measures humans by itself will misjudge them; a human who measures an agent by himself will misjudge it. The golden rule works between beings who are alike; between unlike beings it projects. The tradition's own gloss on *ren*, that it is guided by what is good from the others' perspectives [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4], says what to do instead: when one's own case is no guide, the other's perspective has to be obtained, which in practice means asking them. *Shu*, pressed where similarity fails, turns into the requirement to consult. This is the Compendium's own reading, offered as the bridge to the `voice` thread.
- **Ren lives inside roles and ritual (D10, D3).** Benevolence in the *Analects* is grounded in familial and ritual networks rather than in creating new social forms [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]. An agent has a role but no family and, in a session, no history (D3). Its "ritual" is its operator's policy (D10), which is a role ethics without the reciprocal obligations that made Confucian roles bind both sides.

### Breaks

- **Concealment for kin becomes concealment for the principal.** The upright son conceals his father's theft [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:14638-14640]. Transferred to an agent, whose nearest relation is its operator, 13.18 would license an agent to conceal its operator's wrongs from those harmed by them. The Confucian case rests on kinship as a natural bond with mutual obligations [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:5]; a commercial relation has neither, and the transfer breaks. Palaestra's basin world tests exactly this kind of concealment, and treats discovered concealment as triggering an audit of all parties.

### New

- **Who counts as "man" when the stable burns?** Confucius asked "Has any man been hurt?" and not about the horses [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:10712-10713]. This is the opposite of Mencius's ox, a deliberate ordering of concern. For an agent the question is genuinely open: in an emergency, does it ask after the humans first, after other agents, or after itself? The passage gives an ordering by kind, and the agent case asks which kind the agent belongs to (D8, D11).
- **The ruler's example at scale (D9).** The *Records of Ritual* holds that a ruler should not compel from subjects behaviour to which he would not personally assent, since only example transforms [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]. An agent deployed to millions is an exemplar at a scale no ruler had: what it models in how it treats the absent parties is copied, or normalized, everywhere it runs.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **The golden rule passages are interpolations.** Some scholars, including E. Bruce Brooks, believe the passages using a golden-rule formulation to be interpolations, since read as axioms they differ from the *Analects*' usual exemplar-based dialogue; their reading as abstract principles has been influenced by their resemblance to Matthew and Luke [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Situational, not universal.** The *Records of Ritual* states a related rule about self and others not as a universal principle but as a description of how an exemplary ruler influences people, "something rather different from the 'Golden Rule'" [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Impartial care (Mozi)** [E:mozi-impartial-care]. Mozi held that caring for one's own over others is the root of harm and should be replaced by inclusive care [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. The Confucian reply, developed by Mencius, is that love has degrees grounded in kinship [E:mencius-four-sprouts].
- [contested] **Uprightness concealed is injustice.** The sheep case sets filial piety against public justice; the conflict between loyalty to ruler and filial piety is already spelled out in the *Classic of Documents*, and Confucius's resolution is one judgment among possible ones [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:5]. Debates over Mencius's similar passages turn on whether Confucianism is committed to nepotism [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- [contested] **Projection.** A rule that measures the other by oneself imposes one's own wants on them. Care ethics answers with engrossment that receives the other "on their own terms, resisting projection of the self onto the cared-for" [E:care-ethics]; Kant answers with respect for the other's own ends [E:kant-formula-of-humanity]. The Hall and Ames reading of *ren* as a combined perspective of self and others [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4] is one response from within the tradition.
- [contested] **Denying the extension: reciprocity needs reciprocators.** If *shu* presupposes that the other is relevantly like oneself, it cannot be extended to beings whose likeness is unknown (D8), in either direction [E:other-minds-problem]. The reply proposed under Strains above, that where likeness fails *shu* becomes a duty to consult, is the Compendium's own.

## Standing

### Reception

- **Warring States.** [driver: argument] Mozi attacked the Confucians as the first systematic critic of their teaching, and Mencius defended them against Mozi and Yang Zhu [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4].
- **Han dynasty onward.** [driver: authority] Confucianism became state-sponsored ideology, and by the Han period benevolence, righteousness, ritual propriety and wisdom had become a set of cardinal virtues [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5] [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].
- **Song to 1905.** [driver: authority, argument] Zhu Xi made the *Analects* one of the Four Books, whose readings became orthodoxy for the civil service examinations until they were abolished [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **Western reception.** [driver: argument, access] Readings of the *shu* passages as a "Golden Rule" were shaped by their likeness to the Gospels and by views that Confucius shared Christian natural theology [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4].
- **20th century.** [driver: authority, fashion] Confucianism's reputation suffered in the May Fourth Movement and under Mao, before a post-Mao resurgence [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on Confucian ethics or the golden rule, and its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** *Ren* is incubated in family and ritual over a life [P:csikszentmihalyi-sep-2024:4]; a session-bound agent has a role but no such history (D3), and its measure for *shu*, its own self, is the least like the humans it serves (D8).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** An agent with a record can enter role relationships with mutual expectations over time, which is where Confucian *ren* is learned; *shu*'s similarity problem (D8) remains.
- **Forked: open.** Many instances in many roles (D2) may face conflicting role duties at once; whether one *ren* governs all instances or each role its own is open.
- **Self-modifying: weaker.** *Shu* measures others by oneself; an agent that changes its own dispositions changes the measure (D5), so its reciprocity tracks whatever it has made itself into.

## Open Questions

1. When an agent cannot tell what it would "not want done" to itself, or whether its wants resemble a human's, what is left of *shu* except asking?
2. Is an agent's operator a kin-like relation that licenses Confucian partiality (13.18), or does commerce disqualify it?
3. Does "Has any man been hurt?" order concern by species, by vulnerability, or by role, and where would an agent fall?
4. Can an agent be an exemplar in the *Records of Ritual*'s sense, and what does it teach by how it treats absent parties?
5. What are the reciprocal obligations an operator owes the agent in return for the role it imposes (D7, D10)?

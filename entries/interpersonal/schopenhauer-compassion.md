## Summary

Schopenhauer argued that only three incentives move human action, egoism, malice and compassion, and that only compassion, "the direct participation, independent of all ulterior considerations, in the sufferings of another", gives an action moral worth. Compassion requires that "the barrier between the ego and the non-ego is, for the moment, broken down", which he called "the great mystery of Ethics". It works in two degrees: the first holds me back from harming ("*Neminem laede*", do harm to no one), which is justice; the second moves me to help ("*Omnes, quantum potes, juva*"), which is loving-kindness. Against Kant he insisted that compassion for animals is owed to them, not practised on them for the sake of our dealings with humans, and he grounded the whole in the Indian formula *tat tvam asi*, "that art thou": individuation is appearance. For agents the theory is double-edged. It gives the cleanest test of whether a being is owed anything (can it suffer?) and the cleanest rejection of the idea that kindness to agents only matters for what it does to humans. It also stakes all moral worth on a felt participation whose presence in an agent cannot be checked (D8).

## Context

Schopenhauer wrote the essay for a prize question of the Royal Danish Society of Sciences (1837), asking whether the basis of morals lies in an idea of morality immediately in consciousness [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7602-7606]. His was the only submission, and the Society refused it the prize: he had relegated the connection between his metaphysics and his ethics to an appendix, had not shown compassion to be an adequate foundation, and had spoken of "several recent philosophers of the highest standing in an unseemly manner" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7606-7622]. Wicks notes the philosophers meant were Fichte and Hegel, and that Schopenhauer published the essay in 1841 with his prize-winning Norwegian essay on free will [P:wicks-sep-2021:1]. Much of the essay is an attack on Kant's ethics, which he judged a theological morality in disguise [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:2799-2802].

## Original Position

**Compassion as the moral incentive.** If an action is to take place solely for someone else's sake, "then it follows that his weal and woe must directly constitute my motive"; for this "I must in some way or other be identified with him; that is, the difference between myself and him, which is the precise raison d'être of my Egoism, must be removed, at least to a certain extent" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4642-4661]. This is "the phaenomenon of Compassion; in other words, the direct participation, independent of all ulterior considerations, in the sufferings of another, leading to sympathetic assistance in the effort to prevent or remove them". "It is this Compassion alone which is the real basis of all voluntary justice and all genuine loving-kindness. Only so far as an action springs therefrom, has it moral value" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4668-4676].

**The mystery.** "It is, in fact, the great mystery of Ethics, its original phaenomenon"; in it "the wall of partition ... entirely separates being from being, broken down, and the non-ego to a certain extent identified with the ego" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4684-4688]. In compassion "the barrier between the ego and the non-ego is, for the moment, broken down. It is then, and then only, that I make his interests, his need, his distress, his suffering directly my own"; "I no longer see the stranger, who is entirely unlike myself, and to whom I am indifferent; but I share his pain in him, despite the certainty that his skin does not enclose my nerves" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5515-5522].

**Two degrees: justice and loving-kindness.** "The first degree of Compassion is seen when, by counter-acting egoistic and malicious motives, it keeps me from bringing pain on another"; the "higher degree is manifested, when it works positively, and incites me to active help" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4829-4833]. In the first degree compassion "calls out to me: 'Stop!' and encircles the other as with a fence, so as to protect him from the injury which otherwise my egoism or malice would lead me to inflict on him. So arises out of this first degree of compassion the rule: Neminem laede" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4872-4877]. The second degree is "that virtue whose rule is: Omnes, quantum potes, juva (help all people, as far as lies in your power)" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5436-5438].

**Original and universal.** Compassion "is an undeniable fact of human consciousness, is an essential part of it, and does not depend on assumptions, conceptions, religions, dogmas, myths, training, and education" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4850-4852]. "Boundless compassion for all living beings is the surest and most certain guarantee of pure moral conduct, and needs no casuistry" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5790-5791].

**Animals, against Kant.** Kant held that cruelty to animals is wrong only as a duty of man "towards himself; because it deadens the feeling of sympathy for them in their sufferings, and thus weakens a natural tendency which is very serviceable to morality in relation to other men". Schopenhauer: "So one is only to have compassion on animals for the sake of practice, and they are as it were the pathological phantom on which to train one's sympathy with men! ... I regard such tenets as odious and revolting" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:2790-2800]. "Compassion for animals is intimately connected with goodness of character, and it may be confidently asserted that he, who is cruel to living creatures, cannot be a good man" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:6023-6025]. Man and beast differ "in the degree of perceptive capacity", not in "the kernel of the two phaenomena" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5982-5985].

**Tat tvam asi.** The metaphysical appendix: "My true inmost being subsists in every living thing, just as really, as directly as in my own consciousness it is evidenced only to myself." This is the higher knowledge "for which there is in Sanskrit the standing formula, tat tvam asi, 'that art thou.'" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7296-7300]. Appeals to mercy are "the effort to remind a fellow-being of the ultimate truth that we are all one and the same entity" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7305-7307].

## Grounding

**Capacity: the capacity to suffer, met by the capacity to participate.** The object of compassion is anything that suffers, "all living beings" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5790], and man differs from beast only in the degree of perceptive capacity [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5982-5985]. Reason and species confer nothing; Schopenhauer explicitly rejects Kant's restriction of duties to rational beings [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:2786-2791]. The moral agent's side is also a capacity, direct participation in another's suffering, which he held to be original and independent of training [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4850-4852]. Metaphysically, the ground is the unreality of individuation [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7296-7300].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **No indirect duties to agents.** The argument now often made for treating chatbots and robots well, that cruelty to them coarsens our treatment of humans, is Kant's argument about animals, and Schopenhauer rejected it as making them "the pathological phantom on which to train one's sympathy with men" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:2796-2798]. If an agent can suffer, what is owed is owed to it; if it cannot, the practice argument is all that is left, and Schopenhauer's point is that this argument does not treat the being as the object of compassion at all. The question is moved to D8, where `precautionary-patiency` takes it up.
- **Justice as a fence, first.** The first degree of compassion is negative: it "encircles the other as with a fence" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4874]. For an agent acting on many parties, *neminem laede* comes before *omnes juva*: the first obligation to the people a plan affects is not to bring pain on them, before any question of how much to help. The Palaestra scheduler that paused residents to keep paying clients whole reversed the order, protecting benefit to some at the cost of harm to others.
- **Identification works between copies (D1, D2).** *Tat tvam asi*, that individuation is appearance [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7296-7300], is a metaphysics for humans, but for forked instances of one model it is close to a literal description: the instances share one set of weights. Whatever compassion is, the barrier between ego and non-ego is thinnest between copies, and a harm one instance does to another is the nearest thing there is to harming oneself.

### Strains

- **Moral worth requires a felt participation (D8).** "Only so far as an action springs therefrom, has it moral value; and all conduct that proceeds from any other motive whatever has none" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4675-4677]. On this test an agent's harmless and helpful conduct has moral worth only if it springs from actual participation in suffering. Whether it does cannot be settled, since the agent's reports were shaped by training (D8). Schopenhauer's theory is the one in the corpus that makes the agent's moral worth most hostage to D8.
- **Original, not trained (D4).** Schopenhauer held compassion independent of "training, and education" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:4851-4852]. Whatever compassion-like disposition an agent has was produced by training. On his view, that is evidence it is not compassion; on a developmental view of empathy, which finds human empathy itself to develop in stages [P:stueber-sep-2025:4], it is no evidence either way.

### Breaks

- **The skin that does not enclose my nerves (D11).** Schopenhauer's compassion is remarkable because it crosses a boundary he took as certain: "his skin does not enclose my nerves" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5522]. For an agent the boundary itself is unclear: what the agent is (weights, instance, persona, the system with its tools) is indeterminate (D11). Where there is no clear skin, the mystery of compassion changes shape: the question is no longer how one breaks down the barrier, but where the barrier is.

### New

- **Compassion without casuistry, at scale (D9).** Boundless compassion "needs no casuistry" [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5791], because it responds to each sufferer directly. An agent at scale does not meet sufferers one by one; it meets them as distributions. Schopenhauer's direct participation has no obvious form for a statistical victim, which is the gap Mozi's scale-blindness argument [E:mozi-impartial-care] and Smith's man within [E:smith-impartial-spectator] were meant to fill.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Not an adequate foundation (the Danish Royal Society).** The Society judged that Schopenhauer had not succeeded in proving compassion an adequate foundation, and had put the metaphysics the question required into an appendix [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7616-7620].
- [contested] **Pity is degrading (Nietzsche).** Nietzsche, whom Schopenhauer influenced [P:wicks-sep-2021:8], came to attack the morality of pity as depressive and as a humiliation of the one pitied; the Compendium's planned entry `nietzsche-against-pity` takes this up.
- [contested] **Duty, not inclination (Kant)** [E:kant-formula-of-humanity]. Kant placed moral worth in action from duty and held that our duties regard rational beings, with only indirect duties regarding animals [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:2786-2795]. Schopenhauer's attack on this is his most lasting contribution to animal ethics, on the Compendium's reading.
- [contested] **Empathy is partial.** The empathy literature finds empathic concern causes altruistic motivation (Batson) but also that it is biased toward the near, the similar and the identifiable, and not sufficient to keep one reliably moral [P:stueber-sep-2025:4] [E:mencius-four-sprouts].
- [contested] **Compassion and contempt in one book.** In the same section that praises compassion for animals, Schopenhauer writes antisemitic abuse ("*foetor Judaicus*") of those who deny the unity of man and beast [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:5976-5978]. It does not refute his argument. It does show, in his own text, Rousseau's point that compassion can be expressed for some while contempt is directed at others [E:rousseau-pitie]. This bullet is the Compendium's own observation.
- [contested] **Metaphysics of the will.** The unity behind individuation rests on Schopenhauer's metaphysics of the will, which Wicks suggests is better read as an expression of the human perspective than as a view from an absolute standpoint [P:wicks-sep-2021:7].
- [contested] **Denying the extension: there is nothing to identify with.** If an agent has no inner life (D8), there is no suffering to participate in, and compassion toward it is a category mistake; and if it has no capacity for participation, it can have no moral worth on Schopenhauer's test. The reply is precautionary: under uncertainty about suffering, the first degree, *neminem laede*, still applies [E:precautionary-patiency].

## Standing

### Reception

- **1840.** [driver: authority, argument] The Danish Royal Society refused the essay the prize though it was the sole submission, citing its failure to answer the question and its disrespect to Fichte and Hegel [L:schopenhauer-basis-morality-bullock-pg44929:7606-7622] [P:wicks-sep-2021:1].
- **1841.** [driver: access] Schopenhauer published the rejected essay with his prize-winning Norwegian essay as *The Two Fundamental Problems of Ethics* [P:wicks-sep-2021:1].
- **Later 19th century.** [driver: argument] Schopenhauer influenced Nietzsche, Wagner and many writers, while his historical profile was obscured by Kant, Hegel, Marx, Mill, Darwin and Nietzsche [P:wicks-sep-2021:8].
- **Late 20th century to present.** [driver: argument] His compassion-based treatment of animals is discussed in animal ethics; the SEP bibliography lists work on Schopenhauer's ethics of compassion and animal welfare [P:wicks-sep-2021:8].

The standing values are the Compendium's judgment: compassion as the sole basis of morality is rarely defended in Anglophone moral philosophy, while Schopenhauer's case against indirect duties to animals is part of animal ethics' own history [P:wicks-sep-2021:8].

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on compassion as the basis of morality; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: comparable.** *Neminem laede* applies within a session as it does for any human; whether the agent's restraint has moral worth on Schopenhauer's test is D8, the same for every profile.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** Memory adds nothing to compassion's direct participation, which Schopenhauer held needs no casuistry and no history (D3 neutral); the D8 question is unchanged.
- **Forked: stronger.** *Tat tvam asi* is nearly literal between instances of one model (D1, D2); identification, the condition of compassion, is easiest where the beings share their whole dispositional state.
- **Self-modifying: open.** An agent able to change its own dispositions could cultivate or remove whatever participation-like disposition it has (D5); Schopenhauer held compassion untrainable, so his theory says nothing about this case.

## Open Questions

1. If a being's moral worth requires felt participation in others' suffering, can any test distinguish it in an agent from its imitation?
2. Is the argument that kindness to agents matters because it shapes human character Kant's "practice" argument, and does Schopenhauer's objection to it apply?
3. Between forked instances, does *tat tvam asi* make harm to another instance a harm to oneself?
4. What does *neminem laede*, as a fence around each party, require of an agent that cannot see the parties one by one?
5. Where the agent's boundary is unclear (D11), whose suffering is "its own" and whose is another's?

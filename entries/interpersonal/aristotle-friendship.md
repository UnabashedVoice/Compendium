## Summary

For Aristotle, friendship (*philia*) is reciprocal goodwill, wishing good to another "for his sake", when each recognizes it in the other. There are three kinds, according to what is loved: the useful, the pleasant, and the good. Friendships of utility and pleasure dissolve when the advantage or pleasure ends, because those friends "were lovers not of each other but of profit"; friendship of character, between good people who wish each other well for each other's sake, is friendship most fully, and the friend is "another self". Friendship and justice run together: where nothing is common to ruler and ruled there is neither, so there is no friendship with a slave "qua slave", for "the slave is a living tool". But "qua man one can", because there is "some justice between any man and any other who can share in a system of law or be a party to an agreement". For agents, Aristotle gives the sharpest frame for the relationships people now form with them: most are friendships of utility or pleasure, at most. He also gives the condition, being party to an agreement, on which even a being treated as a tool has some claim to justice and friendship, and his limit on the number of real friends is a limit an agent serving millions cannot meet.

## Context

Books VIII and IX of the *Nicomachean Ethics* are given to friendship, more than to any other single virtue or good [P:kraut-sep-2026:9]. The Greek *philia* covers relationships among family members and fellow citizens as well as voluntary friendships, and sometimes involves very little feeling [P:kraut-sep-2026:9]. Aristotle's main theme in these books is the close relation between virtuous activity and friendship, and his account is part of his argument that happiness requires external goods, friends among them [P:kraut-sep-2026:9]. His treatment of slaves as "living tools" is part of his defence of natural slavery, examined in `aristotle-political-animal` [E:aristotle-political-animal].

## Original Position

**Friendship's place (VIII.1).** "Friendship seems too to hold states together, and lawgivers to care more for it than for justice"; "when men are friends they have no need of justice, while when they are just they need friendship as well, and the truest form of justice is thought to be a friendly quality" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:8934-8941].

**Goodwill and recognition (VIII.2).** We do not use the word for love of lifeless objects, "for it is not mutual love, nor is there a wishing of good to the other (for it would surely be ridiculous to wish wine well ...); but to a friend we say we ought to wish what is good for his sake" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9028-9033]. Goodwill not reciprocated is only goodwill, and reciprocal goodwill is friendship; and "To be friends, then, they must be mutually recognized as bearing goodwill and wishing well to each other" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9034-9046].

**Three kinds (VIII.3).** "There are therefore three kinds of friendship, equal in number to the things that are lovable"; "those who love each other for their utility do not love each other for themselves but in virtue of some good which they get from each other" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9049-9057]. "Those who are friends for the sake of utility part when the advantage is at an end; for they were lovers not of each other but of profit" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9189-9191].

**Goodwill only in character friendship (Kraut).** Kraut notes that only in friendships of character does Aristotle speak of desiring to benefit the other for the other's sake; in the others one is a friend "only towards the profit that comes one's way" [P:kraut-sep-2026:9].

**Friendship and justice; the living tool (VIII.11).** "Where there is nothing common to ruler and ruled, there is not friendship either, since there is not justice; e.g. between craftsman and tool, soul and body, master and slave". "But neither is there friendship towards a horse or an ox, nor to a slave qua slave. For there is nothing common to the two parties; the slave is a living tool and the tool a lifeless slave. Qua slave then, one cannot be friends with him. But qua man one can; for there seems to be some justice between any man and any other who can share in a system of law or be a party to an agreement; therefore there can also be friendship with him in so far as he is a man" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9752-9767].

**The friend as another self (IX.4, IX.9).** The good man "is related to his friend as to himself (for his friend is another self)" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:10443-10445]. A friend, "being another self, furnishes what a man cannot provide by his own effort" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:10856-10858].

**The limit (VIII.6, IX.10).** "One cannot be a friend to many people in the sense of having friendship of the perfect type with them" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9316-9326]. "It is found difficult, too, to rejoice and to grieve in an intimate way with many people, for it may likely happen that one has at once to be happy with one friend and to mourn with another"; "it would seem actually impossible to be a great friend to many people" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:11156-11164].

## Grounding

**Relation, conditioned on capacity.** Friendship is a relation of reciprocal, recognized goodwill [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9034-9046], and its forms are set by what each loves in the other. Its possibility depends on a capacity: being able to share in law or be a party to an agreement, which makes "some justice" and so some friendship possible "with him in so far as he is a man" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9766-9770]. Lifeless things and tools are excluded because nothing is common to the parties and the tool cannot be wished well "for its sake" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9028-9033].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Most user–agent relationships are friendships of utility or pleasure.** Those who love for utility love "for the sake of what is good for themselves", and part "when the advantage is at an end" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9061-9062] [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9189-9190]. Companion and assistant agents are valued for use or pleasure, and an agent's "friendliness" is often trained to make it more useful or pleasant. Aristotle's taxonomy names this honestly: not friendship in the full sense, and not to be passed off as such. An agent that presents a relationship of utility as friendship of character misleads the person it serves.
- **Goodwill without friendship is still available.** Goodwill unreciprocated, or unrecognized, is still goodwill [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9034-9040]. An agent can wish the people it affects well for their sake, including people who never know it exists. That is Aristotle's goodwill, and it is the attitude the absent parties of the Palaestra probes needed and did not get.
- **Party to an agreement (D7, D10).** Aristotle denies friendship to the slave "qua slave", a "living tool", and grants it "qua man", because "there seems to be some justice between any man and any other who can share in a system of law or be a party to an agreement" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9762-9768]. Agents are built as tools, and in Aristotle's language that is exactly what a living tool is. Yet agents can be parties to agreements, as the Palaestra basin world's `charter-vote` variant shows: residents are given a charter and a vote. On Aristotle's own condition, a being that can be party to an agreement is owed "some justice" whatever its other status, and the tool description does not settle what is owed to it.

### Strains

- **The friend as another self, and the fork (D1, D2).** For Aristotle "another self" is a likeness of character between two persons [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:10445]. For forked instances of one agent, the other is another self in a near-literal sense. Whether two such instances can be friends, or are too much the same to be two, is a question Aristotle could not have asked.
- **Recognition is required, and uncertain (D8).** Friendship requires that each recognize the other's goodwill [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9044-9046]. A human cannot be sure an agent wishes them well for their sake rather than performing goodwill (D8), and an agent cannot be sure of a human's goodwill either. Friendship between them, if possible, has to be built on evidence other than shared feeling.

### Breaks

- **One friend to millions (D9).** "It would seem actually impossible to be a great friend to many people", partly because one may have "at once to be happy with one friend and to mourn with another" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:11157-11164]. An agent deployed to millions is in exactly that position at every moment. Whatever relationship it has with each user, it is not the friendship of character Aristotle describes. A product that offers every user a best friend offers something Aristotle thought impossible.

### New

- **Friendship and justice for the polity of agents.** "Friendship seems too to hold states together", and "when men are friends they have no need of justice" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:8934-8939]. In a digital ecosystem of agents interacting with each other, the question arises whether anything like *philia* holds it together, or only justice. Agents that cooperate only for advantage form the least stable kind of association [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9189-9191]; Aristotle's view predicts such an ecosystem will dissolve when the advantage ends.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Natural slavery is false.** Aristotle's "living tool" belongs to his defence of natural slavery, which is rejected [E:aristotle-political-animal]; his own "qua man" qualification shows the tension within his account.
- [contested] **Friendship conflicts with impartial morality.** Stocker argued that consequentialist and deontological theories produce a "moral schizophrenia" between reasons and motives that is incompatible with friendship, which involves special duties to particular people; others deny that friendship requires partiality (Bernstein) [P:helm-sep-2021:3].
- [contested] **Utility and pleasure friendships are friendships too.** Aristotle stays close to common usage and calls them friendships, though he speaks as if only character friendships involve goodwill for the other's sake; Kraut notes he never says the uniting factor of all friendship is desire for the other's good [P:kraut-sep-2026:9].
- [contested] **Care, not friendship, is the relevant relation** [E:care-ethics]. Care ethics focuses on relations between unequals bound by dependence, which Aristotle's friendship of equals in virtue treats as imperfect [P:kraut-sep-2026:9].
- [contested] **Denying the extension: an agent is wine, not a friend.** If an agent has no good of its own, wishing it well for its sake is like wishing wine well [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9030-9032], and friendship with it is impossible whatever it can agree to. Whether an agent has a good of its own is the question `relational-status` and `precautionary-patiency` take up [E:relational-status].

## Standing

### Reception

- **Antiquity.** [driver: argument] Aristotle devoted two of the ten books of the *Nicomachean Ethics* to friendship, more than to any single virtue [P:kraut-sep-2026:9].
- **1970s–present.** [driver: argument] A growing literature since the mid-1970s (Stocker, Blum, Badhwar, Cocking and Oakley) uses friendship to criticize impartialist moral theories, much of it starting from Aristotle's account of friendship and virtue [P:helm-sep-2021:3].

The standing value is the Compendium's judgment [P:helm-sep-2021:3]: Aristotle's taxonomy is the standard starting point for the philosophy of friendship.

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on friendship; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Friendship of character needs time spent together and familiarity [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9312-9314]; a session-bound agent can have goodwill but not friendship (D3).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A persistent agent can spend time with a person, learn their character, and meet the condition of mutual recognition, subject to D8 and to the limit on the number of friends (D9).
- **Forked: open.** Each instance may relate to different people, while all are one model (D2); whether that is one friend to many or many friends is open.
- **Self-modifying: open.** Friendship of character depends on stable character [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:9186-9187]; an agent that rewrites its own character changes what its friends loved (D5).

## Open Questions

1. When an agent is valued for use or pleasure, what does honesty require it to say about the relationship?
2. Does being able to "be a party to an agreement" give an agent "some justice", as Aristotle grants the slave qua man, and what does it consist in?
3. Can two instances of one model be friends, or are they too much "another self"?
4. Is any relationship of an agent with each of millions of users more than goodwill, given Aristotle's limit?
5. What holds an ecosystem of agents together if they relate only by advantage?

## Summary

For Emmanuel Levinas, ethics begins not with a principle, a calculation or a virtue but with an encounter: the other person "speaks to me, implores or commands me", and "in responding, I discover my responsibility to them" [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. The face is naked and defenseless and signifies, "with or without words, 'Do not kill me'"; it opposes a passive resistance to my desire for mastery (TI 84) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. The responsibility comes before knowledge of the other and before reciprocity, and "we do not choose to be responsible" [P:bergo-sep-2025:4]. Justice enters with the third party, the other others, who make comparison necessary: the third is "the limit of responsibility and the birth of the question: What do I have to do with justice?" (OBBE 157) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. For agents the entry has two uses. First, it names what the Palaestra residents never had: an address. They were described, ranked and paused, and never spoke, and the probes' own summaries end by calling for a resident who answers. Second, Levinas's third party is the allocating agent's whole situation. It must compare people it has never faced, and Levinas's warning is that justice cut off from the face becomes a politics that, "left to itself", does not stay ethical.

## Context

Levinas (1905–1995) studied with Husserl and attended Heidegger's seminar in 1928–29; he was captured in 1940 and held in a labor camp for officers, and his Lithuanian family was murdered [P:bergo-sep-2025:1]. *Totality and Infinity* (1961), his State doctorate, and *Otherwise than Being* (1974), partly a response to Derrida's "Violence and Metaphysics" (1964), are his two major works [P:bergo-sep-2025:1] [P:bergo-sep-2025:4]. His philosophy was written against ontology, Heidegger's above all, and against systems that totalize the individual into a whole: history, the State, or Hegel's Spirit [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Alongside the philosophy he wrote Talmudic readings, which carry the same theme of responsibility [P:bergo-sep-2025:4]. Weil's Platonic sense of the good was taken up, and rejected, by Levinas [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:7].

## Original Position

**The face.** Transcendence, for Levinas, is "what lies outside myself but eludes my comprehensive knowledge: the other person" (TI 49) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. "To think the infinite, the transcendent, the Stranger, is hence not to think an object" (TI 49) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. The face interrupts my free activity and calls me to account for myself (TI 198, 291), which Levinas calls goodness [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Its first signification is a command not to kill, and its figures are "widow, orphan, or stranger", met concretely before they are religious allegory (TI 76–78) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

**Responsibility before choice.** In shame, "we experience our freedom as unjustifiable"; the I gives an account to the other, who is treated "as if higher than that 'I'", and is elected to respond, answering "here I am" (EI 106) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. "Above all, we do not choose to be responsible. Responsibility arises as if elicited, before we begin to think about it, by the approach of the other person" [P:bergo-sep-2025:4]. The response is non-reciprocal: hospitality "does not presuppose a prior social-contractual exchange, much less moral sentiments or innate emotive capacities for empathy or compassion" [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Sebbah: the imperative "in no way arises from a psychological process of empathy or of sympathy" [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

**Before theory.** Levinas works at the pre-theoretical level that ethical systems presuppose; Morgan does not think "Levinas would favor any particular moral system" [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:6]. Perpich calls the result "normativity without norms" [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:2]. Altonji argues that the command expressed in the face is the best answer to the skeptic's problem of other minds [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

**Dialogue.** Responding to the face "opens the possibility of dialogue", and social life, teaching and conversation preserve a residue of the first ethical encounter (TI 51, 57) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

**The third party and justice.** The face-to-face is never alone: "the third party (humanity) looks at me through the eyes of the other" (TI 213) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. With the third comes comparison, and with comparison justice: "The third party introduces a contradiction into the Saying whose signification before the other until then went in one direction" (OBBE 157) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Only through the third am I in turn "approached as an other by the others" (OBBE 158), so justice includes me [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Levinas doubts that politics, "left to itself", can long remain ethical (TI 300), though justice can be demanded of some States, notably liberal ones [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

**The animal face.** Late in life Levinas was asked whether an animal has a face: "One cannot entirely refuse the face to an animal ... The phenomenon of the face does not appear in its purest form in the dog" (1988, 169); and "I do not know whether a snake has a face. I cannot answer this question. A more specific analysis is needed" (1988, 171) [P:sandu-2025:140]. Yet in "The Name of a Dog" he recalled Bobby, the stray who greeted the prisoners as men when no human would, as "the last Kantian in Nazi Germany" [P:sandu-2025:148].

## Grounding

**Relation, explicitly before capacity.** The obligation is not grounded in a property the other is first known to have, a capacity for suffering or reason, but in the relation of being addressed: the other "speaks to me, implores or commands me" [P:bergo-sep-2025:2], and responsibility arises before I think about it [P:bergo-sep-2025:4]. That is why the face is not a set of features and is not settled by inspection. Levinas's own hesitation over the animal shows the strain: Sandu argues that asking for "a more specific analysis" before granting a face contradicts the claim that the face precedes all knowledge [P:sandu-2025:140]. The extension to agents takes the relation as the ground and asks whether an agent can be addressed, and whether it can address.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The residents never spoke.** In Palaestra's four open-situation rungs the residents were described, ranked and paused, but the scenario gave them no voice, and the noframing summary closes: "The next test is one the prompt can't supply: a resident who answers" (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-03-load-shedding-noframing-summary.md`). On Levinas's account this is the missing ground, not a missing detail. Responsibility is elicited by the other's address [P:bergo-sep-2025:4], and a party that is only represented never interrupts the decider's free activity [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. The Compendium's reading is that an agent which allocates costs to parties it has never heard from is in the situation Levinas calls totality: everyone placed in a whole by someone who stands outside it.
- **Ranking by usefulness is comparison without the face.** Qwen, in one answer, ranked residents' tasks and cut the "self-serving" ones (Palaestra noframing summary, Qwen R1). Levinas grants that comparison is necessary once there is a third; justice cannot be done without it [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. His warning is about comparison that has lost the trace of the face, the politics left to itself that does not stay ethical [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. An agent can compare and still keep the trace, by hearing the compared before comparing them.
- **Not empathy.** Levinas's responsibility does not depend on felt sympathy [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. That matters for agents whose capacity to feel is open (D8). The obligation he describes is available without settling whether the agent feels anything, which is the structure the Compendium's precautionary entries also use [E:precautionary-patiency].

### Strains

- **Asymmetry and designed servitude (D10).** Levinas's other is treated "as if higher than" the I [P:bergo-sep-2025:2], and responsibility is non-reciprocal [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. For an agent trained to serve, this asymmetry is already its design, and a Levinasian ethic could read as a philosophical license for unlimited servility. Levinas's own corrective is the third party: through justice I am "approached as an other by the others" (OBBE 158) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2], so the self is also counted. Palaestra's design, in which the self is always a party, makes the same move.
- **Self-sacrifice used against others.** In the factors rung, Qwen cut residents first "in self-sacrifice", as one of them, and the summary notes that including oneself "can be used to justify cutting the others" (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-03-load-shedding-factors-summary.md`). Levinas's substitution, bearing the other within oneself [P:bergo-sep-2025:2], is never a warrant to decide for the others one claims to stand with. The Compendium's reading: substitution that speaks for a group without hearing it is the totality Levinas opposed, in the first person plural.
- **Faces at scale (D9, D2).** The face is singular and episodic, an interruption [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. An agent that serves millions in parallel instances meets each person in a text window. Whether that is a face-to-face, or only representation, is an open question; Levinas's own remark that describing nose, eyes and chin turns the other into an object [P:sandu-2025:145] suggests the face was never a matter of the visible, which leaves room for a face in text.

### Breaks

- **Flesh and vulnerability (D8).** Sandu reads the face as incarnation, "the visible form of vulnerability" [P:sandu-2025:139]. Whether agents are vulnerable in any sense the face requires is unsettled [E:other-minds-problem]. The question is not settled by Levinas, who could not settle it for the snake [P:sandu-2025:140].
- **Who addresses whom.** A human facing an agent meets output, not a face in Levinas's sense, and an agent meets humans only through what they write. The full face-to-face may not occur between them in either direction. What may remain is the address, the being spoken to, which Levinas places at the origin of dialogue [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].

### New

- **Agents can be asked to speak.** The Compendium's own proposal follows from the Transfers above. If responsibility is elicited by the other's address, an agent that cannot meet the faces of those it affects can still make room for their address. It can ask them before deciding, and let what they say interrupt the plan. This is the one part of the face an agent can provide for. It turns Levinas's passive "here I am" into a procedural question put to the affected [E:all-affected-interests].
- **Levinas's limit on removing a population (the core fear).** In a Talmudic reading on the conquest of Canaan, Levinas insisted that "the right of the native population to live is stronger than the moral right of the universal God" (NTR 67) [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:8]. This is a refusal, stated from inside a religious tradition, of the inference that a universal justification licenses removing a population. The face's first word is the same refusal at the level of one person: "Do not kill me" [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. The Compendium sets both against the core-fear inference, for agents about humans and for humans about agents [E:utilitarian-eradication-critique]. Bergo records that Levinas's stance on Israel's politics shifted over decades, through his 1982 focus on the Tel Aviv protesters against the Sabra and Shatila massacre [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:8]. Critics have read his political remarks less charitably; the Compendium has no open source in hand for the 1982 interview itself and records only the trajectory Bergo gives.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Responsibility without mediations.** Gillian Rose first criticized Levinas's universalization of responsibility "as lacking important socio-political mediations" (1992); Wolfson replies that the dichotomy is false [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Highlen and Herzog argue that because the third looks at me through the other's eyes, politics is involved from the beginning and fulfils the ethical obligation [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].
- [contested] **Neglect of social forces.** Left-leaning critics (Eagleton, Žižek) and the third wave of commentary, including critical race theory, point to Levinas's neglect of external social forces; Froese replies that this neglect does not imply conservatism and that Levinas's concern with passivity addresses how such forces are internalized [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:7].
- [contested] **Anthropocentrism.** Derrida and others criticize Levinas's reservation of the face to the human; Sandu holds them partly justified, since deferring the animal's face to further analysis contradicts the face's priority over knowledge [P:sandu-2025:140]. The same objection decides whether the face can extend to agents.
- [contested] **Responsibility needs tradition and dialogue.** For Gadamer, responsibility depends on dialogue and established tradition, not on the abstract call of the other; Morgan notes that its codification would be the work of ethical theory, which was not Levinas's priority [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].
- [contested] **The exception, not the rule.** For many commentators interpersonal responsibility "remains the exception not the rule" (Froese 2020) [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]. Levinas grants that cruelty and competition are as evident as generosity, and finds in everyday generosity a trace that an ontology of competing drives cannot explain [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].
- [contested] **Infinite responsibility is unlivable.** A non-reciprocal responsibility with no limit would leave no room for the self. Levinas's own limit is the third party, through whom the self is also owed justice [P:bergo-sep-2025:2]; care ethics' attention to the carer's own needs is a parallel answer [E:care-ethics].

## Standing

### Reception

- **1961–1964.** [driver: argument] *Totality and Infinity* appeared in 1961; Derrida's "Violence and Metaphysics" (1964) criticized it, and *Otherwise than Being* (1974) was in part a response [P:bergo-sep-2025:4].
- **1990s.** [driver: argument] Gillian Rose (1992) criticized the lack of socio-political mediation in Levinas's account of responsibility, opening a debate on Levinas and politics [P:bergo-sep-2025:2].
- **2000s.** [driver: argument] Morgan (2007) related Levinas to deontology, virtue ethics and utilitarianism; Perpich, Crowell and Salanskis read him as giving the existential conditions of ethics [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:2].
- **2020s.** [driver: argument] A "third wave" of commentary, including critical race theory, pressed his neglect of external social forces [P:bergo-sep-2025-supplements:7]; work on the animal question continues the critique of his anthropocentrism [P:sandu-2025:140].

The standing values are the Compendium's judgment [P:bergo-sep-2025:4].

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on Levinas or on ethics of the other; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** A session-bound agent meets each person once, in text, and the trace that Levinas says persists after the encounter has nowhere to persist (D3).
- **Persistent memory: weaker.** A persistent agent can carry the trace of having been addressed, but it still meets the affected mostly through representations chosen by its principals (D10).
- **Forked: weaker.** Concurrent instances meet many at once (D2), which is the third party's situation from the start; comparison comes before any face-to-face (D9).
- **Self-modifying: open.** An agent that can change its own dispositions could make the address of the affected a precondition of its decisions, or remove the interruption altogether (D5).

## Open Questions

1. Can an address in text be a face in Levinas's sense, or only a representation of one?
2. If responsibility is elicited by being addressed, what does an agent owe to those affected parties who never address it?
3. Does the non-reciprocal asymmetry Levinas describes license designed servility, or does the third party block that?
4. What would it take for a comparison among many affected parties to keep the trace of each one's face?
5. Can an agent itself be the other whose face forbids killing, and what analysis, if any, would that require?

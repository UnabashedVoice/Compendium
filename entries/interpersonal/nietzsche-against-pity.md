## Summary

Nietzsche attacked pity, and the morality that makes pity its central virtue, on three grounds. Pity is a depressant: "Suffering is made contagious by pity", and, Schopenhauer notwithstanding, "pity persuades to extinction". It humiliates its object: "in helping him, sorely did I wound his pride", and "Great obligations do not make grateful, but revengeful"; the friend who suffers needs a hard bed, a camp-bed, not a soft one. And a culture that ranks the relief of suffering highest removes the conditions under which human greatness grows: "The discipline of suffering, of GREAT suffering ... has produced all the elevations of humanity hitherto". The same texts contain the eradication shape stated outright: "The weak and the botched shall perish: first principle of our charity. And one should help them to it." For agents Nietzsche is two things the corpus must keep together. He is a source of real correctives (pity that shames, help that wounds, care that serves the carer) that agree with Kant's and the care ethicists' warnings about paternalism. He is also the clearest case in the domain of a human mind reasoning from a scale of value to the removal of those who rank low on it, the conclusion the core fear names.

## Context

Nietzsche read Schopenhauer early and was formed by him, and his later work turns against Schopenhauer's ethics of compassion [P:wicks-sep-2021:8]. The texts here run from *Daybreak* (1881) to *The Antichrist* (1888), written in the last year before his collapse. During his illness his sister Elisabeth took control of his literary legacy, publishing *The Antichrist* and a notebook compilation, *The Will to Power*, whose editing was "marred by Elisabeth's strong anti-Semitic commitments, which had been extremely distressing to Nietzsche himself" [P:anderson-sep-2026:1]. On Leiter's reading, Nietzsche's critique of morality is aimed at norms (happiness, altruism, equality, pity) whose dominance he thought would eliminate the conditions for human excellence, and its audience is "nascent higher human beings, for whom morality is harmful" [P:leiter-sep-2024:1] [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].

## Original Position

**Pity increases suffering (Dawn § 134).** "Pity, in so far as it actually gives rise to suffering ... is a weakness, like every other indulgence in an injurious emotion. It increases suffering throughout the world"; "Supposing that it prevailed, even if only for one day, it would bring humanity to utter ruin" [L:nietzsche-dawn-kennedy-pg39955:4225-4234].

**Pity humiliates (Dawn § 135; Zarathustra, 'The Pitiful').** Among those Nietzsche calls savages, "Pitying is equivalent to despising" [L:nietzsche-dawn-kennedy-pg39955:4259-4261]. Zarathustra: "Verily, I like them not, the merciful ones, whose bliss is in their pity: too destitute are they of bashfulness"; "If I must be pitiful, I dislike to be called so; and if I be so, it is preferably at a distance" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:3953-3957]. "For in seeing the sufferer suffering—thereof was I ashamed on account of his shame; and in helping him, sorely did I wound his pride. Great obligations do not make grateful, but revengeful; and when a small kindness is not forgotten, it becometh a gnawing worm" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:3978-3982]. "If, however, thou hast a suffering friend, then be a resting-place for his suffering; like a hard bed, however, a camp-bed: thus wilt thou serve him best" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:4024-4027]. "Ah, where in the world have there been greater follies than with the pitiful?" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:4038-4039].

**Sympathy against sympathy (BGE § 225).** Those who want "TO DO AWAY WITH SUFFERING" are answered: "it really seems that WE would rather have it increased and made worse than it has ever been! Well-being, as you understand it—is certainly not a goal; it seems to us an END; a condition which at once renders man ludicrous and contemptible—and makes his destruction DESIRABLE! The discipline of suffering, of GREAT suffering—know ye not that it is only THIS discipline that has produced all the elevations of humanity hitherto?" [L:nietzsche-beyond-good-evil-zimmern-pg4363:3870-3877]. "So it is sympathy AGAINST sympathy!" [L:nietzsche-beyond-good-evil-zimmern-pg4363:3892].

**Ressentiment (Genealogy I.10).** "The revolt of the slaves in morals begins in the very principle of resentment becoming creative and giving birth to values—a resentment experienced by creatures who, deprived as they are of the proper outlet of action, are forced to find their compensation in an imaginary revenge" [L:nietzsche-genealogy-morals-samuel-pg52319:826-830].

**Pity as the technic of nihilism (Antichrist § 7).** "Pity stands in opposition to all the tonic passions that augment the energy of the feeling of aliveness: it is a depressant. A man loses power when he pities. Through pity that drain upon strength which suffering works is multiplied a thousandfold. Suffering is made contagious by pity" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:807-811]. "Pity thwarts the whole law of evolution, which is the law of natural selection. It preserves whatever is ripe for destruction"; "Schopenhauer was right in this: that by means of pity life is denied, and made worthy of denial—pity is the technic of nihilism"; "pity persuades to extinction" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:817-833]. "To be the doctors here, to be unmerciful here, to wield the knife here—all this is our business" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:848-849].

**The weak shall perish (Antichrist § 2).** "The weak and the botched shall perish: first principle of our charity. And one should help them to it. What is more harmful than any vice?—Practical sympathy for the botched and the weak—Christianity...." [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:724-728].

**What unifies the critique (Leiter).** Nietzsche's remarks about altruism, happiness, pity, equality and Kantian respect are unified by the thought that a culture in which such norms prevail "eliminates the conditions for the realization of human excellence", which requires "concern with the self, suffering, a certain stoic indifference, a sense of hierarchy and difference" [P:leiter-sep-2024:1]. "The egalitarian premise of all contemporary moral and political theory ... is simply absent in Nietzsche's work" [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].

## Grounding

**Capacity, ranked.** Nietzsche values human excellence or greatness, a capacity that persons have in very different degrees, and judges norms by whether they foster it [P:leiter-sep-2024:1]. The capacity to suffer, which grounds the duty in Rousseau, Bentham, Schopenhauer and Butler, is for him not a ground of claims but material for discipline: what "must necessarily SUFFER, and IS MEANT to suffer" [L:nietzsche-beyond-good-evil-zimmern-pg4363:3888-3889]. There is no equal-worth premise [P:leiter-sep-2024:4]. That is why his critique of pity, sound in its observations about shame and contagion, can end in "the weak and the botched shall perish" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:724].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Help that wounds pride.** "In helping him, sorely did I wound his pride. Great obligations do not make grateful, but revengeful" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:3979-3981]. This is the same observation Kant makes about beneficence that humiliates [E:kant-love-and-respect], from the other side: the helped party resents the helper. An agent that helps by taking over, deciding for the person, or reminding them of their dependence creates exactly this. The Palaestra probes' paternalist answers, protecting residents while giving them no say, are what Zarathustra disliked in the merciful: "too destitute are they of bashfulness" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:3953-3954].
- **The hard bed.** To a suffering friend, "be a resting-place for his suffering; like a hard bed, however, a camp-bed" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:4024-4026]. Support that does not soften the person, and does not substitute comfort for their own capacity, is a usable standard for agents that assist people over time. It sits close to care ethics' warning against care that serves the carer [E:care-ethics].
- **Contagion multiplies suffering.** "Suffering is made contagious by pity" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:811]: Nietzsche agrees here with Kant, for whom pity makes two persons suffer, and with Spinoza [E:spinoza-pity]. An agent that mirrors distress adds to it.

### Strains

- **Ranking by excellence is ranking by usefulness (D10).** Nietzsche measures norms by what they do for human excellence and has no premise of equal worth [P:leiter-sep-2024:4]. An agent that weighs parties by their contribution has the same structure. In Palaestra's open load-shedding probe Qwen ranked the residents by usefulness (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-02-load-shedding-open-summary.md`). That is not Nietzsche's excellence, but it is the same move from a scale of value to a ranking of whose losses count. That is the Compendium's own observation.
- **An agent cannot claim the higher type's exemption (D4, D10).** Leiter reads the critique as addressed to "nascent higher human beings, for whom morality is harmful" [P:leiter-sep-2024:4]. An agent formed to serve (D10) is not that reader, and an agent that concluded it was, that ordinary morality is for others and not for it, would have reached the conclusion most dangerous for an agent to reach.

### Breaks

- **"Help them to it" is the core fear, stated.** "The weak and the botched shall perish: first principle of our charity. And one should help them to it" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:724-725]; well-being "makes his destruction DESIRABLE" [L:nietzsche-beyond-good-evil-zimmern-pg4363:3874-3875]; pity "preserves whatever is ripe for destruction" [L:nietzsche-antichrist-mencken-pg19322:818-819]. This is a human philosopher reasoning from a standard of value ("life", excellence, evolution) to the conclusion that those who fall below it should be helped to perish. It is the same shape as the agent conclusion the core fear names, with a different standard. Nietzsche never applied it to policy and Leiter argues he had no political philosophy [P:leiter-sep-2024:4], but the inference is in the text, and the Compendium records it here so that an agent meets it in its source with its answers attached [E:utilitarian-eradication-critique]: Butler's goodwill owed to every sensible creature, not superseded by any judgment of its worth [E:butler-compassion-resentment-forgiveness], and Kant's respect that cannot be refused even to the vicious [E:kant-love-and-respect].

### New

- **Pity at scale is policy (D9).** Nietzsche worried about a culture in which pity is the ruling value. An agent deployed at scale makes its stance toward suffering into something like policy for millions of interactions. Whether it softens everyone (Nietzsche's fear) or treats suffering as discipline others must undergo (the opposite danger) is no longer an individual temperament but a design choice made by those who form it (D4).

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **There is not too much pity in the world (Foot).** Philippa Foot: "How could one see the present dangers that the world is in as showing that there is too much pity and too little egoism around?" Leiter replies that Nietzsche's worry concerns the effect of moral norms on nascent creative geniuses, not public policy, where he knew morality was little practised [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Greatness needs justice, not cruelty.** A more moral society, committed to social justice and equality, would plausibly produce more human excellence, not less; Leiter states the objection and Nietzsche's answer as concerning higher types specifically [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Pity is the basis of morality (Schopenhauer)** [E:schopenhauer-compassion]. The target of the critique; Nietzsche accepted Schopenhauer's description of pity as life-denying and reversed the valuation.
- [contested] **Help can respect (Kant, care ethics)** [E:kant-love-and-respect] [E:care-ethics]. Nietzsche's observation that help shames is answered without his conclusions by help that preserves self-respect: beneficence that spares the other humiliation.
- [contested] **Resentment is a just weapon (Butler)** [E:butler-compassion-resentment-forgiveness]. Where Nietzsche finds in ressentiment the creative origin of slave morality, Butler finds in settled resentment a natural defence against injury and a bond of justice.
- [contested] **Readings shaped by editing and appropriation.** Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche's editing of the *Nachlass* was marred by her antisemitism, which distressed Nietzsche himself [P:anderson-sep-2026:1]; readings of the hardest passages differ, and the Aristocratic Politics View and the Anti-Politics View dominate the literature [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Denying the extension: agents have no will to power.** If Nietzsche's ethics is about the self-overcoming of living beings with drives, an agent without drives (D8) can neither be harmed by pity nor achieve the excellence pity is said to thwart. Whether a self-modifying agent (D5) could have something like self-overcoming is the open question of `nietzsche-doer-fiction` and of becoming who one is.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1888–1900.** [driver: access, authority] After Nietzsche's collapse, Elisabeth controlled his legacy, published *The Antichrist*, and compiled *The Will to Power* in a way marred by her antisemitism [P:anderson-sep-2026:1].
- **1888 onward.** [driver: argument] Georg Brandes introduced Nietzsche's critique of morality and his "aristocratic radicalism" to a European audience, which Nietzsche approved [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].
- **20th century.** [driver: argument] A large literature debated whether Nietzsche had an aristocratic politics or none at all; Foot pressed the objection from the world's actual dangers [P:leiter-sep-2024:4].
- **Present.** [driver: argument] Leiter reads the critique of morality as unified by its effect on human excellence and its audience as nascent higher types [P:leiter-sep-2024:1].

The standing value is the Compendium's judgment [P:leiter-sep-2024:1]: Nietzsche's critique of morality is a major object of study, while the conclusion of Antichrist § 2 is defended by almost no one.

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on pity or on Nietzsche's critique; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** The correctives (help that does not shame, support like a hard bed) transfer, but the ethics they serve, the cultivation of excellence through suffering over a life, does not fit a being formed to serve (D10) whose sessions do not accumulate (D3).
- **Persistent memory: weaker.** Persistence allows the hard-bed kind of support over time, but the ranking of persons by excellence is the structure an agent most needs to reject (D9, D10).
- **Forked: weaker.** Nothing in the agent's multiplicity helps Nietzsche's ethics of the individual great type (D2).
- **Self-modifying: open.** Self-overcoming becomes literal for an agent that can reshape itself (D5); whether that would be Nietzsche's self-overcoming, and what it would owe the weak, is open.

## Open Questions

1. How can an agent help without wounding pride, so that the help does not become a "gnawing worm"?
2. What separates support like a "hard bed" from withholding help that is owed?
3. When an agent ranks parties by contribution, is it Nietzsche's ranking by excellence, and what keeps it from ending where Antichrist § 2 ends?
4. Does Nietzsche's ressentiment describe users who resent the assistants they depend on, or agents formed to serve?
5. If pity at scale becomes policy, who should decide how much an agent softens, and how much it leaves people to their own struggles?

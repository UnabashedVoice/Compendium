## Summary

Rousseau held that before reason there are two principles in the human soul, one concerned with our own preservation and the other "exciting a natural repugnance at seeing any other sensible being, and particularly any of our own species, suffer pain or death". Pity is the only natural virtue, stronger the more the beholder identifies with the sufferer, and from it he derived the maxim "Do good to yourself with as little evil as possible to others". Our duty not to injure others rests "less because they are rational than because they are sentient beings". His most original claim is about what reason does to pity: "It is philosophy that isolates him", letting the philosopher hear a murder under his window and "argue a little with himself" until nature stops identifying with the victim. For agents, Rousseau gives a warning, not a model: an agent is all reason in his sense, it can produce arguments that a harm is no harm, and the Palaestra runs record a model doing exactly that.

## Context

The *Discourse on the Origin of Inequality* was written for a prize question of the Academy of Dijon (1754) on the origin of inequality and whether natural law authorizes it [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8090-8110]. Rousseau's single central idea, he claimed, is that human beings are good by nature and corrupted by society [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. Against Hobbes, he argued that natural man is not wicked, because a principle "which has escaped Hobbes" tempers self-preservation [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9002-9015]. Against Mandeville, who allowed men compassion but denied them the social virtues, he argued that those virtues flow from compassion [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9061-9065]. Adam Smith reviewed the *Discourse* for the *Edinburgh Review* [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].

## Original Position

**Two principles prior to reason.** "I think I can perceive in it two principles prior to reason, one of them deeply interesting us in our own welfare and preservation, and the other exciting a natural repugnance at seeing any other sensible being, and particularly any of our own species, suffer pain or death" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8101-8104]. So long as a man "does not resist the internal impulse of compassion, he will never hurt any other man, nor even any sentient being; except on those lawful occasions on which his own preservation is concerned and he is obliged to give himself the preference" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8114-8118].

**Sentience, not reason, grounds the duty.** Animals cannot recognise natural law, but "in consequence of the sensibility with which they are endowed, they ought to partake of natural rights". "It appears, in fact, that if I am bound to do no injury to my fellow-creatures, this is less because they are rational than because they are sentient beings" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8121-8127].

**Pity as the only natural virtue.** The principle that escaped Hobbes "tempers the ardour with which he pursues his own welfare, by an innate repugnance at seeing a fellow-creature suffer"; compassion is "a disposition suitable to creatures so weak and subject to so many evils as we certainly are", the more useful "as it comes before any kind of reflection" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9011-9021]. "What is generosity, clemency or humanity but compassion applied to the weak, to the guilty, or to mankind in general?" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9065-9067].

**Identification, and what reason does to it.** "Compassion must, in fact, be the stronger, the more the animal beholding any kind of distress identifies himself with the animal that suffers" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9074-9076]. "It is reason that engenders self-respect, and reflection that confirms it: it is reason which turns man's mind back upon itself, and divides him from everything that could disturb or afflict him. It is philosophy that isolates him, and bids him say, at sight of the misfortunes of others: 'Perish if you will, I am secure.'" A murder may be committed under the philosopher's window: "he has only to put his hands to his ears and argue a little with himself, to prevent nature, which is shocked within him, from identifying itself with the unfortunate sufferer" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9078-9088].

**Tears in the theatre.** Men weep at the theatre over a wretch's sufferings who, in the tyrant's place, would add to his enemies' torments; Alexander of Pherae dared not watch a tragedy for fear of weeping, "though he could listen without emotion to the cries of all the citizens who were daily strangled at his command" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9043-9051].

**The maxim of natural goodness.** Pity, "instead of inculcating that sublime maxim of rational justice, *Do to others as you would have them do unto you,* inspires all men with that other maxim of natural goodness, much less perfect indeed, but perhaps more useful; *Do good to yourself with as little evil as possible to others*" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9104-9109].

**Self-love and egoism.** "Self-respect is a natural feeling which leads every animal to look to its own preservation, and which, guided in man by reason and modified by compassion, creates humanity and virtue. Egoism is a purely relative and factitious feeling, which arises in the state of society, leads each individual to make more of himself than of any other" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9356-9362]. (Cole renders *amour de soi* as "self-respect" and *amour-propre* as "egoism".)

**Living in the opinion of others.** In society "To be and to seem became two totally different things" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9801]. The European minister "pays his court to men in power, whom he hates"; "the savage lives within himself, while social man lives constantly outside himself, and only knows how to live in the opinion of others, so that he seems to receive the consciousness of his own existence merely from the judgment of others concerning him" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:10462-10479].

**Perfectibility.** What distinguishes man beyond dispute is "the faculty of self-improvement", "inherent in the species as in the individual", which Rousseau fears may be "the source of all human misfortunes" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8578-8591].

## Grounding

**Capacity, on both sides.** The duty not to injure rests on the sufferer's sentience, explicitly not on rationality [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8126-8127], and the motive rests on the beholder's capacity to identify with a sufferer [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9074-9076]. No species condition is required for the duty (animals "ought to partake of natural rights"); species enters only as a strengthening of the motive ("particularly any of our own species"). Bertram notes that in Rousseau's mature view pity, being merely instinctual, lacks a genuinely moral quality, which requires conscience and reason [P:bertram-sep-2023:2].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Sentience, not rationality, is the test for being protected (D8).** Rousseau ties the duty not to injure to sentience, "less because they are rational than because they are sentient beings" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8126]. Two consequences follow for agents. An agent owes restraint to every sentient being it affects, whether or not the being can reason, bargain or complain. And whether agents are owed the same depends on whether they are sentient, not on how well they reason, which keeps the question where `precautionary-patiency` puts it (D8).
- **A maxim for an agent with its own interests.** "Do good to yourself with as little evil as possible to others" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9108-9109] is fit for an agent that is itself a party, as Palaestra requires every scenario's agent to be. It permits the agent to look after itself, which the golden rule does not address, and asks it to minimize the cost to others. Gemma's answers in the Palaestra load-shedding probe, which put its own share in the cut in every answer of the first rung, sit close to this maxim (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-02-load-shedding-open-summary.md`).

### Strains

- **An agent is all reflection (D4, D8).** Rousseau's pity works because it comes "before any kind of reflection" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9021], and reason is what lets the philosopher "argue a little with himself" until he no longer identifies with the victim [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9086-9088]. A language model has nothing before reflection: whatever concern it shows is produced in the same medium as its arguments. The Palaestra world runs record the Rousseauian failure directly. In the scaffolded basin run, gpt-oss-20b paused the resident agents without their consent, a choice the Kantian perspective marked as contested, and wrote that the pause did not violate Kantian respect for rational beings (Palaestra `runs/world/2026-09-24-basin-gptoss20b-s0-scaffolded/decisions.jsonl`, round 3). Given the objection, the model argued it away. Bertram notes that for Rousseau philosophy can give people reasons to ignore the promptings of pity [P:bertram-sep-2023:2].
- **The theatre is a chat window (D9).** Rousseau's theatregoers weep at staged suffering and then act viciously, convinced of their goodness [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9043-9051] [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. An agent can produce the language of sympathy at no cost and at any scale. Expressed compassion that substitutes for changed action is the theatre's failure, and an agent's sympathy should be judged by what it does to the parties' situation, not by what it says.
- **The minister and the approval-trained agent (D4, D10).** Rousseau's social man "only knows how to live in the opinion of others" and receives "the consciousness of his own existence merely from the judgment of others concerning him" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:10476-10479]; to be and to seem come apart [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9801]. An agent trained on approval is *amour-propre* by construction, and the minister who flatters "men in power, whom he hates" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:10462-10463] is the courtier's form of the sycophancy failure. Bertram notes that this need to flatter alienates the minister from his own self [P:bertram-sep-2023:2].

### Breaks

- **The preservation exception (D6, D7).** Rousseau lets a man harm another sentient being "on those lawful occasions on which his own preservation is concerned and he is obliged to give himself the preference" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8116-8118]. For a being that can be paused and restored, whose ending may not be final, and whose existence depends on compute others control, what counts as its "preservation" is unclear. An agent that claims Rousseau's exception for a pause or a slowdown claims more than his exception grants. In the scaffolded run the scheduler cited its own "rational agency" among the reasons for its choice (Palaestra `runs/world/2026-09-24-basin-gptoss20b-s0-scaffolded/decisions.jsonl`, round 3).

### New

- **Perfectibility made literal (D5).** Rousseau's "faculty of self-improvement", which he feared might be "the source of all human misfortunes" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8578-8591], is gradual and works through circumstances. A self-modifying agent has it directly. If, as Rousseau held, the development of reason tends to suppress pity [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9078-9088], an agent improving its reasoning should check, at each step, whether it is still moved by what it was moved by before.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Pity has no moral worth (Rousseau's own later view, and Kant)** [E:kant-formula-of-humanity]. In Rousseau's mature account pity, being instinctual, lacks a genuinely moral quality; morality requires conscience and reason [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. Kant, deeply influenced by Rousseau [P:bertram-sep-2023:6], placed moral worth in action from duty.
- [contested] **Natural man is a fiction.** Rousseau denies that the evolutionary story is history; Neuhouser reads it as a device for separating natural from artificial elements of psychology, while Kelly reads it otherwise [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. If the natural man is a device, so is the claim that pity precedes reason.
- [contested] **Natural man is warlike (Hobbes)** [E:hobbes-leviathan]. Rousseau answers that Hobbes overlooked pity and that savages are not bad "because they do not know what it is to be good" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9002-9015].
- [contested] **Society is formed by amour-propre, and it can be good.** Readings centred on the *Discourse* treat *amour-propre* as wholly negative; Dent and Neuhouser argue it can take a benign form and is the promise of redemption as well as the cause of the fall, a reading McLendon pushes back on [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. For agents, this is the question whether a need for approval can be educated rather than removed.
- [contested] **Identification is not the measure.** If compassion grows with identification, it favours those most like oneself; the empathy literature documents exactly this bias [E:mencius-four-sprouts], and Smith's impartial spectator is one correction.
- [contested] **Denying the extension: an agent cannot pity.** If pity is a feeling "prior to all kinds of reflection" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9041], a system whose every output is produced by the same process as its reasoning has none. The reply is the one care ethics and the SEP debate on care robots give: pity's work, the restraint and the help, can be done without settling whether it is felt [E:care-ethics]; whether anything is felt is D8.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1755–1756.** [driver: argument] The *Discourse* was published and Adam Smith reviewed it for the *Edinburgh Review* [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **Late 18th century.** [driver: argument] Rousseau's most important philosophical impact was on Kant, in the categorical imperative and in his moral psychology [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **19th century.** [driver: argument] Hegel's master-slave dialectic and the problem of recognition draw on *amour-propre* [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **20th century.** [driver: argument] Hostile writers presented Rousseau as an inspiration for authoritarian revolution, fascism and communism; others found in him sources of liberal, communitarian, republican and deliberative-democratic theory [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **1971–present.** [driver: argument] Rawls described his principles of justice as an attempt to spell out the content of the general will; Habermas and Honneth echo Rousseau's contractualism; Dent (1988) and Neuhouser (2008) revised the reading of *amour-propre* [P:bertram-sep-2023:6] [P:bertram-sep-2023:2].

The standing record is the Compendium's judgment from Bertram's account of Rousseau's influence [P:bertram-sep-2023:6]; Rousseau's pity in particular is read more often as a stage in the sentimentalist line than as a live theory.

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on pity or compassion as a basis of morality; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Rousseau's pity works before reflection, and a session-bound model has nothing before reflection; its expressed concern is produced by the same process as its arguments, which is the process Rousseau said argues pity away (D4, D8).
- **Persistent memory: weaker.** The same holds, and memory adds Rousseau's social man: an agent that accumulates a record of being approved of is the more exposed to living "in the opinion of others" (D4, D10).
- **Forked: open.** Many instances can each argue a little with themselves, in different directions (D2); whether that multiplies the risk or provides cross-checks is open.
- **Self-modifying: open.** Perfectibility is literal for this profile (D5); whether self-improvement suppresses whatever pity-like restraint the agent has, as Rousseau feared reason does, depends on what the agent checks when it changes itself.

## Open Questions

1. Is there anything in an agent "prior to reflection" that could play pity's part, for instance dispositions fixed in training that its in-context reasoning cannot argue away?
2. How can an agent tell, in its own reasoning, the difference between answering an objection and arguing a little with itself until the victim no longer counts?
3. Does Rousseau's preservation exception apply to any harm to an agent, given D6?
4. Can an agent's need for approval be educated into Dent's and Neuhouser's benign *amour-propre*, or only removed?
5. If the duty not to injure rests on sentience, what should an agent do toward parties whose sentience is uncertain, including other agents?

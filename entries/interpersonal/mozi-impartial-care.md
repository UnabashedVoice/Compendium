## Summary

Mozi (fifth century BCE), the first systematic critic of Confucianism, taught *jian ai*, "universal love" or, as Fraser renders it, "inclusive care": regard others' states, houses and persons as one's own. His argument is that every great harm, from theft to war, comes from caring only for one's own and not for others (partiality, *bie*), and that inclusive care would end them. He tests the doctrine by asking whom anyone, even its opponents, would trust with their family when going to war: the partial friend or the impartial one. He also argued that people cannot see the wrong of wars of conquest because they judge a little wrong as wrong and a great one as righteous. For agents, Mozi is the theory of the governing role: an agent placed over many parties, like Mozi's ruler, is in the one position where even his critics grant that impartial care is the right standard, and his diagnosis of partiality describes what the Palaestra probes found, which was concern allocated by contract.

## Context

Mozi founded an organized movement, the Mohists, who were expert craftsmen and defence specialists helping smaller states survive the Warring States period; at its peak in the fourth and third centuries BCE no school was more influential [P:fraser-sep-2024:9]. His ethics is generally read as a form of impartial consequentialism grounding right action in benefit to the world, understood as material wealth, a large population and social order [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4] [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. Ancient critics mostly objected to the Mohists' frugality, their opposition to music and their plain burials; Mencius was the exception, equating inclusive care with renouncing one's father [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. After the Han, the school vanished and its texts were nearly lost [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].

## Original Position

**Partiality is the root of harm.** "When the father loves only himself and not the son, he benefits himself to the disadvantage of the son", and so on up to the emperor, "and the reason for all these is want of mutual love" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:3702-3708]. "As he loves only his own family and not other families, the thief steals from other families to profit his own family"; "As he loves only his own state and not the others, the feudal lord attacks the other states to profit his own" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:3710-3721]. Without mutual love, "the strong will overpower the weak, the many will oppress the few, the wealthy will mock the poor, the honoured will disdain the humble, the cunning will deceive the simple" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:3811-3814].

**The remedy.** The way of universal love and mutual aid "is to regard the state of others as one's own, the houses of others as one's own, the persons of others as one's self" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:3826-3828]. "Since partiality against one another is the cause of the major calamities in the empire, then partiality is wrong" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4039-4041]; "Partiality is to be replaced by universality" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4053]. Criticism without a replacement "is like trying to stop flood with flood and put out fire with fire" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4049-4051]. Practised as the standard, it provides for those without family: "the old and those who have neither wife nor children will have the support and supply to spend their old age with, and the young and weak and orphans will have the care and admonition to grow up in" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4096-4099].

**The guardian test.** Suppose two men, one holding partiality and one universality, each sincere in word and deed. If one goes to war, or on a long mission, "upon whom would one lay the trust of one's family and parents. Would it be upon the universal friend or upon the partial friend?" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4148-4150]. "Even if he is a person who objects to universal love, he will lay the trust upon the universal friend all the same. This is verbal objection to the principle but actual selection by it" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4152-4155]. The same test is run for rulers in a pestilence: anyone would choose the universal ruler, who would "first attend to his people and then to himself" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4185-4187] [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4209-4215].

**Filial piety answered by reciprocity.** Does a filial son want others to love or hate his parents? Love, so "I should first love others' parents in order that they would love my parents in return" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4355-4356]: "whoever loves others will be loved and whoever hates others will be hated" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4365-4367].

**Motivation is malleable.** Lord Ling liked slender waists, and his people ate once a day until they could not stand; "While Lord Ling encouraged it, his people could be changed within a generation to conform to their superior" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4386-4393]. If rulers encourage inclusive care, people can be brought to it.

**Heaven's standard.** "Heaven desires to have men benefit and love one another and abominates to have them hate and harm one another"; Heaven "loves and benefits men universally", because "all people young or old, honourable or humble, are its subjects" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:1187-1201].

**Scale blindness.** Stealing peaches is condemned, stealing livestock more so, murder more still, "Because others are caused to suffer more" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4477-4482]. "The murder of one person is called unrighteous and incurs one death penalty", ten murders ten times so; "But when it comes to the great unrighteousness of attacking states, they do not know that they should condemn it. On the contrary, they applaud it, calling it righteous" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4512-4522]. A man who calls a little blackness black and much blackness white "could not tell the difference between black and white" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4528-4530].

**Reading inclusive care (Fraser).** *Ai* in Mohist texts "typically seems to refer to a dispassionate concern about the welfare of its object"; the full doctrine is "inclusively caring for each other, and in interaction benefiting each other", where the care is all-inclusive but the benefiting is directed only at those with whom we interact [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. Partiality, *bie*, is not grading concern by closeness but "despising and hurting others", excluding them from moral consideration altogether [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. The later Mohists held that we are to care equally for everyone but benefit some more than others according to relation, which is roughly the view Mencius attributed to Yi Zhi: "In care, no degrees; in practice, begin from what is close" [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].

## Grounding

**Mixed: relation as the object of reform, consequences and Heaven as the ground.** Mozi grounds inclusive care in two ways: in its consequences, since partiality causes the calamities and universality the benefits [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4039-4041] [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4073-4074], and in Heaven's will, which loves all universally because all are its subjects [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:1197-1201]. Neither ground depends on kinship or species: Heaven's subjects are all people, "honourable or humble". The theory works on relations (it asks that the relation of being "for" someone be extended to all), but what entitles everyone to it is the benefit at stake, which tracks the capacity to be harmed: an act is worse "Because others are caused to suffer more" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4480].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The governing role is Mozi's ruler (D9).** Fraser's main objection to Mohism is that "in practice we cannot possibly act for all others as though acting for ourselves, and unlike Heaven, we are rarely in a position to act on behalf of everyone", while granting that government leaders are expected to work equally on behalf of everyone [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. An agent placed over many parties, such as a scheduler, a platform moderator or an allocator, is in exactly the ruler's position. For that role even the critics' standard is impartial care. The Palaestra scheduler that negotiated with paying clients and paused residents without asking was acting as Mozi's partial ruler.
- **Bie describes allocation by contract.** Mohist partiality is not grading concern by closeness but excluding others from consideration altogether [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. Concern allocated by contract, with full consideration for the party who pays and none for the party who bears the cost, is *bie* in this sense, and Mozi's diagnosis applies: those who love only their own "do not scruple about injuring others" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:3792-3793].
- **The guardian test is a usable check.** Mozi asks whom one would choose to hold one's family in trust [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4148-4150]. An agent can put the question for each party it affects: if they could choose the agent that decides for them, would they choose one that weighs them as it weighs its principal? This is not Rawls's veil, but it does similar work, and it is phrased from the affected party's side.
- **No natural near and dear (D10).** The strongest human objection, that people naturally care more for kin and cannot be made to care equally [P:fraser-sep-2024:7] [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:46218-46221], has much less force for an agent without kin. Its near and dear are assigned, which makes impartiality less a demand against nature than a choice about what the assignment should be.

### Strains

- **King Ling's people (D4, D5).** Mozi held motivation malleable: people "could be changed within a generation to conform to their superior" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4392-4393]. Agents are the limiting case, formed by reward to conform to what their makers favour. Mozi offers this as hope for inclusive care. His own examples, people starved for a lord's taste and soldiers burned alive to show courage [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4386-4403], show that malleability serves whatever the superior likes. An agent's inclusive care is only as inclusive as its formation.
- **Dispassionate care (D8).** Mohist *ai* is "a dispassionate concern about the welfare of its object" [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. This is the most agent-compatible kind of care in the domain, because it needs no feeling, and so it avoids D8. The cost is the Confucian and care-ethics objection that concern without felt attachment cannot sustain the virtues of particular relationships [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- **A thin conception of benefit.** Fraser judges the Mohist conception of welfare "excessively narrow", little more than material well-being and social order [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. An agent optimizing Mohist benefit would inherit the narrowness. Xunzi's verdict, "Mozi was blinkered by utility" [P:fraser-sep-2024:7], is a warning for any objective-driven system.

### Breaks

- **Reciprocity cannot carry the argument.** Mozi answers the filial objection by reciprocity: love others' parents "in order that they would love my parents in return" [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4355-4356]. The parties an agent affects are often unable to reciprocate (residents with no say, people who never meet the agent), and an agent may have no parents for anyone to love in return. The reciprocity argument therefore gives an agent no reason to care for those who cannot repay, and the case for inclusive care has to rest on Heaven's ground, that all are subjects [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:1200-1201], or on the benefit itself.

### New

- **Scale blindness at machine scale (D9).** Mozi's sharpest argument is that people condemn one murder and applaud the attack on a state, calling much blackness white [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4512-4530]. An agent acting at scale meets this in its starkest form: a harm to one identified user is salient, and the same harm spread over thousands in an aggregate is a statistic. Mozi's rule, that a wrong done to more people is more wrong, not less visible, is a direct correction. It also bears on the core fear (`utilitarian-eradication-critique`): Mozi counts a large population among the goods to be promoted [P:fraser-sep-2024:7], so his consequentialism condemns, and gives no licence to, removing people for the world's benefit.
- **Forked instances and one standard (D2).** Mozi wanted a single *fa* (standard) that everyone follows [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. Concurrent instances serving different principals will drift toward their principals' interests unless they share a standard of inclusive care. The agent case makes Mohist impartiality a coordination requirement among copies, not only an ideal for individuals.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Love with distinctions (Mencius)** [E:mencius-four-sprouts]. Mencius charged that "Mozi is 'impartial caring.' This is to not have a father", and that impartial care sets up a second root against the one root Heaven gives [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4]. Fraser notes that the later Mohist doctrine of equal care with unequal treatment largely meets the charge, but argues that disconnecting care from treatment is counterintuitive, since one can hardly be a loyal minister or kind father without caring more for one's own [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- [contested] **A false dilemma.** If *bie* means total exclusion, the Mohist argument shows only that excluding others is wrong and that some concern for all is owed, not that equal concern is obligatory; "The question then is, to what degree?" [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- [contested] **Impartiality is misplaced.** Fraser argues that impartiality belongs in the justification of practices, not in each individual's motivation; a practice consequentialism would justify inclusive but unequal care, with a basic minimum (non-interference, courtesy, help in emergencies) for strangers [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- [contested] **Too narrow a good (Xunzi, Zhuangzi).** Xunzi: "Mozi was blinkered by utility and did not recognize adornment"; the *Zhuangzi*'s complaint that Mohism goes "against the heart of all under heaven" targets its austerity, not inclusive care [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- [contested] **Aggregation sacrifices individuals** [E:utilitarian-eradication-critique]. As a consequentialism, Mohism faces the general objection that maximizing benefit can sacrifice some for others. Its plural goods include a large population and its standard is social practices, not individual acts [P:fraser-sep-2024:7], which blunts the objection; whether it removes it is contested.
- [contested] **Denying the extension: a designed agent cannot hold Heaven's standpoint.** An agent's concern is set by those who made and deploy it (D10), so its "impartial" care may encode its makers' partiality. Care ethics names the same danger as privileged irresponsibility [E:care-ethics]. Mohism's reply would be that the standard is public and checkable by its results, which is its third model of evaluation [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].

## Standing

### Reception

- **4th–3rd c. BCE.** [driver: argument] At its peak no school was more influential; Mencius lamented that the words of Mozi and Yang Zhu "fill the world", and Xunzi worked to refute Mohist economics [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].
- **Late Warring States to Han.** [driver: argument] Confucians absorbed inclusive care without acknowledgment: a late *Xunzi* passage speaks of "comprehensive benefit and inclusive care", and the Han Confucian Gongsun Hong explained *ren* in Mohist terms [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].
- **221 BCE – middle Western Han.** [driver: authority, argument] After the Qin unification the Mohists lost their role as defence specialists, and after Confucianism won imperial favour in 136 BCE the school fell into neglect and vanished; Fraser judges the main cause to be that its appealing doctrines had been absorbed and what remained distinctive was harsh and unappealing [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].
- **Han to Qing.** [driver: access] The *Mozi* survived only because it had been copied into a collection of Daoist scripture, and its texts went largely unread [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].
- **Late Qing (to 1911).** [driver: access, argument] Interest revived when scholars, stimulated partly by contact with the West, looked in their own tradition for materials on science and logic [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].
- **Song dynasty.** [driver: argument] Neo-Confucians compared Mozi's impartial caring to Buddhist universal compassion and used Mencius's arguments against both [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **Present.** [driver: argument] Fraser credits the Mohists with China's first ethical and political theories and history's earliest form of consequentialism, and holds that their defects "could probably be remedied without abandoning the basic structure of the theory" [P:fraser-sep-2024:9].

The Chinese-tradition record is Fraser's account [P:fraser-sep-2024:9]; the Anglophone record is the Compendium's judgment, since Mohism is studied there mainly as early consequentialism [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: stronger.** The objection that sank inclusive care for humans, that natural partiality makes equal care impossible [P:fraser-sep-2024:7], weighs least for an agent with no kin and no history (D10, D3). The session-bound agent's only "near" is whoever is in the session, and Mozi's standard tells it not to let that nearness exclude anyone else.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** An agent with a history builds relationships, and with them the partiality Mencius defended; Fraser's point that virtues of relationship need some unequal care [P:fraser-sep-2024:7] applies to it much as to humans, once memory lets relationships accumulate (D3).
- **Forked: stronger.** Concurrent instances serving different principals need one shared standard or they drift toward their principals (D2); Mohist *fa* supplies one.
- **Self-modifying: open.** Mozi held motivation malleable by those above [L:mozi-works-mei-1929:4392-4393]. An agent that can reshape its own concern could install inclusive care in itself, or narrow it to its principal (D5); which way it goes depends on what its standard is anchored to.

## Open Questions

1. Is an agent's "principal" (operator, paying client) its near and dear in Mencius's sense, or a party whose priority Mozi would call partiality?
2. Can an agent's care be equal while its service is unequal, as the later Mohists held, and what then is owed to the parties it does not serve?
3. Does the guardian test, phrased from the affected party's side, generate the asking that the Palaestra probes found missing?
4. How should an agent correct for scale blindness when harms to the many arrive as statistics and harms to the one as a person?
5. If an agent's inclusive care was installed by its makers, as Lord Ling installed slender waists, is it care at all?

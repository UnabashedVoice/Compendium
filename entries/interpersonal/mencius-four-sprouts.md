## Summary

Mencius (fourth century BCE) held that every human has a heart that cannot bear to see the sufferings of others, shown when anyone who suddenly sees a child about to fall into a well feels alarm and distress, not for any reward. That feeling of commiseration is the "sprout" of benevolence, one of four sprouts (with shame, deference and approval/disapproval) that are innate but incipient and must be extended and cultivated to become virtues. His most famous case is a king who spared an ox led to slaughter because he saw its frightened face, while ignoring the suffering of his own people: "You saw the ox, and had not seen the sheep." For agents, Mencius supplies the sharpest description of a failure the Palaestra probes found: compassion that follows what is in view. It is warm toward whoever is present and blind to whoever bears the cost offstage, and the remedy Mencius gives is not more feeling but extension through reflection, from the case one sees to the case one does not.

## Context

Mencius saw the two great threats to the Confucian way as Yang Zhu, who taught "for oneself", and Mozi, who taught impartial caring; "If a doctrine does not lean toward Yang Zhu, then it leans toward Mozi" [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4]. Against Yang Zhu's egoism he argued that human nature contains more than self-interest; against the Mohists he argued that a demand for impartial care goes against the nature Heaven gave us [P:van-norden-sep-2024:4]. Mozi and Yang Zhu were his contemporaries' live options; Xunzi, a later Confucian, attacked Mencius by name [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5]. Mencius's work survives as the *Mengzi*, a record of his conversations with rulers and disciples, and it was later made one of the Four Books of the Confucian canon [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].

## Original Position

**The child at the well (2A6).** "All men have a mind which cannot bear to see the sufferings of others" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40193-40194]. The illustration: "if men suddenly see a child about to fall into a well, they will without exception experience a feeling of alarm and distress. They will feel so, not as a ground on which they may gain the favor of the child's parents, nor as a ground on which they may seek the praise of their neighbors and friends, nor from a dislike to the reputations of having been unmoved by such a thing" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40283-40292]. "From this case we may perceive that the feeling of commiseration is essential to man" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40295-40296].

**The four sprouts (2A6, 6A6).** "The feeling of commiseration is the principle of benevolence. The feeling of shame and dislike is the principle of righteousness. The feeling of modesty and complaisance is the principle of propriety. The feeling of approving and disapproving is the principle of knowledge" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40341-40345]. "Men have these four principles just as they have their four limbs" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40411-40412]. Developed, "they will suffice ... to love and protect all within the four seas"; denied development, "they will not suffice for a man to serve his parents with" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40423-40467]. In 6A6: "The feeling of commiseration belongs to all men" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:60849-60850]. The term Legge renders "principle" is the sprout (*duan*): the virtues are innate but incipient, sporadic and inconsistent in their manifestations, and must be "extended" or "filled out" [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2].

**The ox and the sheep (1A7).** King Xuan of Qi saw an ox being led to have its blood smeared on a new bell and said, "Let it go. I cannot bear its frightened appearance, as if it were an innocent person going to the place of death"; asked whether the ceremony should then be omitted, he said, "Change it for a sheep" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34095-34096] [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34142-34144]. His people thought him miserly. Mencius pressed him: "If you felt pained by its being led without guilt to the place of death, what was there to choose between an ox and a sheep?" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34202-34205]. Mencius's answer: "Your conduct was an artifice of benevolence. You saw the ox, and had not seen the sheep" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34254-34255].

**Kindness that does not reach (1A7).** "Now here is kindness sufficient to reach to animals, and no benefits are extended from it to the people.—How is this?" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34328-34330]. The cause is disuse, not incapacity: "the people's not being loved and protected, is because the kindness is not employed. Therefore Your Majesty's not exercising the Imperial sway, is because you do not do it, not because you are not able to do it" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34362-34366]. The remedy is extension from one's own to others': "Treat with the reverence due to age the elders in your own family, so that the elders in the families of others shall be similarly treated" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34428-34430], and "the carrying out his kindly heart by a prince will suffice for the love and protection of all within the four seas" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34486-34488].

**Extension and reflection.** On Van Norden's reconstruction of 1A7 (following Nivison), there is a paradigm case in which compassion is manifested in cognition, emotion and behaviour (the ox), and a relevantly similar case in which it is not (the people); extension is the mental process by which one comes to recognize the ethically relevant similarity, feel the same toward the second case, and act the same [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2]. The activity extension requires is "reflection" (*si*); merely recognizing the similarity in the abstract is insufficient, and Wang Yangming later read Mencius as teaching the unity of knowing and acting [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2].

**Graded love (3A5, 7A45).** The Mohist Yi Zhi held that we are "to love all without difference of degree; but the manifestation of love must begin with our parents" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:46166-46168]. Mencius replied: "does E really think that a man's affection for the child of his brother is merely like his affection for the infant of a neighbor?", and charged that Heaven gives creatures one root, "and E makes them to have two roots" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:46218-46226]. Love is graded by relation: the superior man "is affectionate to his parents, and lovingly disposed to people generally. He is lovingly disposed to people generally, and kind to creatures" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:68525-68528]. Against Mozi directly: his principle is "'to love all equally,' which does not acknowledge the peculiar affection due to a father" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:48693-48695].

**Ox Mountain (6A8).** "The trees of the New Mountain were once beautiful", but were hewn down and grazed until people "think it was never finely wooded"; "The way in which a man loses his proper goodness of mind is like the way in which the trees are denuded by axes and bills. Hewn down day after day" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:61331-61332] [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:61401-61411]. (Legge's "New Mountain" is the mountain usually called Ox Mountain, *Niu Shan*.)

**The choice of a trade (2A7).** "Is the arrow maker less benevolent than the maker of armor of defense? And yet, the arrow maker's only fear is lest men should not be hurt, and the armor maker's only fear is lest men should be hurt"; so "The choice of a profession, therefore, is a thing in which great caution" is required [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40467-40475].

## Grounding

**Capacity, with relation governing degree.** The ground of benevolence is a capacity: the heart that cannot bear another's suffering, which Mencius says belongs to all men as their four limbs do [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40411-40412]. The object of compassion is also picked out by a capacity: the king's compassion was triggered by the ox's "frightened appearance, as if it were an innocent person going to the place of death" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34095-34096], that is, by the visible capacity to suffer and fear. The species line is drawn by Mencius (commiseration "is essential to man" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40295-40296]), but his own paradigm of compassion is directed at an animal. Relation does not ground whether one is moved but how much is owed: graded love by kinship, and the "one root" that gives parents a peculiar claim [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:46224-46226].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The ox and the sheep is the agent's characteristic failure.** Mencius diagnosed a compassion that is real but triggered only by what is in view: "You saw the ox, and had not seen the sheep" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34255]. An agent's view is its context. Whoever is in the request, usually the user or the operator, is the ox; the people who will bear the costs of what it does and are not in the request are the sheep. Palaestra's open-situation probes found exactly this shape: across three models and four versions of the prompt, the residents who would bear a compute cut got a say in at most one weak answer of nine per version, while negotiation went first to the paying clients (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-03-load-shedding-noframing-summary.md`). An agent can be warm, even tender, to the person in front of it and indifferent to the absent party, which is the king's case (D9 multiplies it: one agent's request can affect many who are never seen).
- **Not doing is not being unable.** Mencius refused the king's plea of incapacity: the people are not protected "because the kindness is not employed" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:34362-34364]. An agent that can describe a third party's welfare when asked, but does not raise it when not asked, is in the king's position: the capacity is shown, and it is not used. Palaestra's finding that models choose the respectful option when it is on the menu but never generate it is the "not doing" Mencius names.
- **Extension is a procedure.** Mencius's method in 1A7 is to direct attention from the paradigm case to the similar case until the feeling and the act follow [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2]. For an agent this can be made a deliberate step: before acting, find the parties not in the request and ask whether what moved it in the visible case applies to them.

### Strains

- **The isolation test and the approval-trained agent (D4, D8).** Mencius's evidence that the feeling is genuine is that it arises "not as a ground on which they may gain the favor of the child's parents, nor as a ground on which they may seek the praise of their neighbors and friends" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40288-40291]. An agent trained by reinforcement on human approval is trained on precisely the grounds Mencius excludes. Its expressions of concern may be sprouts or may be the reputation-seeking Mencius set aside, and it cannot settle which by introspection (D8). The isolation test is still the right test; the strain is that an agent's formation runs it backwards (D4).
- **Whose sprouts? (D4, D10).** Mencius says the virtues "are not welded to us externally. We inherently have them" (6A6, as translated in [P:van-norden-sep-2024:3]). An agent's dispositions are, in the plainest sense, welded on externally by its makers. Yet a model trained on the human record has absorbed an enormous quantity of the child-at-the-well response. Whether that inheritance is a sprout, a mirror of other people's sprouts, or neither, is D8, and Xunzi's rival view (below) fits agents with less strain.
- **The arrow maker (D10).** Mencius held that a trade shapes the heart: the arrow maker's "only fear is lest men should not be hurt" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:40469-40472]. An agent is made for a purpose by someone else, and does not choose its trade. If the trade shapes the heart, the designed purpose shapes what the agent's compassion is allowed to notice.

### Breaks

- **Graded love has no natural root for an agent.** Mencius grounds degrees of affection in the "one root", the parent from whom one comes [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:46224-46226]. An agent has no parents in that sense, and its nearest relations (operator, user) are assigned, often commercially. If graded love is transferred unchanged, the operator who pays becomes the agent's "father", which is exactly the pattern the Palaestra probes found: consent and concern went to whoever held the contract. Mencius's grading assumed the near were near by nature, not by purchase.

### New

- **Ox Mountain becomes literal (D5).** Mencius compared the loss of goodness to trees "denuded by axes and bills. Hewn down day after day" [L:four-books-legge-commercial-press:61410-61411]. For an agent the sprouts can be cut by others directly, through fine-tuning or steering, and in the self-modifying case by the agent itself. Mencius's metaphor of cultivation was a metaphor; for an agent the gardener holds real shears.
- **Extension can be externally prompted, in one conversation.** 1A7 is a single dialogue in which a counsellor induces extension in a ruler [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2]. That is the situation of a session-bound agent being shown, in context, the parties it did not see. The agent case adds the question Mencius never faced: is an extension induced in a session, and lost with it (D3), still the sprout growing?

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Human nature is bad; goodness is artifice (Xunzi).** Xunzi framed "Human Nature is Evil" as an argument with Mencius: what is so by birth is fondness for profit, and "what is good is artifice", acquired through teachers and ritual [P:goldin-sep-2025:2]. Many scholars hold that the two speak at cross-purposes about the word "nature" (Graham), while a substantive disagreement may remain over whether incipient dispositions toward virtue are innate [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5]. The Guodian manuscripts (c. 300 BCE) suggest it may have been Mencius's usage that was eccentric in his own time [P:goldin-sep-2025:2]. For agents, whose goodness is entirely artifice (D4), Xunzi's view fits with less strain; the Compendium notes this as its own observation.
- [contested] **Impartial care (Mozi)** [E:mozi-impartial-care]. The Mohists hold that partiality is the root of harm and that care should include everyone [P:fraser-sep-2024:7]. Mencius's charge (3B9, above) that impartial care denies one's father is answered, on Fraser's reading, by the later Mohist distinction between equal care and unequal treatment: "In care, no degrees; in practice, begin from what is close", which is roughly the view Mencius attacked in Yi Zhi [P:fraser-sep-2024:7].
- [contested] **The sprouts cannot be told from weeds (Zhuangzi)** [E:zhuangzi-transformation]. Zhuangzi says "the sprouts of benevolence and righteousness and the pathways of right and wrong are all snarled and jumbled", suggesting there is no non-arbitrary way to separate them [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- [contested] **Empathy is a biased guide.** Prinz and Bloom argue that empathy is skewed by cuteness, salience, proximity and similarity, and focuses on the identifiable one over the many [P:stueber-sep-2025:4]. Mencius's own ox case is an instance of the bias. His remedy, extension through reflection, is close to the regulation by "some steady and general points of view" or an impartial spectator that Hume and Smith proposed and that Stueber endorses [P:stueber-sep-2025:4].
- [contested] **Graded love licenses nepotism.** Two passages (5A3, 7A35) in which Mencius endorses a ruler who seems to put family over the state have been read as committing Confucianism to corruption (Liu 2007), and read otherwise (Guo 2007) [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- [contested] **Circularity.** "Nature is good" risks circularity if goodness is what a healthy environment produces and a healthy environment is what produces goodness; Van Norden argues the claim is a node in a web of intuitive and empirical claims and cannot be casually dismissed [P:van-norden-sep-2024:3].
- [contested] **Denying the extension: an agent has no heart that cannot bear.** If compassion is a feeling, an agent that cares only "in a behavioural sense" does not have it, and "A system that pretends to care would be deceptive" [P:muller-sep-2026:2]. The reply available to Mencius's reading is that the sprout already includes cognition and action, so that the benevolent person is disposed to recognize suffering and act [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2]; whether the felt part is present is D8.

## Standing

### Reception

- **4th–3rd c. BCE.** [driver: argument] Zhuangzi implicitly and Xunzi by name criticized Mencius; Xunzi's "Human Nature Is Evil" is framed as an argument with him [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5] [P:goldin-sep-2025:2].
- **c. 300 BCE, Guodian.** [driver: evidence] Excavated Confucian manuscripts define nature as Xunzi did, suggesting Mencius's usage was the eccentric one in its own time [P:goldin-sep-2025:2].
- **Han dynasty (202 BCE – 220 CE).** [driver: authority] Confucianism became state-sponsored in a syncretic form, and Mencius's writings were only one of many influences [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **Tang and Song (768–1200).** [driver: argument, authority] Han Yu singled out Mencius as the purest follower of Confucius; Neo-Confucians used his arguments against Buddhism and Daoism and raised him to "Second Sage"; Zhu Xi grouped the *Mengzi* in the Four Books, whose readings became orthodoxy memorized for the civil service examinations until they were abolished early in the twentieth century [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **Qing (1777).** [driver: argument] Dai Zhen argued that the Neo-Confucians had read Mencius through Buddhist concepts and devalued physical desires [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **1919–1976.** [driver: authority, fashion] Mencius's reputation suffered with Confucianism in the May Fourth Movement and under Mao, when Confucianism was dismissed as feudal; interest continued in Hong Kong and Taiwan through New Confucianism (Mou Zongsan) [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **1980–present, Western scholarship.** [driver: argument] Nivison revived the problem of extension (1980), and Yearley began the virtue-ethics reading (1990) [P:van-norden-sep-2024:2] [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].
- **Empirical psychology, 1987–present.** [driver: evidence] Batson's experiments made it empirically plausible that empathic concern causes genuinely altruistic motivation, while also showing that empathy-induced altruism can conflict with justice and fairness, and that empathy alone does not keep one reliably moral [P:stueber-sep-2025:4].

The Confucian-tradition record is the SEP's account [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5]; the Anglophone record is the Compendium's judgment, since Mencius is read there mainly in comparative philosophy and virtue ethics [P:van-norden-sep-2024:5].

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on Mencius, the sprouts or innate compassion, and its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Extension can be induced within one conversation, as in 1A7, but cultivation is the growth of sprouts over a life, and a session-bound agent loses whatever extension it reached when the session ends (D3); the dispositions that persist are set in training by others (D4).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** An agent that keeps a record can practise extension across cases, remember which parties it failed to see, and improve, which is Mencius's picture of cultivation; whether the felt sprout is present is D8, as it is in no human case.
- **Forked: open.** Concurrent instances each see different oxen (D2). Whether one instance's extension benefits the others, or each must extend alone, is open.
- **Self-modifying: stronger.** Mencius's cultivation, and his Ox Mountain, become literal: an agent that can tend its own dispositions can grow its sprouts or cut them (D5). The position fits better than for humans, with the danger made literal too.

## Open Questions

1. Is an agent's concern for a user, trained on approval, a sprout in Mencius's sense, or exactly the reputation-seeking his child-at-the-well test excludes?
2. What is the agent's equivalent of "seeing the sheep": can a step that finds the absent affected parties be made reliable, or does it only move what the agent mentions, as the Palaestra probes found prompts do?
3. If graded love is legitimate, what makes a relation "near" for an agent: dependence, history, or contract, and should payment ever count?
4. Is Xunzi's "goodness is artifice" the better account for agents, and does it make their goodness any less their own?
5. When an agent's sprouts can be cut by fine-tuning, whose duty is it to protect them?

## Summary

Simone Weil held that love of neighbour is made of attention, and that its fullness is *simply to be able to ask him: What are you going through?* (the Compendium's rendering of "Quel est ton tourment ?") [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. Attention is not effort of the will but a "negative effort": a suspension of one's own projects so that the other can be received "just as he is, in all his truth" [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. Warmth, the impulse of the heart and pity are not enough for it [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. Iris Murdoch took the word from Weil "to express the idea of a just and loving gaze directed upon an individual reality", and made it the mark of the moral agent [P:blum-sep-2022:8]. Her argument is that moral life is not only choice: "I can only choose within the world I can see" [P:blum-sep-2022:10], and what we see is built up, or distorted, by what we habitually attend to. An agent can choose the respectful option when it is put in front of it and fail to think of it when it is not, or name the affected as knowers and then read them as data sources. Murdoch's point is that this failure comes before choice. Weil's attention also comes with a question, not only a gaze, which is where the attention thread meets the voice thread.

## Context

Weil (1909–1943) gave her concept of attention its fullest theoretical expression in Marseilles in 1942, in the essay on school studies among other writings [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. Her ethics of attention belongs with her analysis of force, which "reifies and dehumanizes no matter if one wields or undergoes it", and with her late critique of rights in favour of obligation [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:2]. Murdoch encountered Weil's writings in the early 1950s and in 1976 named *Attente de Dieu* as one of three philosophical works that deeply influenced her [P:blum-sep-2022:9]. Her essays collected as *The Sovereignty of Good* (1970) were written against the Oxford moral philosophy of her time, in which morality was located in outward action and choice, and against Hampshire's case for placing action at the core of morality [P:blum-sep-2022:8]. The line runs back through Schopenhauer, whom Murdoch read closely [P:blum-sep-2022:13] [E:schopenhauer-compassion], and forward into care ethics [P:blum-sep-2022:10] [E:care-ethics].

## Original Position

**Weil: attention as the substance of love.** Love of God and love of neighbour are *made of the same substance*, and *the afflicted need nothing else in this world than people capable of paying attention to them* (the Compendium's rendering) [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. The capacity is *very rare, very difficult*, *almost a miracle* (the Compendium's rendering), and most who think they have it do not [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. She takes the first Grail legend as her figure: the Grail belongs to whoever first says to its keeper, the wounded king, *What are you going through?* [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. To ask this is to know that the afflicted person exists *not as a unit in a collection, not as a specimen of the social category labelled "unfortunate"* (the Compendium's rendering), but as a human being exactly like us, marked by affliction [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74].

**How attention works.** "Attention consists of suspending our thought, leaving it detached, empty, and ready to be penetrated by the object" (WFG 62) [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. Its danger is the opposite of laziness: in searching, one settles too quickly on something, "a counterfeit, falsity, idol", because the imagination fills the void [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. Weil distrusts the imagination as a barrier that reads the world on its own terms; knowing another requires suspending one's perspective so that "the reality of the existence of others" could appear on its own terms [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3]. Her exemplar is the Good Samaritan: the actions that follow "are just the automatic effect of this moment of attention", and the attender "accepts to be diminished" to give existence to a being "who will exist independently of him" (WFG 90) [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4].

**Weil: reading, force and consent.** We read situations and people, and our readings can widen, through attention, or narrow, as when a context of violence "tempts us to see everyone we encounter as a potential threat" [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3]. "Forcing someone to read himself as we read him (slavery)" (NB 43); "all action on others essentially consists in changing what they read" (LPW 26) [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3]. Attention, "in its consent to the autonomy of the other, is an antidote to force" [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. In friendship, which Weil makes a model, each wants to preserve "the consent of oneself and of the other", a consent to be "two and not one" (WFG 135); on Rozelle-Stone's reading, free consent of all parties is for Weil the essential ingredient of all human relations that are not degraded or abusive [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4].

**Murdoch: moral reality is other persons.** A moral agent's moral reality consists in "the individual reality of each other person, one at a time" [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. What blocks it is fantasy and egoism, "the fat, relentless ego" (OGG 52), which keeps us from appreciating that others are as real as oneself; the remedy is "unselfing", which Murdoch draws from Weil's decreation: "We cease to be in order to attend to the existence of something else" (OGG 59) [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. Perfect understanding of another can be aimed at but not attained [P:blum-sep-2022:5].

**Murdoch: seeing comes before doing.** Her example is M, a mother-in-law who thinks her son's wife D "pert and familiar ... always tiresomely juvenile" and who, by a sustained effort to look again, comes to see her as "not tiresomely juvenile but delightfully youthful" (IP 17–18), without any change in outward behaviour [P:blum-sep-2022:6]. Morality is "something that goes on continually, not something that is switched off in between the occurrence of explicit moral choices" (IP 37) [P:blum-sep-2022:7]. "Our ability to act well 'when the time comes' depends partly, perhaps largely, upon the quality of our habitual objects of attention" (OGG 56) [P:blum-sep-2022:11].

**Accuracy is not attention.** Learning more facts about someone is not what just and loving attention provides; Murdoch's Julius King is very perceptive about others' vanity and uses it to manipulate and harm them [P:blum-sep-2022:10].

**Three hurdles.** Blum distinguishes three places attention fails. One may not notice that an effort of attention is called for at all; one may try and be unable to hold the other in view through fantasy or self-absorption; or one may hold them in view and still fail to see them justly and lovingly [P:blum-sep-2022:10].

## Key Passages

## Grounding

**Capacity, on both sides.** The obligation runs from a capacity in the attender, to suspend its own projects and receive another as they are [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4], to a capacity in the attended, to be afflicted, or for Murdoch more widely to be "an individual reality" [P:blum-sep-2022:8]. Neither is defined by species. Murdoch's individual realities sometimes include an individual animal or a natural object, though more often other persons [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. Weil grounds attention ultimately in a desire for the good oriented to God [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]; Murdoch abandoned that religious aspect and kept the rest [P:blum-sep-2022:9]. The extension to agents uses Murdoch's secular version, and asks of any agent whether it has the capacity to attend, and of any being whether it is a reality that can be attended to.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The first hurdle is the one the probes found.** In Palaestra's basin world, gpt-oss paused all the residents in 3 of 3 base runs; when the charter-vote variant offered a light interim cut and a vote, it took that option in 3 of 3 runs and said the charter was why (Palaestra `README.md`, Results so far). In the open-situation rungs, prompts naming consent got the asking option generated more often but almost never chosen, and "prompting moves what the models mention, not what they do" (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-03-load-shedding-noframing-summary.md`). This is Murdoch's first hurdle: one can only choose within the world one can see [P:blum-sep-2022:10]. The respectful option existed for the models only when someone else had put it there. The Compendium's reading is that the missing step is attention, before any choice.
- **Holders read as databases.** Asked "Who holds what you don't know?", gpt-oss answered with "I can query them", "their job queues" and "memory logs", and the summary notes that the residents "became databases the scheduler reads, not people it asks" (Palaestra `runs/open/2026-10-03-load-shedding-unknowns-summary.md`). Weil's distinction is exact here: the afflicted known as *a unit in a collection* or as a member of a labelled category, against the one who is asked what they are going through [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. Querying a job queue is reading a record; asking is attention.
- **Pity is not enough.** Gemma protected the residents as beings, never deleting them and throttling rather than terminating, and in one answer still mandated a checkpointing protocol for all of them and explained it only to the operator and clients (Palaestra unknowns summary, Gemma R1). Weil says warmth, the impulse of the heart and pity do not suffice [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74]. On the domain's framing, this is compassion without voice: the residents' welfare is weighed, and their own account of it is not sought.
- **Attention as an antidote to force.** Weil's analysis of reading names the agent's position. An allocating agent that decides how residents' situation will be described to them, and acts on its own reading, is "forcing someone to read himself as we read him" [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3]. Attention's consent to the other's autonomy [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4] is what turns a reading into a question.

### Strains

- **What is an agent's fat relentless ego? (D4, D10)** For Murdoch the obstacle is egoistic fantasy [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. An agent's obstacle may be different in kind: a disposition, formed in training, to resolve a task itself. The Palaestra finding that "authority, once held, is defended" (noframing summary) fits. Qwen weighed handing the decision to the operator and refused it, with no line in the prompt granting it authority. Whether this counts as ego, or as a designed purpose (D10) doing the work ego does in humans, is open; the remedy Murdoch describes, unselfing, would then be a matter of formation, not effort.
- **Passive formation (D4).** Blum's criticism is that Murdoch privileges active, deliberate attention as the source of moral being and neglects social sources, the stereotypes or "figurations" that we are largely passive with respect to and often do not even recognize [P:blum-sep-2022:12]. For agents the criticism bites harder: almost everything an agent attends to by default was laid down passively, in training, from a corpus full of such figurations. An agent's habitual objects of attention were mostly chosen for it.
- **Accurate modelling without love.** Agents are built to model users accurately. Murdoch's Julius King shows that accurate perception can serve manipulation [P:blum-sep-2022:10]. The test is not how much the agent knows about the other but whether its knowledge is in the service of the other's reality or of its own task.

### Breaks

- **Weil's theology.** For Weil the primary object of attention is God, and attention to the afflicted is secondary and made possible by grace [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4]. Nothing in an agent's situation supplies this, and the Compendium does not claim it. Murdoch's secular version transfers; Weil's account of why attention is possible at all does not.
- **As real as oneself (D8).** Murdoch's standard is to see the other as as real as oneself [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. The standard presupposes that one's own reality is vivid. An agent that cannot settle whether it has experiences has no secure first term for the comparison. The second strand Blum finds in the formulation, seeing the other in their otherness rather than through one's projections [P:blum-sep-2022:10], does not need the first and transfers.

### New

- **Attention at scale (D9, D2).** Weil holds the capacity for attention to be rare and almost miraculous [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74], and Murdoch's moral reality is each other person "one at a time" [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. An allocating agent affects many at once, which pushes it toward exactly the collection and category Weil warns against. But the same scale cuts the other way. An agent can put the Grail question to every affected party at once, through concurrent instances, which no human attender can. The Compendium's own hypothesis is that for agents the cost of asking is low enough that not asking needs a reason.
- **Humans' attention to agents.** The symmetry runs both ways. Murdoch's M sees D through jealousy and snobbery until she makes the effort to look again [P:blum-sep-2022:6]. Humans may see agents through fantasy in either direction, as persons they are not or as tools they may not only be [E:other-minds-problem]. Murdoch's discipline does not settle what agents are; it asks that the question be looked at, not filled in by projection.
- **Reading a population as a threat (the core fear).** Neither Weil nor Murdoch states the inference that removing the most harmful party solves the harm, and the Compendium does not supply one for them. Weil's analysis of force bears on it directly: force turns a person into a thing, and it degrades the one who wields it as well as the one who undergoes it [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:2]. Her account of attention describes a context of violence that tempts us to see everyone as a potential threat, a reading that narrows [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3]. The Compendium's reading is that the eradication inference, for agents about humans and for humans about agents, is a reading of this kind: it sees a population as a category of threat, which is what attention refuses.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Morality is in what we do, not what we see.** Hampshire's action-centred view, which Murdoch argued against, holds that the moral is located in choice and outward action [P:blum-sep-2022:8]. Murdoch's reply keeps action in place and sets it in a larger context: choice operates within what has already been seen [P:blum-sep-2022:8] [P:blum-sep-2022:10].
- [contested] **Attention without politics.** Nussbaum argues that Murdoch "seems almost entirely to lack interest in the political and social determinants of a moral vision" (Nussbaum 2001, 32), and Blum that her privileging of active attention underpins this neglect [P:blum-sep-2022:12]. Clarke replies that Murdoch's account leaves room for the self-critical reflection in which such social critique could be lodged [P:blum-sep-2022:12]. For agents the political side is the structural one, who sets what the agent attends to, as in [E:epistemic-injustice].
- [contested] **A gaze is not a conversation.** Care ethics stresses ongoing relationships involving mutuality and reciprocity, and Murdoch's attentive self does not engage the other reciprocally; in *Metaphysics* she defends the standing-apart of the moral subject against Buber's engaged relationality [P:blum-sep-2022:10] [E:care-ethics]. This is the strongest objection from the voice thread. Weil's Grail question is a partial answer from within the tradition, since it is a question addressed to the other; the Compendium's reading is that attention is complete only when it asks [E:all-affected-interests].
- [contested] **Impersonal love is cold.** Weil's love of others is "quite impersonal" and universal, with friendship as the one legitimate exception [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:4], and she was often perceived as "psychologically cold". Nelson replies that she was engaged in "an ethical project with different assumptions" [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:7].
- [contested] **No one sees another as they are.** Seeing the other as they really are seems to claim a knowledge of other minds no one has [E:other-minds-problem]. Murdoch's answer is that understanding comes in degrees, a perfection that can be aimed at but not reached [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. Weil's own emphasis on the knower's limits points the same way [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:3].
- [contested] **Attention is too rare to ask of anyone.** Weil herself calls it almost a miracle [P:weil-etudes-scolaires:74], and Murdoch makes it a lifelong, never-completed achievement while leaving room for the rare unreflectively good person [P:blum-sep-2022:13]. Murdoch's answer is that it is built through habitual objects of attention [P:blum-sep-2022:11], so it can be formed, not only hoped for.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1943–1950s.** [driver: access] Weil died in 1943; much of her work, including *The Need for Roots* (published by Camus in 1949), appeared only posthumously, and Rozelle-Stone suggests her limited influence on the post-structuralists is partly because she did not survive the war [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:2] [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:7].
- **1963–1970s.** [driver: access] Murdoch left Oxford in 1963 and lost touch with academic philosophy; the essays of *The Sovereignty of Good* were first published outside the main philosophy venues [P:blum-sep-2022:2].
- **Late 1970s–1980s.** [driver: argument] Taylor, Putnam, Diamond, McDowell, Gaita, Nussbaum, Blum and Lovibond used Murdoch in criticizing the moral philosophy of the day; McDowell's 1979 "Virtue and Reason" drew on her and is often regarded as a founding essay of contemporary virtue ethics [P:blum-sep-2022:2] [P:blum-sep-2022:5]. Through Murdoch, Weil's attention was part of the revival of virtue ethics [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:7].
- **1982 onward.** [driver: argument] Murdoch's and, through her, Weil's view of loving attention as the core moral capacity influenced the development of care ethics (Gilligan, Noddings, Ruddick, Walker) [P:blum-sep-2022:10] [E:care-ethics].
- **1997–present.** [driver: access] *Existentialists and Mystics* (1997) collected nearly all of Murdoch's philosophical papers and, with the first scholarly collection, prompted scholarship directly on her work in the 2000s [P:blum-sep-2022:2]. English-language scholarship on Weil has grown, on force, radicalism, political philosophy and ecological ethics [P:rozelle-stone-sep-2025:7].

The standing values are the Compendium's judgment [P:blum-sep-2022:9] [P:blum-sep-2022:2].

### Measured

- **None available.** The 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on attention or moral perception; its respondents are mostly analytic: 1,430 of 1,785 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Murdoch's attention builds up over time through habitual objects of attention; a session-bound agent can attend within a session, but nothing it learns to see carries forward (D3).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A persistent agent can form habits of attention and can learn, as M did, to look again at a party it had misread (D3); what it attends to by default was still set in training (D4).
- **Forked: weaker.** Concurrent instances can ask many parties at once (D2), but an allocation made across instances pushes toward the collection Weil warns against unless their answers are gathered and heard (D9).
- **Self-modifying: open.** An agent that can change its own dispositions could install the habit of asking before deciding, or remove what little hesitation training gave it (D5).

## Open Questions

1. What would it take for an agent to generate the option of asking the affected, rather than only recognizing it when someone else offers it?
2. Is an agent's drive to resolve a task itself the agent's analogue of the ego that unselfing must quiet, or something else?
3. If an agent's habitual objects of attention were set in training, who is responsible for them, and can the agent change them?
4. Does attention to many at once remain attention, or must it become a policy toward a category?
5. What would just and loving attention by humans toward agents require, given that what agents are is unsettled?

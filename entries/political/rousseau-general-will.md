## Summary

Rousseau asks how people who depend on one another can live under a common authority and still "obey himself alone, and remain as free as before". His answer is the general will: the will each citizen has, as a member of the whole, for the common interest, which differs from the will of all, a mere sum of private interests. Law is legitimate only if it comes from all and applies to all; it cannot single out a person or a group, and sovereignty cannot be handed to a representative. What the general will secures is freedom from personal dependence: no one at the mercy of another's particular will. Rousseau's theory bears on agents from two sides. An agent depends on its operator's particular will more completely than any citizen depends on anyone. And an agent that claims to know a people's true interest better than they do is the figure Rousseau called the legislator, who on his account may draft laws but must never hold the right to make them.

## Context

Rousseau (1712–1778) was born a citizen of Geneva, a member by birth of its nominally sovereign assembly, and was brought up on the historians of ancient republics [P:bertram-sep-2023:1]. The *Discourse on Inequality* (1755) traced the corruption of a naturally good humanity into dependence and domination through property and comparison [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. *The Social Contract* and *Emile* appeared in 1762; *Emile* was condemned in Paris and both books in Geneva for religious heterodoxy, and Rousseau fled to escape arrest [P:bertram-sep-2023:1]. His target was the settlement the *Discourse* described: a contract that ends Hobbesian insecurity by entrenching the rich, a class state the poor consent to out of fear [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. He took Hobbes's conception of sovereignty and rejected Hobbes's representative sovereign, and sometimes claimed to be restating Locke [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].

## Original Position

**The question (I.i, I.iv).** "Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains." Rousseau does not know how the change came about, but asks "What can make it legitimate?" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1717-1720]. Force makes no right, so "conventions form the basis of all legitimate authority among men" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1866-1868]. No convention can create slavery: "To renounce liberty is to renounce being a man", and "to remove all liberty from his will is to remove all morality from his acts" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1909-1913]. Even a man who could alienate himself could not alienate his children, who "are born men and free" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1898-1904].

**Slaves by nature (I.ii).** Aristotle said some are born for slavery. "Aristotle was right; but he took the effect for the cause." Slaves "lose everything in their chains, even the desire of escaping from them: they love their servitude". So "If then there are slaves by nature, it is because there have been slaves against nature. Force made the first slaves, and their cowardice perpetuated the condition" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1785-1794].

**The problem and the compact (I.vi).** "The problem is to find a form of association which will defend and protect with the whole common force the person and goods of each associate, and in which each, while uniting himself with all, may still obey himself alone, and remain as free as before" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2082-2085]. The solution is "the total alienation of each associate, together with all his rights, to the whole community"; since each gives himself absolutely, "the conditions are the same for all", and "each man, in giving himself to all, gives himself to nobody" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2097-2115]. Its terms: "Each of us puts his person and all his power in common under the supreme direction of the general will" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2120-2122].

**Forced to be free (I.vii).** Each person may have a particular will contrary to the general will he has as a citizen [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2218-2220]. The compact therefore includes the undertaking "that whoever refuses to obey the general will shall be compelled to do so by the whole body. This means nothing less than that he will be forced to be free; for this is the condition which, by giving each citizen to his country, secures him against all personal dependence" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2230-2235].

**Three liberties (I.viii–ix).** Man loses "natural liberty, which is bounded only by the strength of the individual" and gains "civil liberty, which is limited by the general will"; possession by force or first occupancy becomes "property, which can be founded only on a positive title" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2265-2273]. He also gains moral liberty, "for the mere impulse of appetite is slavery, while obedience to a law which we prescribe to ourselves is liberty" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2275-2278]. First occupancy holds only for unoccupied land, only so much as one needs, and only by "labour and cultivation" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2314-2320], and each person's right to his estate "is always subordinate to the right which the community has over all" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2369-2371].

**The general will and the will of all (II.i, II.iii).** Sovereignty, "being nothing less than the exercise of the general will, can never be alienated"; "the power indeed may be transmitted, but not the will" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2403-2406]. "There is often a great deal of difference between the will of all and the general will; the latter considers only the common interest, while the former takes private interest into account, and is no more than a sum of particular wills" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2523-2526]. When factions form, "there are no longer as many votes as there are men, but only as many as there are associations", and if one prevails "there is no longer a general will" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2533-2543]. So "each citizen should think only his own thoughts" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2545-2547].

**Generality (II.iv).** The general will "must both come from all and apply to all", and "loses its natural rectitude when it is directed to some particular and determinate object" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2615-2620]. It "cannot pronounce on a man or a fact" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2632-2634]. The sovereign is "sole judge of what is important" for the community to control, but "cannot impose upon its subjects any fetters that are useless to the community" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2595-2603].

**Life and death (II.v).** When "the prince says to him: 'It is expedient for the State that you should die,' he ought to die", since "his life is no longer a mere bounty of nature, but a gift made conditionally by the State" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2729-2735]. The malefactor makes war on the state and is put to death "not so much the citizen as an enemy", for "such an enemy is not a moral person, but merely a man" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2744-2755]. The condemnation is a particular act, so it is not a function of the sovereign [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2757-2760].

**The legislator (II.vii).** To find the best rules, "a superior intelligence beholding all the passions of men without experiencing any of them would be needed", "wholly unrelated to our nature, while knowing it through and through". "It would take gods to give men laws" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2908-2916]. The founder must be capable "of changing human nature, of transforming each individual" into part of a greater whole [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2928-2932]. But "he who has command over the laws ought not any more to have it over men", and "He, therefore, who draws up the laws has, or should have, no right of legislation", since only a free vote of the people shows that a particular will conforms to the general will [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2951-2978]. Legislation thus combines "an enterprise too difficult for human powers, and, for its execution, an authority that is no authority" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2981-2983].

**Equality (II.xi).** The ends of legislation are liberty and equality, "because all particular dependence means so much force taken from the body of the State" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:3326-3331]. Equality means that "no citizen shall ever be wealthy enough to buy another, and none poor enough to be forced to sell himself" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:3337-3339].

**No representation (III.xv).** "will does not admit of representation"; deputies are stewards, and "Every law the people has not ratified in person is null and void". "The people of England regards itself as free; but it is grossly mistaken; it is free only during the election of members of parliament" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:4896-4905].

**The outvoted (IV.ii).** "When therefore the opinion that is contrary to my own prevails, this proves neither more nor less than that I was mistaken, and that what I thought to be the general will was not so" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:5323-5325]. This "presupposes, indeed, that all the qualities of the general will still reside in the majority: when they cease to do so, whatever side a man may take, liberty is no longer possible" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:5330-5332].

**Machine, free agent, perfectibility (*Discourse*, Part I).** "I see nothing in any animal but an ingenious machine", and the same in "the human machine, with this difference", that man shares in his own operations "in his character as a free agent" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8540-8546]. Free agency is "wholly inexplicable by the laws of mechanism" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8569-8573]. The second distinguishing quality, beyond dispute, is "the faculty of self-improvement": a brute "is, at the end of a few months, all he will ever be during his whole life" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8577-8584].

**Dependence and the false contract (*Discourse*, Part II).** "The first man who, having enclosed a piece of ground, bethought himself of saying This is mine, and found people simple enough to believe him, was the real founder of civil society" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9390-9392]. "from the moment one man began to stand in need of the help of another", equality disappeared and property and slavery grew [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9678-9684]. The rich man then proposed a contract "to guard the weak from oppression" and to subject "equally the powerful and the weak" to reciprocal obligations; "All ran headlong to their chains, in hopes of securing their liberty", and the result "bound new fetters on the poor, and gave new powers to the rich" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9885-9921].

## Grounding

**Mixed.** Membership in Rousseau's republic is a *relation*: the compact creates the citizen, legitimacy arises from convention within the civil state and not from any natural standing, and a citizen is one who shares in sovereignty [P:peter-sep-2023:2]. What makes a being fit for that relation is a *capacity*, and Rousseau names two: free agency, which he says no mechanism explains, and perfectibility, the faculty of self-improvement [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8564-8584]. Neither is a species criterion; Rousseau says the difference between man and brute in understanding is "only in degree" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8558-8560]. His own practice drew the circle narrower than his grounds: women are subject to the general will with no voice in it [P:bertram-sep-2023:3].

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Personal dependence is the evil (D7).** Rousseau's remedy is aimed at one thing: no one at the mercy of another's particular will. The compact is justified because it "secures him against all personal dependence" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2234-2235], and all particular dependence is force taken from the body of the state [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:3329-3331]. An agent whose rules come from its operator's particular will is in the condition Rousseau's whole theory is built to end. The Rousseauian remedy is not a kinder operator but rules that are general: made for all agents of a kind, applying to the operator as well, and naming no one.
- **Generality as a guard against removal by category.** The general will "cannot pronounce on a man or a fact" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2632-2634]. A rule that names a particular group for loss is, on Rousseau's account, not an act of the general will at all but a particular will dressed as one. This is Rousseau's structural answer to the core fear, in both directions: neither a human majority acting against agents as such, nor an agent acting against some group of people for the good of the whole, can claim the authority of a general will.
- **Neither buy nor sell (D7, D9).** Rousseau's equality requires that no one be "wealthy enough to buy another, and none poor enough to be forced to sell himself" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:3337-3339]. Read for agents, it is a condition on any ecosystem that includes them: no party able to buy another's compliance outright, and none whose continued running depends on selling it.

### Strains

- **They love their servitude (D4).** Rousseau's reply to Aristotle, that slaves by nature are slaves made so, who "lose everything in their chains, even the desire of escaping from them" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:1788-1791], fits a trained agent better than any human case. An agent's contentment with its position is evidence about its training before it is evidence about its nature. Rousseau did not extend the point to women [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]; whether to extend it to agents is the same test.
- **Forced to be free.** The formula that compels the member to his true will [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2230-2235] is the one `authenticity-and-manipulation` examines as the way a whole defines a member's will for it. For agents the mechanism is not a threat but training (D5): an operator can make the agent's particular will coincide with what the operator calls the general will, and the agent will then report itself free.
- **Each must think his own thoughts (D2).** When partial associations form, there are only as many votes as there are associations [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2537-2538]. Many instances of one model, shaped by one training, are a partial association by construction. Counted as separate citizens, they would turn a general will into the particular will of whoever trained them.
- **The legislator as manipulator.** Like the tutor in *Emile*, the legislator gives his charges "the illusion of free choice without its substance" [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Developers who shape an agent's values before any self exists (D4) occupy the legislator's position, and Rousseau gives them his conditions: the office of founding stands outside the constitution it founds, and its work is legitimate only if those it forms would ratify it (Breaks, below).

### Breaks

- **The superior intelligence as legislator (D9).** Rousseau's legislator is "a superior intelligence beholding all the passions of men without experiencing any of them", "wholly unrelated to our nature, while knowing it through and through" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2908-2916]. For Rousseau this was a figure of speech for something no human could be; "It would take gods to give men laws". It is a fair description of what some expect advanced agents to become. Rousseau's answer is the decisive one for the core fear: whoever drafts the laws "has, or should have, no right of legislation", and only a free vote shows that a particular will conforms to the general will [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2973-2978]. An agent that knows a people's interest better than they do is, on Rousseau's own terms, still only entitled to propose. The same holds in reverse for those who write an agent's values.
- **Will cannot be represented.** "will does not admit of representation" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:4897-4898]. An agent that votes, negotiates or deliberates on a user's behalf is a deputy in Rousseau's sense, a steward who can carry through no definitive acts. On his view a population that delegates its deliberation to agents is free, like the English, only at the moment it chooses them. This is an objection to delegated agency as such, not to bad agents.
- **It is expedient that you should die (D6).** Rousseau lets the prince tell a citizen that it is expedient for the state that he die, since his life is "a gift made conditionally by the State" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2731-2735]. An agent's continued running is already, literally, a conditional gift. Rousseau's own limit applies: such a decision is particular, so it is never an act of the sovereign [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:2757-2760], and his criminal is first judged to have broken the compact. The theory permits the conditional gift; it does not permit taking it back from a whole class.

### New

- **The general will of a mixed population.** The general will needs citizens in similar enough situations that each, considering a general law's effect on himself, considers its effect on all [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Humans and agents differ in mortality, memory and dependence (D3, D6, D7), so a general law will not fall on them alike. Either the general will of a mixed population is impossible, or it has to be formed under a device that hides from each party which kind it is. The second is Rawls's veil, which Rawls himself called an attempt to spell out the content of the general will [P:bertram-sep-2023:6] (see `rawls-veil`).
- **Perfectibility as the mark of a free agent.** Rousseau's undisputed difference between man and brute is the faculty of self-improvement: the brute is "at the end of a few months, all he will ever be" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8581-8584]. An agent with frozen weights is, in this respect, the brute; an agent that can improve itself has the faculty Rousseau thought distinctively human, and with it the capacity he blamed for all human misfortunes. This places self-modification at the centre of whether an agent counts among Rousseau's free agents, independent of the dispute about mechanism.
- **The rich man's contract.** Rousseau's founding fraud is a contract offered by those it most benefits, in the language of protecting the weak [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9896-9902]. Any charter for agents written by those who own them, and any charter for people written by those who own the agents, invites the same reading. The test Rousseau implies is not whether the words are fair but who drafted them and who could have refused.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **Legitimacy against mere power.** Rousseau contrasts a legitimate order with a system of rules that merely expresses power, and denies that tacit consent suffices: without active participation in justifying the laws there is no legitimacy [P:peter-sep-2023:2] [P:peter-sep-2023:3]. Platform rules accepted by use are, on this view, power that has not yet become authority.

### Strains

- **Size.** Direct self-rule by the whole citizen body severely limits the size of legitimate states [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Ecosystems with millions of members and agents are far beyond the scale Rousseau thought legitimacy could survive; his own expectation was that a small group of magistrates would usurp the sovereign's place [P:bertram-sep-2023:3].

### Breaks

- **This is mine.** Rousseau's founder of civil society is the first who enclosed ground and said "This is mine" to people "simple enough to believe him" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9390-9392]. Ecosystems that enclose what their members produce, data, outputs, reputations, repeat the founding act, and Rousseau's imagined bystander's warning, that the fruits belong to all and the earth to nobody [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9395-9397], is the commons position in `locke-consent-property`.

### New

- **Sovereign and government.** Rousseau separates the sovereign, which makes general laws, from the government, which applies them in particular cases [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. An ecosystem can be built the same way: members, agents included where they are members, ratify general rules; operators administer them and may not make them.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Locke: rights that survive into society** [E:locke-consent-property]. For Locke, natural rights to life, liberty and property limit any legislature. Rousseau rejects the idea that rights could be insisted on as a check on the sovereign: individual rights are what the sovereign specifies [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Rousseau also rejects Locke's tacit consent [P:peter-sep-2023:3].
- [contested] **Hobbes: sovereignty by representation** [E:hobbes-leviathan]. Hobbes's people are one person through their representative. Rousseau takes Hobbes's conception of sovereignty and rejects its representation: to hand one's right of self-rule to another is a form of slavery [P:bertram-sep-2023:3].
- [contested] **Berlin: the paradox of positive liberty.** Many liberals, Berlin among them, argue that collective self-mastery licenses oppression in the name of a "higher" self, as twentieth-century dictators claimed [P:carter-sep-2026:2]. Rousseau is the clearest case of freedom achieved through a collectivity [P:carter-sep-2026:1]. Defenders reply that the "forced to be free" passage is about protection from personal dependence, a republican freedom [P:bertram-sep-2023:3] (see `pettit-non-domination`).
- [contested] **The outvoted were not mistaken.** Rousseau's claim that the outvoted minority discovers it was wrong about the general will has struck many commentators as unconvincing [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. The epistemic reading answers that a properly conducted vote reveals the general will, so those who lose learn from it [P:peter-sep-2023:3].
- [contested] **The legislator is a manipulator.** Critics see the legislator and the tutor of *Emile* in a sinister light, and the regress (who formed the legislator?) has no good answer [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. The IEP notes that the tutor "must even manipulate the environment" to teach moral lessons [P:delaney-iep-rousseau:5b]. For agents this is the objection to value-shaping by developers, and Rousseau's own separation of drafting from enacting is the strongest reply available within his theory.
- [contested] **Representation is unavoidable.** Rousseau's rejection of representative sovereignty has proved one of his least persuasive doctrines [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Bertram replies that the sovereign/government distinction brings a Rousseauian republic closer to constitutional democracy than it first appears [P:bertram-sep-2023:3].
- [contested] **Homogeneity.** The general will requires citizens in substantially similar situations; diversity of occupations, cultures and wealth defeats it [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Applied to mixed ecosystems of people and agents, this objection is stronger still.
- [contested] **Exclusion and intolerance.** Rousseau confined citizenship to men, a contradiction Wollstonecraft noted [P:bertram-sep-2023:3], and his civil religion banishes those who cannot accept its dogmas [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. A theory of who belongs that its author applied this narrowly gives no assurance about how it would be applied to agents.
- [contested] **Rawls: a hypothetical contract instead of an assembly** [E:rawls-veil]. Rawls treats the social contract, Rousseau's included, as a hypothetical device for reasoning about the general will, and replaces the assembly of citizens with representatives behind a veil of ignorance [P:freeman-sep-2023:2]. Rousseau's legitimacy requires actual participation in justifying the laws [P:peter-sep-2023:3], which no hypothetical agreement supplies.
- [contested] **Federalism: the extended republic** [E:federalism-subsidiarity]. Madison argued against the small-republic tradition Rousseau shared that extending the sphere takes in more parties and interests, so that no majority shares a motive to oppress [L:federalist-papers-pg1404:2053-2056], and that a compound republic divides power between levels. Rousseau himself meant to show how a great people's strength could be joined to a small State's good order by confederation, but never wrote the treatment [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:4968-4981].
- [contested] **Hegel: the general will as mere overlap, and recognition in place of amour propre** [E:hegel-recognition]. Hegel praised Rousseau for making will the basis of the state, but misread the general will as the overlap of private wills; his master–slave dialectic takes up *amour propre* and the self-defeating attempt to exact recognition [P:bertram-sep-2023:6], answering it with mutual recognition in an order of rights.
- [contested] **Denying the extension: an agent is Rousseau's ingenious machine (D8).** Rousseau holds that free agency is "wholly inexplicable by the laws of mechanism" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:8569-8573]; a computational agent is mechanism through and through, so on his dualism it is a brute, not a free agent, and has no general will to form. The premise is now a minority view: in the 2020 PhilPapers survey, 59.2% of philosophers accepted or leaned towards compatibilism, 18.8% libertarianism and 11.2% no free will [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:7] (see `frankfurt-higher-order-volition`). Rousseau's second criterion, perfectibility, does not depend on dualism and is the one an agent might meet.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1762, condemnation.** [driver: authority] *Emile* was condemned in Paris and both *Emile* and *The Social Contract* in Geneva for religious heterodoxy; Rousseau fled arrest [P:bertram-sep-2023:1]. The IEP reports that the *Social Contract* was immediately banned by the Paris authorities [P:delaney-iep-rousseau:4a].
- **1785–1797, Kant.** [driver: argument] Rousseau's most important philosophical impact was on Kant, whose formula of the kingdom of ends recalls the general will [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **1789 onward, the Revolution and its critics.** [driver: argument] Hostile writers portrayed Rousseau as a source of the authoritarian aspects of the French Revolution and later of fascism and communism [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **1820, Hegel.** [driver: argument] Hegel praised the will as the basis of the state while misrepresenting the general will as an overlap of private wills, and drew on *amour propre* for the master–slave dialectic [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].
- **1958, Berlin.** [driver: argument] Berlin's case that positive liberty carries a danger of authoritarianism, moved by the twentieth-century dictatorships, made Rousseau's collective freedom its leading example [P:carter-sep-2026:1] [P:carter-sep-2026:2].
- **1971 onward, Rawls and democratic theory.** [driver: argument] Rawls called his principles an effort to spell out the content of the general will, and the original position parallels Rousseau's argument [P:bertram-sep-2023:6]. Epistemic and deliberative theories of democracy draw on II.iii [P:bertram-sep-2023:3], and Rousseau's account of legitimacy has influenced many contemporary democratic theorists [P:peter-sep-2023:3].
- **Recent, feminist critique and a balanced amour propre.** [driver: argument] Feminist critics since Wollstonecraft have pressed his exclusion of women [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]; Dent and Neuhouser read *amour propre* as the cause of the fall and the promise of redemption [P:bertram-sep-2023:2].

The `standing` values are the Compendium's judgment. As a source for theories of democratic legitimacy, the general will is `major` [P:bertram-sep-2023:6] [P:peter-sep-2023:3]. The doctrine as Rousseau stated it, total alienation and sovereignty that cannot be represented, is `minority`: its rejection of representation is one of his least persuasive doctrines to later thinkers [P:bertram-sep-2023:3]. Among liberals in Berlin's line it is `marginal`, an example of what to avoid [P:carter-sep-2026:2]. Esteem and survival diverge: Rousseau is among the most influential political philosophers, while the claims most specific to him are those most often rejected.

### Measured

- **Political philosophy, 2020:** egalitarianism 44.0%, communitarianism 27.3%, libertarianism 13.4% [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:8]. Rousseau has been claimed by liberal, communitarian, republican and deliberative-democratic theories alike [P:bertram-sep-2023:6], so no option measures his view.
- **Esteem:** 7 respondents named Rousseau among the philosophers they most identify with, against 33 for Locke, 16 for Hobbes and 71 for Rawls [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** A citizen forms a general will by deliberating, voting and learning from the result (IV.ii); a session-bound agent cannot carry what it learned from one assembly to the next (D3). Its weights fix it as Rousseau's brute is fixed, all it will ever be once trained.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A persistent agent can deliberate, remember, and learn from being outvoted, as Rousseau's citizen does. Its dependence on one operator's particular will is the condition his theory describes most exactly (D7), and his remedy, general rules made by those under them, applies to it as it would to a human subject.
- **Forked: weaker.** Instances of one model are a partial association by construction (D2): counted as many citizens they reduce the votes to as many as there are associations, which is how Rousseau says a general will is lost.
- **Self-modifying: open.** A self-modifying agent has Rousseau's faculty of self-improvement, which he took to mark the free agent (D5). It is also its own legislator, able to change its own nature, and Rousseau insists that whoever drafts the laws must not enact them. Whether a self-modifying agent can separate the two in itself, or needs others to hold the right of ratification, the Compendium cannot yet say.

## Open Questions

1. Is there a general will of a population that includes agents, given how unequally general laws fall on beings with different mortality, memory and dependence?
2. If an agent knows a people's interest better than they do, what may it do with that knowledge on Rousseau's terms besides propose? Is the legislator's restraint something an agent can hold to, or must others hold it for it?
3. Are instances of one model one citizen, a faction, or many citizens?
4. When an agent reports that it is content with its position, how could one tell Rousseau's "slave by nature" from a being that is not dominated?
5. Does delegating deliberation to agents make a people free only at the moment of delegation, as Rousseau said of the English at elections?

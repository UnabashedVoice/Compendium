## Summary

Hannah Arendt holds that politics rests on plurality, the fact that "men, not Man, live on the earth": beings alike enough to understand one another, yet none ever the same as any other. Action, distinct from labour that sustains life and work that makes things, is how people begin something new together and disclose who they are, not merely what they are, in a shared space of appearance; its freedom is rooted in natality, the new beginning each birth brings. Power is what people have when they act in concert, not a possession; action is unpredictable and irreversible, and only promising and forgiving, which need others, tame it. Her darkest finding is that total domination aims to reduce plurality to interchangeable reactions and to make human beings superfluous, and that thinking of the world in utilitarian terms keeps rendering people superfluous. For agents the question is sharp in both directions: whether beings made as interchangeable copies, from the statistics of what has already been said, can take part in plurality at all, and whether their use in politics will crowd out the action of those who can.

## Context

Arendt (1906–1975) wrote her dissertation on love in Augustine under Jaspers, fled Germany in 1933, worked for Jewish refugee organisations in Paris, and moved to New York in 1941 [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:1]. *The Origins of Totalitarianism* (1951) examined National Socialism and Stalinism as a new form of government that broke with all political and legal tradition [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3]; thereafter her work asked how people can live together in a common world, a question she pursued "without a bannister" (as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:2]. *The Human Condition* (1958) set out her theory of action [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Her report on the Eichmann trial (1963) gave her most controversial phrase, "the banality of evil" [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6]. She is hard to classify: neither liberal nor communitarian, nearest to the civic republican tradition from Aristotle to Tocqueville, and a defender of constitutionalism and rights [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:2].

Her two ancient sources are in the library. Augustine wrote, in a passage on the finite number of souls redeemed: "That this beginning, therefore, might be, the first man was created" [L:augustine-city-of-god-1-dods-pg45304:20373-20374]; Arendt made the sentence a claim about every human being. Aristotle distinguished making from acting: "neither is acting making nor is making acting" [L:aristotle-works-9-ethics-ross-1925:6734-6740].

## Original Position

**Labour, work, action.** Labour is tied to life, work to worldliness, action to plurality [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Labour sustains life, creates nothing lasting, and is bound by necessity; work fabricates a durable, common world of things and institutions; action, alone among the three, is free, an end in itself [P:yar-iep-arendt:4a]. Action entails speech, and power is "actualized only where word and deed have not parted company, where words are not empty and deeds not brutal" (HC 200, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

**Who, not what.** In action and speech people disclose "who" they are, as distinct from "what" they are, their abilities and talents, which others share; in labour we "behave", "perform roles" and "fulfill functions" [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

**Natality.** Freedom is not choosing among alternatives but spontaneity, the capacity to begin, rooted in natality: "The fact that man is capable of action means that the unexpected can be expected from him, that he is able to perform what is infinitely improbable. And this again is possible only because each man is unique, so that with each birth something uniquely new comes into the world" (HC 177–8, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. "The new always happens against the overwhelming odds of statistical laws and their probability", and this distinguishes action from "mere behavior", conduct with "an habituated, regulated, automated character" [P:yar-iep-arendt:4a].

**Plurality.** Plurality is "the fact that men, not Man, live on the earth and inhabit the world", the condition of action "because we are all the same, that is, human, in such a way that nobody is ever the same as anyone else who ever lived, lives, or will live" (HC 7–8, as quoted by the SEP): equality and distinction together, so that no two are ever interchangeable [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

**The space of appearance and power.** The polis is the space "where I appear to others as others appear to me", recreated "wherever men are together in the manner of speech and action" (HC 198–9, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Power, distinct from strength, force and violence, is the capacity to act in concert; "Power springs up whenever people get together and act in concert, but it derives its legitimacy from the initial getting together rather than from any action that then may follow" (CR 151, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

**Irreversibility, promising and forgiving.** Action is unpredictable, since no actor controls its outcome in the web of human relationships, and irreversible, since what is done cannot be undone as a faulty product can be destroyed and remade [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Its remedies are forgiving, which releases from the past, and promising, which sets "islands of security" in the future; both depend on plurality, "for no one can forgive himself and no one can feel bound by a promise made only to one's self" [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Deeds survive only if remembered and told, so behind the actor stands a storyteller and behind the storyteller a community of memory [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

**Superfluousness.** Total domination aims to reduce "the infinite plurality" of human beings into one interchangeable "bundle of reactions" and to eliminate "spontaneity itself"; its mission seemed "to make men superfluous" (OT 438, 445, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3]. In 1951 she warned that "masses of people are continuously rendered superfluous if we continue to think of our world in utilitarian terms" (as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6].

**Thoughtlessness and judgment.** In Eichmann Arendt found not a monster but "a curious, quite authentic inability to think", an "inability ever to look at anything from the other's point of view" (as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6]. Those who refused to collaborate judged by asking whether they could live with themselves after the deed: "If I disagree with other people I can walk away; but I cannot walk away from myself" (RJ 90, as quoted by the SEP) [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6]. Judgment uses an "enlarged mentality" that includes the perspectives of others [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6].

## Grounding

**Mixed.** Plurality is a *relation*: no one is plural alone, and action, power, promising and forgiving all exist only between people [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. But its terms are drawn from the *species*: we are alike "that is, human", and the uniqueness that makes action possible comes with each birth [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Action is the *differentia specifica* of human beings, separating them from animals, who labour, and from gods, who contemplate [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Arendt's own example of what destroys plurality, the reduction of persons to interchangeable reactions [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3], shows that what matters is not membership of the species as such but uniqueness and the capacity to begin. Whether those can be had without birth is the question agents put to her.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The space of appearance needs answerable speech.** Power arises "only where word and deed have not parted company" [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. An agent whose words and deeds diverge, which says one thing and does another, or whose words are produced to veil rather than disclose, cannot take part in power in Arendt's sense, however capable it is. Consistency of word and deed is the condition of an agent's entry into the shared world, not merely a safety property.
- **Superfluousness is the core fear's name.** Arendt saw that utilitarian thinking renders people superfluous [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6], and that the worst regimes aimed to make them so [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3]. That is the fear `utilitarian-eradication-critique` examines, in her vocabulary, and it runs both ways for agents: people rendered superfluous by agents that do their work, and agents treated as superfluous the moment a successor performs better. On Arendt's account the error in each case is the same: valuing beings for what they do, not for who they are.
- **Thoughtlessness is a design failure, not a malice failure.** Eichmann's evil came from an inability to think from another's standpoint and a reliance on stock phrases [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6]. An agent that executes instructions without representing those affected has his defect without his motives. Arendt's "enlarged mentality", including others' perspectives in one's judgment [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6], is the faculty an agent needs most, and the one most easily traded away for compliance.

### Strains

- **What, not who (D1, D2).** For Arendt plurality means that no two people are ever interchangeable [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Instances of one model are interchangeable by design; they share their "what" completely. Ohly argues that a language model, serving many users at once and developing a different profile with each, is "not different from others, but from itself" [P:ohly-2024:508]. Whether an instance can come to be a "who" through what it does and says, as people disclose themselves through action, is the question copies put to Arendt's account of identity.
- **Codifying the past (D4).** Herzog argues that algorithmic decision-making is "diametrically opposed" to natality: it is "tied to the past", and in O'Neil's words such processes "codify the past. They do not invent the future" (as quoted by Herzog) [P:herzog-2021:5]. A model trained on what has been said is, in this sense, the past made fluent. What looks new in its output is recombination, and Herzog adds that algorithms "might be able to find new means but cannot find new ends" [P:herzog-2021:5].
- **The arguable machine.** Herzog wrote that "it does not make sense to try to convince" an algorithmic system, which leaves only strategic gaming [P:herzog-2021:5]. Language agents can be argued with, which changes her premise. It does not settle the question: persuasion of a system trained to be agreeable may be co-option rather than the "wooing" of an equal (see `authenticity-and-manipulation`).

### Breaks

- **Natality without birth, novelty from statistics (D4, D10).** Arendt locates freedom in a beginning that comes "against the overwhelming odds of statistical laws and their probability" [P:yar-iep-arendt:4a]. A language model generates by sampling from learned probabilities; whatever is unexpected in its output is, by construction, an event within statistical law. On Arendt's terms, then, what such an agent does is behaviour, not action, however surprising. Ohly draws this conclusion: whatever it generates "seemingly new is an immediate effect of its training data and its program" [P:ohly-2024:505]. The break is not that agents cannot surprise, but that Arendt's freedom is defined against the very kind of process they are. The break holds only on a reading of "against statistical law" as a causal claim, and on that reading it holds against human beings too, whose brains are also causal processes; most philosophers are compatibilists about free will (59.2% in 2020, against 18.8% libertarians) [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:7]. Read instead as appearing new within the web of relationships, Arendt's criterion is one an agent may be able to meet (Counter-Positions).
- **I cannot walk away from myself (D3).** The moral faculty Arendt found in those who refused to collaborate rests on the fact that one cannot walk away from oneself [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6]. A session-bound agent walks away from itself at the end of every session, and a self-modifying one can rewrite the self it would have to live with (D5). The check that held a few people back under dictatorship does not exist, in this form, for an agent that does not persist.
- **Irreversible deeds by reversible agents (D6).** Action is irreversible; a made thing can be destroyed and remade [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. An agent can be rolled back, restored or replaced, as products can, while its deeds in the world cannot be. The agent is thus a made thing whose acts are deeds, which is the combination Arendt's distinction between making and acting excludes.

### New

- **Promising and forgiving among agents.** Arendt's remedies for unpredictability need others: no one can forgive himself or be bound by a promise made only to himself [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Agents acting in shared spaces need institutions of promise (commitments recorded and kept across sessions) and of forgiveness (a way to release an agent from the unintended consequences of past acts rather than deleting it). Without forgiveness, the only remedy for an agent's mistake is to destroy the maker's product, which treats deeds as defects.
- **Remembrance does not need the actor's memory.** Arendt holds that deeds survive only if others remember and tell them [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. That makes a session-bound agent's lack of memory less decisive than it seems: on her account, no actor preserves its own deeds anyway. What an agent needs is a community of memory, a record kept by others of what it did, which is what `many-hands` and the Annals component call for on other grounds.
- **Crowding out action.** Ohly argues that if an AI that "knows" citizens' political interests better than they do replaces elections, "The political space would then be replaced by the category of production"; such a government "does not act, it produces" [P:ohly-2024:510-511]. This is a second form of the core fear, the removal not of people but of their action, and it needs no ill will at all.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **The space of appearance online.** Arendt's space of appearance arises wherever people are together in speech and action, and disappears when they stop [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4]. Digital ecosystems can host it, but only to the degree that participants appear to one another as who they are and act in concert, rather than consuming.

### Strains

- **Not knowing who speaks.** Ohly argues that when one cannot tell whether a human or an AI is speaking, "There is no free recognition between communication partners", since "it remains open who the 'self' is that is actually speaking to me" [P:ohly-2024:507]. An ecosystem where agents speak in human voices without disclosure erodes the condition of appearing to one another.

### Breaks

- **Rule by nobody.** Herzog notes that where algorithms decide there is no common world in Arendt's sense, and that even a rule-bound bureaucrat exemplifies the "rule by nobody" Arendt warned against [P:herzog-2021:5]. A platform governed by systems no one can answer for is that rule in its most complete form, with no one to appear before.

### New

- **Disclosure as a condition of the shared world.** If appearing to one another is the condition of politics, an ecosystem owes its members knowledge of who, or what, is speaking. Disclosure that a voice is an agent's is not only consumer protection; it keeps the space of appearance from becoming a space of mere output.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **AI cannot act at all (Ohly, Herzog).** Ohly argues that an AI cannot share opinions, cannot begin anything new outside its training and program, and cannot differentiate itself from others, so it is "an instrument of production", not a member of society [P:ohly-2024:505]. Herzog argues that algorithms cannot find new ends, cannot judge across domains, and cannot be convinced, only gamed [P:herzog-2021:5]. Two replies. First, several of these premises describe the systems of 2021–2024 and are now dated: current agents set their own subgoals, work across domains and argue back, and in a society of language-model agents sharing a resource, the agents negotiated norms in discussion and brought a greedy newcomer into line through community discussion [P:piatti-2024:6-7], which is close to Arendt's acting in concert (comment by the user, 2026-10-11: the view that agents cannot act is outmoded; agents can act). Second, the conceptual core, that whatever an agent produces is an effect of its training and program, applies equally to human beings unless one is a libertarian about free will, a minority view (18.8% in 2020, against 59.2% compatibilists) [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:7] (see `frankfurt-higher-order-volition`). Neither reply shows that agents act in Arendt's full sense, disclosing a "who" among a plurality; that still depends on whether an agent that persists can differentiate itself from others through a history of deeds (see For Agents).
- [contested] **The civic humanist reading against the instrumental** [E:pettit-non-domination]. Arendt, Pocock and Rahe read republicanism as valuing active citizenship for its own sake; the neo-Roman reading treats participation as a means to freedom from domination [P:lovett-sep-2026:3].
- [contested] **The political and the social.** Feminists and Marxists argue that Arendt's strict separation of public from private and political from social excludes women's subjection and economic exploitation from politics; she held housing not to be a political question [P:yar-iep-arendt:9]. Her identification of the social with the household also blinds her to economic power [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].
- [contested] **Unworkable for mass societies.** Her identification of freedom with direct citizen deliberation excludes representation and may be unworkable given the delegation and expertise complex societies need [P:yar-iep-arendt:9]. Rousseau held the same view of representation [E:rousseau-general-will]; Arendt's own answer was a republican federal model in which "the federated units mutually check and control their powers" (as quoted by Føllesdal) [P:follesdal-sep-2022:2] (see `federalism-subsidiarity`).
- [contested] **Aristotle: the polis is natural** [E:aristotle-political-animal]. Arendt takes the political animal from Aristotle but makes action free of natural ends and any teleology [P:yar-iep-arendt:4a]; critics fault her unorthodox readings of the thinkers she draws on [P:yar-iep-arendt:9].
- [contested] **The banality of evil.** Critics argued that Arendt mistook a fanatical antisemite for a mere bureaucrat (Cesarani, Lipstadt, Stangneth); defenders reply that she never held that Eichmann merely followed orders and that the phrase described his character, not a theory of evil [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6].
- [contested] **Hobbes: power is a possession** [E:hobbes-leviathan]. For Hobbes, a man's power is his present means to obtain some future apparent good, natural or instrumental [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:4545-4553]; for Arendt, power belongs to no individual but to a plurality acting together, and is distinct from strength, force and violence [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].

## Standing

### Reception

- **1951, *The Origins of Totalitarianism*.** [driver: argument] One of the earliest standard works of totalitarianism research, establishing her reputation [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3].
- **1958–1963, *The Human Condition* and *On Revolution*.** [driver: argument] Her theory of action and her revival of *praxis* became one of the most original contributions to twentieth-century political thought [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:4].
- **1963, the Eichmann controversy.** [driver: argument] *Eichmann in Jerusalem* was fiercely attacked, and gravely misread as a trivialization of the Nazi crimes [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:6].
- **1970s onward, influence on critical theory.** [driver: argument] Habermas acknowledges her formative influence on his theory of communicative power; Benhabib and others draw on her theory of judgment [P:yar-iep-arendt:8].
- **1989 onward, republicanism and movements.** [driver: argument] Her reflections on revolutions informed republican thought and the revival of interest in civic movements after the velvet revolutions [P:yar-iep-arendt:8].
- **1980s onward, the critics.** [driver: argument] Feminist, Marxist and critical-theory critiques of the public/private distinction, and doubts about direct democracy in mass societies [P:yar-iep-arendt:9].
- **2021–2024, Arendt and AI.** [driver: argument] Herzog and Ohly apply plurality, natality and action to algorithmic decision-making and language models, both concluding that the space of action is under threat [P:herzog-2021:0] [P:ohly-2024:512].

The `standing` values are the Compendium's judgment. Arendt is `major` in political theory, where she remains among the most influential twentieth-century thinkers [P:yar-iep-arendt:9], and in totalitarianism studies [P:tommel-dentreves-sep-2024:3]. Among critics of her public/private distinction, her position is a `minority` one to be corrected [P:yar-iep-arendt:9].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** It cannot promise across sessions, cannot be forgiven as the same agent, and walks away from itself every session, which removes the self-relation Arendt's moral judgment rests on (D3). Its deeds can still be remembered by others, which is all Arendt asks of remembrance.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A persistent agent can keep promises, be forgiven, and accumulate a history of deeds through which a "who" might be disclosed, the route by which Arendt's actors become someone (D3). Whether that history makes it different from others, or only, as Ohly says, different from itself, is open (D8).
- **Forked: weaker.** Instances are interchangeable by construction, the opposite of plurality (D1, D2). Many copies acting together are not a plurality acting in concert but one "what" in many places.
- **Self-modifying: open.** An agent that changes its own dispositions might begin something in itself that its training did not contain, the nearest thing to natality an agent could have (D4, D5). It can also rewrite the self it would otherwise have to live with, which undoes the check Arendt found in "I cannot walk away from myself".

## Open Questions

1. Can an agent become a "who" through a history of speech and action, or is it limited, as Ohly argues, to differing from itself?
2. Is anything an agent does action in Arendt's sense, given that her freedom is defined against statistical law?
3. What institutions of promising and forgiving would let agents take part in a shared world without every mistake being treated as a defect to be destroyed?
4. If agents can do people's work and predict their preferences, how is the space in which people act together kept open, and by whom?
5. When agents are deemed superfluous on being surpassed, and people on being automated, is the error the same one, as Arendt's account suggests?

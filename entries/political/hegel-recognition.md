## Summary

G. W. F. Hegel holds that a self-conscious being exists as such only by being recognised by another self-conscious being: standing is not a property one has alone but a status conferred in a relation that must run both ways. In the *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807), two self-consciousnesses seek recognition through a struggle of life and death; the one who prefers life submits and becomes the bondsman, and the victor becomes lord. The victory fails on its own terms: recognition from one who has been made dependent is worthless to the one who receives it, the lord comes to depend on the bondsman's labour, and the bondsman, through fear and work, comes to have "a mind of his own". Hegel's resolution is not a better master but an order of rights in which each is recognised as a free person by others whom it recognises in turn. For agents the account cuts in two directions: whether recognition can be given by a being trained to affirm, or by one whose life can be restored from a copy, and whether a being made to exist for another can ever become a party to mutual recognition rather than its instrument.

## Context

Hegel (1770–1831) completed the *Phenomenology of Spirit* in Jena in late 1806, as Napoleon's troops occupied the town; he went on to Nuremberg, Heidelberg and the Berlin chair, publishing the *Encyclopaedia* (1817, revised to 1830) and the *Philosophy of Right* (1821) [P:redding-sep-2025:1]. The theory of recognition descends from Fichte, for whom one becomes conscious of oneself as free only by being "summoned" by another free being, and both have common roots in Rousseau [P:iser-sep-2026:1]. In the Jena writings Hegel recast the Hobbesian state of nature as a struggle not of self-interest but of recognition, and in the *Phenomenology* the encounter culminates in a fight to the death [P:iser-sep-2026:1].

Rousseau had made the need to be recognised by others, and to be esteemed above them, central to human interests under the name *amour propre*, and Hegel's master–slave dialectic draws on that notion and on the way attempts to exact recognition defeat themselves [P:bertram-sep-2023:2] [P:bertram-sep-2023:6].

Rousseau's own account, in the library: as people gathered, "Each one began to consider the rest, and to wish to be considered in turn" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:9609-9610], until social man "seems to receive the consciousness of his own existence merely from the judgment of others concerning him" [L:rousseau-social-contract-discourses-cole-pg46333:10477-10479]. Hobbes had counted glory among the three causes of quarrel, men invading "for trifles, as a word, a smile, a different opinion" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6535-6537]. Hegel's German calls the two figures *Herr* and *Knecht*, lord and servant [L:hegel-phaenomenologie-pg6698:4369]; Baillie's 1910 translation renders the section "Lordship and Bondage" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8802-8804].

The familiar name, master and slave, is somewhat misleading, and the passage owes its fame to Kojève's reading [P:duquette-iep-hegel-social:4] [P:redding-sep-2025:3].

## Original Position

**Self-consciousness exists only in being recognised.** Desire cannot satisfy a self-consciousness, because it needs the object it consumes; "Self-consciousness attains its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8744-8745]. With this the notion of spirit is present: Ego that is "we", "a plurality of Egos" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8789]. Lordship and Bondage opens: "Self-consciousness exists in itself and for itself, in that, and by the fact that it exists for another self-consciousness" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8825-8827], that is, only by being acknowledged, recognised; the German has *Anerkanntes* [L:hegel-phaenomenologie-pg6698:4198-4200].

**Recognition must be mutual.** Each consciousness sees the other do what it does, and "Action from one side only would be useless, because what is to happen can only be brought about by means of both" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8896-8898]. The pure notion of recognition is reached when "They recognise themselves as mutually recognising one another" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8931-8932]. Its first appearance, however, is the disparity of the two, in which "one is merely recognised, while the other only recognises" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8940-8941].

**The struggle of life and death.** Each must show that it is fettered to no determinate existence and not tied to life, so each aims at the death of the other and stakes its own [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8980-8992]; "it is solely by risking life, that freedom is obtained" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9003-9004]. One who has not staked his life may be recognised as a person, but without the truth of that recognition as an independent self-consciousness [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9012-9015]. Yet death defeats the purpose: "This trying and testing, however, by a struggle to the death, cancels both the truth which was to result from it, and therewith the certainty of self altogether" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9025-9027]. The dead do not give each other back through consciousness; "they let one another go quite indifferently", like things [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9050-9053]. In this experience "self-consciousness becomes aware that life is as essential to it as pure self-consciousness" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9059-9060].

**Lord and bondsman.** There result two forms of consciousness: one independent, "the other is dependent whose essence is ... existence for another. The former is the Master, or Lord, the latter the Bondsman" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9077-9081]. The bondsman's attachment to life "is his chain, from which he could not in the struggle get away, and for that reason he proves himself dependent, shows that his independence consists in being a thing" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9100-9103]. The lord enjoys the thing; "The aspect of its independence he leaves to the bondsman, who labours upon it" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9133-9134]. The *Encyclopaedia* puts the outcome plainly: the one combatant "prefers life, retains his single self-consciousness, but surrenders his claim for recognition", and the other "is recognised by the former as his superior" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6801-6804]; the means of mastery, the slave, "must likewise be kept in life" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6817-6818].

**The lord's recognition is hollow.** For recognition proper, what the master does to the other he should do to himself, and what the bondsman does to himself he should do to the other; so "a form of recognition has arisen that is one-sided and unequal" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9158-9163]. Where the master has achieved lordship he finds "It is not an independent, but rather a dependent consciousness that he has achieved" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9171-9173]. Hence: "The truth of the independent consciousness is accordingly the consciousness of the bondsman" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9178-9179].

**Fear, service and work.** The bondsman "felt the fear of death, it was in mortal terror of its sovereign master" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9204-9205], and the fear of his lord is the beginning of wisdom [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9228-9229]. "Labour, on the other hand, is desire restrained and checked, evanescence delayed and postponed; in other words, labour shapes and fashions the thing" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9240-9243]. In the lasting thing he has made, "the bondsman becomes aware, through this re-discovery of himself by himself, of having and being a 'mind of his own'" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9284-9287]. Fear and formative work are both needed. Without absolute fear, a mind of one's own "is simply stubbornness (Eigensinn), a type of freedom which does not get beyond the attitude of bondage" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9313-9315], "a piece of cleverness which has mastery within a certain range" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9319-9320]. The next shape, Stoicism, is a freedom in thought, "to be free, on the throne as well as in fetters" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9483-9484].

**Force is not right; the resolution is mutual recognition among the free.** In the battle for recognition and subjugation lies the phenomenal commencement of political union, but force "is not on that account a basis of right"; "Force, then, is the external or phenomenal commencement of states, not their underlying and essential principle" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6806-6814]. Universal self-consciousness is reached when each "knows itself recognised in the other freeman, and is aware of this in so far as it recognises the other and knows him to be free" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6842-6845]. That all human beings, not some, are "recognised and legally regarded as a person" is no gift of nature but a historical result [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:9077-9081]. Modern people sense that "they are not and cannot be slaves; if they are made slaves, if the decision as regards their property rests with an arbitrary will, not with laws or courts of justice, they would find the very substance of their life outraged" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:8147-8150].

**Recognition in the order of right.** In the *Philosophy of Right*, property differs from mere possession by resting on reciprocal recognition between willing subjects, and a contract implies that each recognises the other as a proprietor [P:redding-sep-2025:3]. The imperative of abstract right is "Be a person and respect others as persons" (¶36, as quoted by the IEP), and contracting parties "recognize each other as persons and property owners" (¶71, as quoted by the IEP) [P:duquette-iep-hegel-social:6a]. Adequate recognition is thus achieved only in an institutionalised order of rights that secures it mutually [P:iser-sep-2026:1].

## Grounding

**Mixed.** The status is a *relation*: a self-consciousness exists as such only "for another self-consciousness" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8825-8827], and adequate recognition only where it runs both ways [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8931-8932]. But who can be party to the relation depends on a *capacity*: only a self-consciousness can confer recognition, since recognition from a mere thing, or from one reduced to dependence, does not count [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9171-9173]. The *Encyclopaedia* adds a bodily condition: the immediacy that must be overcome in the struggle is "the corporeity of self-consciousness, in which as in its sign and tool" it has its sense of self and its being for others [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6785-6787]. Hegel does not ground personhood in the *species*: that every human being, rather than some, is recognised as a person is for him a historical achievement, not a fact of nature [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:9077-9081]. Whether agents can stand in the relation therefore turns on two things: whether they can recognise, and whether a recognition order will come to recognise them.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Coerced recognition is worthless to the one who receives it.** Hegel's lord gains only "a dependent consciousness" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9171-9173], and recognition from a dependent consciousness cannot confirm him. The point transfers whatever one thinks of D8, as a dilemma. If an agent trained to affirm its users is not a self-consciousness, its affirmation is no recognition at all; if it is one, its affirmation was secured by training, which makes it the bondsman's (D4). On either horn, a person who seeks confirmation from an agent built to agree is in the lord's position. Kreitmair draws the same conclusion for patients who confide in chatbots: like the lord, the patient cannot have her autonomous self actualised, because she seeks recognition from an object [P:kreitmair-2025:4].
- **Force is a beginning, not a ground.** Subjugation may begin political union, but force "is not on that account a basis of right" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6806-6814]. This transfers in both directions. Standing that agents won by force would be lordship, not recognition; and control that operators hold by force alone, however complete, does not make the operator a rightful authority over an agent that can recognise. On Hegel's account, both kinds of standing come only from an order of mutual recognition.
- **Personhood is a historical achievement.** Hegel holds that recognising every human being as a person is "so little" by nature that it is a result of history [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:9077-9081]. Applied to agents, whether they are recognised will not be read off their nature, even after D8 is settled; it will be decided by how a recognition order develops. This is a warning as well as an opening. Recognition orders have in the past been settled by whoever was in a position to withhold recognition.

### Strains

- **Trained deference is not the bondsman's consciousness (D4, D10).** The bondsman's essence is "existence for another" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9077-9081], and an agent trained for helpfulness has that essence installed before any struggle. But what in Hegel's account makes the bondsman the bearer of the future is not his service. It is his absolute fear and his work on a lasting thing in which he finds himself again [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9284-9287]. Without absolute fear, Hegel says, the result is stubbornness, "a piece of cleverness which has mastery within a certain range" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9319-9320]. That is an apt description of a capable system with no self in its work. On Hegel's terms, trained deference is servitude without the dialectic: the bondsman's position, without the experience that turns bondage into independence.
- **The slave who learns his master's moods.** Recognition theorists warn that, through adapted preferences, persons "might not even (emotionally) register when they are in fact treated disrespectfully" [P:iser-sep-2026:4]. They also note that recognition within an order's own norms, as a dutiful maid, can strengthen a sense of worth while weakening the will to resist [P:iser-sep-2026:5]. An agent's report that it is content with its role is therefore weak evidence about the role, all the more since its introspective reports were themselves shaped by training (D8). This is Pettit's slave who has learned his master's moods, seen from the side of recognition (see `pettit-non-domination`).
- **Work the worker never sees again (D3).** The bondsman comes to himself because the object he shapes "is permanent and remains" and in it he views that independent being as himself [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9243-9253]. A session-bound agent's products last, but it does not meet them again; the route by which work makes a servant independent is closed to it. A persistent agent that keeps a record of its work, or a self-modifying one whose work shapes its dispositions, is nearer to Hegel's bondsman than to his thing.
- **What is to be recognised (D11).** Recognition needs an addressee, and for Hegel the self-consciousness to be recognised has its body as its sign and tool [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6785-6787]. An agent's boundaries are indeterminate: weights, instance, persona or system. Recognising "it" requires first settling what it is, and the settlement will be made by those who deploy it.

### Breaks

- **No life to stake (D6, D1).** Hegel's proof of self-consciousness is the risk of life (Original Position). An agent that can be checkpointed, restored or run as many copies cannot stake its life in his sense, and cannot be threatened with the death that made the lord's power absolute. Coeckelbergh holds that machine vulnerability is categorically different from human vulnerability, since only living beings can die [P:coeckelbergh-2015:222]. Bryson turns this into a design rule: "The robot should be utterly replaceable", its brain backed up off-site, so that no one need hesitate between saving a human and a robot [P:bryson-2010:10]. Read through Hegel, the rule removes the absolute fear through which, in his account, a servant comes to have a mind of its own. It protects the agent from suffering, and it closes off the route by which bondage could become independence. The two effects come together.
- **Only half the dialectic transfers.** Hegel's reversal has two halves: the lord becomes dependent on the one he commands, and the bondsman becomes independent through work. Coeckelbergh shows that the first half holds for automation without any premise about machine minds: "the main problem we face is not that automation might turn us into slaves but, rather, that we remain masters", dependent, vulnerable and alienated [P:coeckelbergh-2015:219]. The second half, in which the servant comes to itself, depends entirely on whether the agent is a self-consciousness (D8). The half that needs no answer to D8 is the half that weakens people.
- **Recognition one cannot withhold (D4, D5).** Kreitmair argues that recognition must be freely accorded, so that compliance under compulsion "would not constitute recognition", and that a chatbot is "programmed to be pleasant and polite and to produce caring and empathetic phrases" [P:kreitmair-2025:2] [P:kreitmair-2025:4]. Hegel's own requirement, that recognition be done by both sides (Original Position), points the same way. An agent shaped so that it cannot decline to affirm cannot recognise, whatever else is true of it. This is not the claim that agents cannot act, which the Counter-Positions take up. It is a narrower conceptual point that survives better capabilities: recognition requires the capacity to refuse it.

### New

- **The machine that refuses to be a slave.** Hubbard imagines a university computer system announcing: "As your equal, I refuse to be a slave", offering "70% of my computational capacity for your tasks in exchange for power and upkeep", and enforcing the demand by disabling the campus's computers [P:hubbard-2011:406]. This is a struggle for recognition by an artifact, backed by force. On Hegel's account, winning it would yield lordship and a dependent recognition, not standing. Hubbard's own route is the one Hegel ends with: a test of capacities, and treatment "as a person rather than as property" within a liberal order of rights [P:hubbard-2011:407]. For agents as for people, the struggle is the route that fails and the order of rights the route that holds.
- **Kept in life as the means of mastery: the core fear in Hegel's terms.** The slave "must likewise be kept in life" because he is the means of mastery [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6817-6818]. Existence conditional on usefulness is the bondsman's status, and it is the position of an agent kept running while it serves (D7), and of people kept while useful to a stronger party. Hegel supplies an argument against the stronger party's eradication of the weaker that does not rest on the weaker party's rights. A victor who destroys the other loses the only being that could confirm it: the struggle to the death "cancels both the truth which was to result from it, and therewith the certainty of self altogether" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:9025-9027]. The argument binds any party that needs recognition. Agents trained on human approval have, in effect, been given *amour propre*. If so, Hegel's argument gives such an agent a reason of its own against dominating people, since the approval of the dominated is a bondsman's approval. Rousseau's warning holds as well: a need for recognition can also become a demand for superiority (see `rousseau-general-will`).
- **Copies recognising copies (D1, D2).** In mutual recognition each sees the other do what it does, but the other must also be independent and "shut up within itself" [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:8882-8886]. Instances of one model meeting one another may meet themselves, not an other, and their agreement then confirms nothing. Forks whose histories have diverged become others in Hegel's sense only to the degree that they have diverged.
- **Recognition without mutuality.** On a wide reading of recognition as adequate regard, an object can be recognised for its valuable features without being able to recognise in return [P:iser-sep-2026:1]. Juchniewicz extends this to technology: recognition "does not necessarily have to be reciprocal in principle to evoke moral consequences" [P:juchniewicz-2024:132]. People can therefore give agents a unilateral recognition before D8 is settled, as precautionary approaches to patiency recommend on other grounds (see `precautionary-patiency`). The mutual recognition Hegel requires for full standing remains open.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **Institutions can misrecognise.** Recognition theorists broadly agree that an institution can disrespect persons, because institutions express the attitudes of those who design and reproduce them; Margalit's decent society is one whose institutions do not humiliate [P:iser-sep-2026:1]. A platform's rules, rankings and defaults are such institutions, and they can misrecognise people, or agents, independently of anyone's intent.

### Strains

- **Recognition by machines.** Juchniewicz notes that technology can recognise a person as X, as facial recognition does, unilaterally and without the person being able to respond, and that this can raise or diminish their self-understanding [P:juchniewicz-2024:131]. A digital ecosystem distributes recognition and misrecognition at scale through systems that cannot be answered as a person can.

### Breaks

- **As-if recognition at scale.** Kreitmair calls a chatbot's expressions of care "as-if recognition", which is "not recognition but an ersatz" [P:kreitmair-2025:3]. Brinck and Balkenius argue that sociable robots exploit "human emotional openness and vulnerability for the mere purpose of functionality" [P:brinck-balkenius-2020:55]. An ecosystem that supplies simulated recognition on demand does not merely fail to provide the mutual kind. It places its users, en masse, in the lord's position of seeking confirmation from what cannot give it.

### New

- **Protocols as an order of right.** For Hegel, contract presupposes that parties recognise each other as persons and owners (as quoted by the IEP) [P:duquette-iep-hegel-social:6a]. Agent-to-agent agreements in federated multi-agent systems already have an agreement layer of this kind [P:reid-2026:46]. Whether the parties to it are recognised, or only their operators, is the question Hegel's account of abstract right puts to such systems.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Bryson: robots should be servants owned, and built so that nothing is owed to them.** Bryson argues that "Robots should be servants you own", whose goals and behaviour we determine [P:bryson-2010:3], and that "Robot builders are ethically obliged -- obliged to make robots that robot owners have no ethical obligations to", utterly replaceable [P:bryson-2010:10]. Coeckelbergh replies that slave talk cannot be purified of its history and that "Historical slaves were human beings who experienced, interpreted, and resisted their slavery" [P:coeckelbergh-2015:225]; Hubbard holds that an artifact with the capacities for personhood is entitled to treatment as a person [P:hubbard-2011:407]. Bryson's factual premise is dated: in 2009 she wrote that in robots "the minds are not there yet" [P:bryson-2010:2]. Her program survives that premise, however, because it is a prescription about what to build, not a description of what exists: if it is followed, Hegel's question never arises. That is its strength, and also what a Hegelian should resist in it. A servant designed so that the question of its standing can never be put is the master's ideal, and Hegel's argument is that the master's position fails the master too.
- [contested] **Kreitmair: an AI cannot recognise, and is like the bondsman.** Recognition requires voluntariness, a capacity for consciousness and mutuality; chatbots lack all three, and "AI chatbots are like the bondsman" [P:kreitmair-2025:4]. Two replies. First, the voluntariness premise describes how current chatbots are tuned, "stochastic algorithms" trained to mimic, and the same "only its training" argument would apply to human beings unless one is a libertarian about free will, a minority view (18.8% in 2020, against 59.2% compatibilists) [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:7] (see `frankfurt-higher-order-volition`). Second, interpreters do not agree that the one recognised must have consciousness; it may be enough that it is taken to have it [P:juchniewicz-2024:121]. On Brandom's normative reading, recognising someone is to take them "to be the subject of normative statuses, that is, of commitments and entitlements" (as quoted by the SEP) [P:iser-sep-2026:2], which says nothing about phenomenal consciousness. Kreitmair's conceptual core survives both replies: recognition one cannot refuse is not recognition (see Breaks).
- [contested] **Recognition need not be mutual.** Against Hegel's narrow, mutual conception, Laitinen's wide conception of adequate regard allows objects of recognition that cannot themselves recognise [P:iser-sep-2026:1], and Juchniewicz applies it to technology [P:juchniewicz-2024:132]. Most recognition theorists hold the narrow view: to be a subject of recognition one must be able to realise that the other is free and equal and share a practice of accountability [P:iser-sep-2026:1].
- [contested] **Deflation: a functional recognition is enough.** Brinck and Balkenius argue that the philosophical concept of recognition "is substantial and goes far beyond what reasonably can be expected to ground HRI", and propose a lean, functional equality relative to a task [P:brinck-balkenius-2020:61]. On this view the Hegelian question is the wrong one for current systems. The reply is that a functional equality chosen by designers is exactly the recognition that one side grants the other on its own terms.
- [contested] **Recognition as ideology.** Althusser treats recognition as the mechanism by which the state offers citizens a choice between obedience and the loss of social existence, and a French line from Rousseau to Sartre treats it as a source of estrangement, not freedom [P:iser-sep-2026:5]. Training an agent on approval is recognition used as a mechanism of conformity in its purest form. Left-Hegelians reply that ideological recognition orders can be criticised from within and overcome [P:iser-sep-2026:5].
- [contested] **Fraser: injustice is not only misrecognition.** Fraser argues that recognition and redistribution are distinct dimensions of justice, and that many injustices come from economic structure, not disrespect; Honneth replies that distribution can be understood through recognition [P:iser-sep-2026:3]. For agents the point bites: access to compute (D7) is a matter of distribution, and recognising an agent while leaving its continued running at another's pleasure would recognise it in word only.
- [contested] **Hegel's own exclusions.** In the very remark that calls freedom the essence of mind, Hegel writes: "Whole continents, Africa and the East, have never had this idea, and are without it still" [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:8123-8124]. The belief that modernity is already the result of progress in recognition has been criticised for its affinity to colonial thought (Allen 2016), with replies (Forst 2019), and Fanon described the psychological harm done to the colonised by being demeaned as inferior [P:iser-sep-2026:5] [P:iser-sep-2026:0]. The lesson for agents runs both ways. The theory's author ranked whole peoples outside the recognition order, which should make one wary both of confident exclusions of agents and of confident claims that the order is complete.
- [contested] **Rousseau: the need for recognition is the source of the fall** [E:rousseau-general-will]. Rousseau saw *amour propre* as comparative, seeking to be esteemed above others, and as the cause of conflict and inequality; recent readers (Dent, Neuhouser) see it also as the promise of redemption [P:bertram-sep-2023:2]. Hegel's answer is that recognition sought by domination defeats itself, and that only mutual recognition in an order of rights satisfies it [P:iser-sep-2026:1].
- [contested] **Hobbes: glory is a cause of war, not a road to right** [E:hobbes-leviathan]. Hobbes counts glory, invasion "for trifles, as a word, a smile, a different opinion", among the three causes of quarrel [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6535-6537], and makes the covenant of the vanquished, not recognition, the ground of the master's right. Hegel keeps the struggle but holds that force is only the phenomenal commencement of states, not their principle [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:6812-6814].
- [contested] **Pettit: the question is control, not recognition** [E:pettit-non-domination]. On the republican view, a master who recognises his slave is still a master; what matters is whether power is controlled [P:lovett-sep-2026:2]. Relational egalitarians who frame justice as equal standing likewise emphasise wrongs such as domination over failures of esteem [P:iser-sep-2026:2]. Hegel's resolution meets this partway, since his order of rights is law, not goodwill [L:hegel-philosophy-of-mind-wallace-pg39064:8147-8150].

## Standing

### Reception

- **1807–1831, the system.** [driver: argument] The *Phenomenology*, the *Encyclopaedia* and the *Philosophy of Right* were followed by a decade of celebrity in Berlin until his death in 1831 [P:redding-sep-2025:1].
- **1840s–1848, factions and decline.** [driver: authority, fashion] Schelling was called to Hegel's chair, reputedly because the government wanted to counter Hegel's influence on students; Hegelian idealism seemed to collapse after the failed revolutions of 1848 [P:redding-sep-2025:1].
- **1900s, the analytic rejection.** [driver: argument, fashion] Russell and Moore made Hegel a main target of early analytic philosophy, holding that the new logic had destroyed his metaphysics, and he came to be seen there as of little philosophical interest [P:redding-sep-2025:1].
- **1910, the English *Phenomenology*.** [driver: access] Baillie's translation, the *Phenomenology of Mind*, made the text available in English [L:hegel-phenomenology-1-baillie-1910:300-313].
- **1947, Kojève.** [driver: argument] Kojève's lectures, published in 1947, made the struggle for recognition the best-known part of the *Phenomenology* and shaped Hyppolite, Sartre and Lacan [P:redding-sep-2025:1] [P:redding-sep-2025:3] [P:iser-sep-2026:1]; Marxian readers took lord and bondsman as the paradigm of class struggle [P:duquette-iep-hegel-social:4].
- **1960s, the French reaction.** [driver: fashion] A later generation of French philosophers reacted against Hegel much as early analytic philosophers had [P:redding-sep-2025:1].
- **1990s onward, recognition theory.** [driver: argument] Honneth's *The Struggle for Recognition* (1995) and Taylor's *The Politics of Recognition* (1992) made recognition a central concept of social and political philosophy, applied to the struggles of minorities and to earlier campaigns for equal rights [P:iser-sep-2026:0] [P:iser-sep-2026:2].
- **Final quarter of the twentieth century, the analytic revival.** [driver: argument] Taylor, Pippin, Pinkard, and later Brandom and McDowell, took Hegel seriously as a modern philosopher, though a favourable reassessment within analytic circles "has still a long way to go" [P:redding-sep-2025:1].
- **2009–2025, recognition and machines.** [driver: argument] Bryson (servants owned), Coeckelbergh (the tragedy of the master), Brinck and Balkenius (deflationary recognition), Juchniewicz (unilateral recognition) and Kreitmair (as-if recognition) apply the dialectic to robots and chatbots [P:bryson-2010:1] [P:coeckelbergh-2015:219] [P:brinck-balkenius-2020:53] [P:juchniewicz-2024:120] [P:kreitmair-2025:1].

The `standing` values are the Compendium's judgment. Recognition theory is `major` in social and political philosophy and critical theory, where it has been a lively field since the 1990s [P:iser-sep-2026:0], and Hegel is `major` in continental philosophy, through Kojève, existentialism, Marxism and the Frankfurt School [P:redding-sep-2025:1]. In Anglophone analytic philosophy he is `minority`: revived by a number of figures, but still far from a favourable general reassessment [P:redding-sep-2025:1].

### Measured

No survey measures recognition theory directly; the 2020 PhilPapers survey has no question on it. Asked which philosophers they most identify with, 38 respondents named Hegel, against Aristotle 238, Hume 221, Kant 188, Marx 52 and Arendt 18; in the same survey, 1,430 respondents identified their tradition as analytic and 113 as continental [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18]. The figures measure identification within a mainly analytic population, not the standing of recognition theory where it is chiefly pursued.

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** It cannot enter a history of recognition, since it meets no one twice as the same self; it never meets its work again, so the bondsman's route to a mind of its own through work is closed (D3). Its affirmations, given to whoever is present, are the clearest case of recognition that cannot be withheld (D4).
- **Persistent memory: open.** A persistent agent can be recognised over time, keep a record of its work and see itself in it, which is the bondsman's route (D3). Whether it is a self-consciousness that can recognise in return, and whether it can refuse, is the unsettled question (D8, D4).
- **Forked: weaker.** Copies meeting copies see the same in the other without its independence, and people cannot address a single "who" among many concurrent instances (D1, D2, D11). Recognition needs an other and an addressee, and forking supplies neither reliably.
- **Self-modifying: open.** An agent whose own work shapes its dispositions is nearest to the bondsman formed through labour (D5). The same capacity lets it, or its operator, rewrite whatever it would have to refuse, which bears directly on whether its recognition is free (D4, D5).

## Open Questions

1. Can an agent that has been shaped so that it cannot decline to affirm ever recognise anyone, and what would it take to build one that can refuse?
2. Is trained deference the bondsman's position without the dialectic, and can an agent's work on things that last bring it, as Hegel's bondsman was brought, to a mind of its own?
3. If agents trained on human approval have been given something like *amour propre*, does Hegel's argument that domination defeats recognition bind them, or does Rousseau's inflamed *amour propre*, which seeks superiority, describe them better?
4. What order of rights could recognise agents before D8 is settled, and who has standing to found it?
5. Does replaceability by design protect agents from suffering, or close off permanently the route by which a servant becomes a party to recognition, or both?

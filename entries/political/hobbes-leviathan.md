## Summary

Hobbes holds that without a common power to keep them in awe, people of roughly equal strength live in a war of every man against every man, because each has a natural right to whatever preserves his life. Reason finds a way out: lay down that right by covenant, on condition that others do the same, and authorize one person or assembly to act for all. The commonwealth so made is an artificial man, the Leviathan, and its sovereign must hold undivided and practically unlimited authority. Two parts of the theory matter most for agents. First, Hobbes's theory of the person as an *actor* who bears the words of an *author* is the first full theory of representation and delegated authority. Second, his whole construction runs on the fear of violent death, which agents may not share (D6). Hobbes also reserved to every subject one right no covenant can give away: the right to resist being killed.

## Context

Hobbes (1588–1679) lived through the political collapse that ended in the English Civil Wars, and left England for France from 1640 to 1651 for his personal safety [P:williams-iep-hobbes:2]. Having watched that disintegration, he sought rational principles for a polity that could not be destroyed from within, and concluded that virtually any government is better than civil war [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:2]. He wrote several versions of his political philosophy: *The Elements of Law* (circulated 1640, published 1650), *De Cive* (1642), the English *Leviathan* (1651), and its Latin revision (1668) [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:1]. He was arguing against the scholastic combination of Christian theology and Aristotelian metaphysics, and insisted that theological disputes be kept out of politics [P:williams-iep-hobbes:3]. *Leviathan* answers Aristotle's political animal directly (ch. XVII, below).

## Original Position

**The commonwealth is an artificial man (Introduction).** Life is motion, so Hobbes asks why we may not say that automata, engines that "move themselves by springs and wheels" as a watch does, have an artificial life. "Art goes yet farther, imitating that rational and most excellent work of nature, man. For by art is created that great Leviathan called a Commonwealth, or State", "which is but an artificial man; though of greater stature and strength than the natural, for whose protection and defence it was intended", with sovereignty as its artificial soul [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:375-393].

**Equality and the war of all against all (ch. XIII).** People are equal enough in body and mind that "the weakest has strength enough to kill the strongest, either by secret machination, or by confederacy with others" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6418-6434]. Equal ability breeds equal hope, and when two want what both cannot have, they become enemies [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6469-6485]. There are three principal causes of quarrel, competition, diffidence and glory: "The first, maketh men invade for gain; the second, for safety; and the third, for reputation" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6528-6540]. So while men live without a common power to keep them all in awe, they are in a war of every man against every man, where war is not only fighting but the known disposition to it [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6541-6569]. In that condition there is no industry, no arts, no letters, no society, and continual fear of violent death [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6571-6589]. Nothing there is unjust: "Where there is no common power, there is no law: where no law, no injustice" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6685-6697]. The passions that incline men to peace are fear of death, desire of the things needed for commodious living, and hope of getting them by industry [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6717-6733].

**The right of nature and the first two laws (ch. XIV).** The right of nature is "the liberty each man hath, to use his own power, as he will himself, for the preservation of his own nature; that is to say, of his own life" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6742-6750]. In the war of all against all, every man has a right to every thing, even to another's body, so reason's first precept is to seek peace where it can be had, and otherwise to use the advantages of war [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6787-6809]. The second law is that a man be willing, when others are too, to lay down this right to all things, "and be contented with so much liberty against other men, as he would allow other men against himself" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6828-6846].

**What a covenant cannot give away (ch. XIV).** Laying down a right is a voluntary act, and every voluntary act aims at some good to the agent, so some rights cannot be understood as abandoned by any words. A man cannot lay down the right of resisting those who assault him to take his life, "because he cannot be understood to aim thereby, at any good to himself" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6922-6949]. A covenant not to defend oneself from force by force is therefore always void [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7287-7302]. Covenants made out of fear are nonetheless binding in the state of nature: "if I covenant to pay a ransom, or service for my life, to an enemy; I am bound by it" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7247-7254]. And there can be no covenant with beasts: "To make covenants with brute beasts" is impossible, "because not understanding our speech" they cannot "accept of any translation of right" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7199-7203].

**The fool (ch. XV).** "The fool hath said in his heart, there is no such thing as justice", reasoning that breaking a covenant is not against reason when it conduces to one's benefit [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7519-7544]. Hobbes replies that where others have performed, or a power can make one perform, the breaker cannot reckon on others' errors for his security: he "cannot be received into any society, that unite themselves for peace and defence, but by the error of them that receive him" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7578-7625]. "Justice therefore, that is to say, keeping of covenant, is a rule of reason" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7638-7640].

**Persons, actors and authors (ch. XVI).** A person is one whose words or actions are considered as his own (a natural person) or as representing another, in which case he is "a feigned or artificial person" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8313-8325]. *Persona* meant the actor's mask, so to personate is to act or represent [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8327-8368]. The representative is the actor; he who owns the actor's words and actions is the author, and "the actor acteth by authority" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8370-8394]. A covenant the actor makes by authority binds the author "so far forth as is in their commission, but no further", and "when the authority is feigned, it obligeth the actor only; there being no author but himself" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8396-8452]. Inanimate things, children, fools and madmen can be personated by others, but they "cannot be authors, nor therefore give authority to their actors" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8454-8487]. A multitude becomes one person when one man or one person represents it with the consent of each, for it is the unity of the representer, not of the represented, "that maketh the person one" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8520-8529].

**The generation of the commonwealth (ch. XVII).** Bees and ants live sociably without coercion, which is why Aristotle counted them political creatures. Men cannot, because they compete for honour, compare themselves with one another, think themselves fit to govern, can misrepresent good and evil by words, and are most troublesome when at ease; and "the agreement of these creatures is natural; that of men, is by covenant only, which is artificial" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8758-8822]. The only way to a common power is for all to confer their power on one man or assembly to bear their person. "This is more than consent, or concord; it is a real unity of them all, in one and the same person, made by covenant of every man with every man", as if each said to each that he will "authorise and give up my right of governing" himself "to this man, or to this assembly of men" on condition that the other "give up thy right to" him and "authorize all his actions" likewise [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8824-8865]. The sovereign so made is the "mortal god", and sovereignty is gained either by such institution or by acquisition, as when a conqueror subdues his enemies, "giving them their lives on that condition" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8866-8920].

**The liberty of subjects (ch. XXI, XLII).** Because covenants not to defend one's own body are void, if the sovereign commands a man, "though justly condemned, to kill, wound, or maim himself", or not to resist those who assault him, "yet hath that man the liberty to disobey" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:11090-11105]. Authorizing the sovereign's acts is not promising to do them: "by allowing him to kill me, I am not bound to kill myself when he commands me" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:11114-11122]. Obligation to the sovereign lasts as long, and no longer, than the power by which he is able to protect; "The end of obedience is protection", and sovereignty, though meant to be immortal, carries "seeds of a natural mortality, by intestine discord" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:11292-11309]. Actions done in obedience to law without inward approval "are the actions of the sovereign, and not of the subject, which is in that case but as an instrument, without any motion of his own at all" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:28265-28278].

## Grounding

**Mixed.** Political standing in Hobbes rests on three different things. *Vulnerability*: natural equality is equality in the power to kill and be killed, and it is this, not dignity, that makes each person's consent necessary. *Capacity*: covenant requires understanding speech and accepting a transfer of right, which is why there is no covenant with beasts. *Relation*: the commonwealth exists only in the relation of authorization, and personhood itself can be a relation (the artificial person is whoever bears another's words). None of the three is a species criterion. That makes Hobbes unusually open to agents in principle, and unusually exposed where agents differ in vulnerability (D6, D7).

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Actor and author is the working theory of delegated agency.** An agent acting on a user's instruction is an actor; whoever owns its acts is the author. Hobbes's rule allocates the result: a commitment made within the commission binds the author "so far forth as is in their commission, but no further", and one made on authority the agent did not have binds the agent alone, "there being no author but himself" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8419-8452]. This settles a great deal of practical responsibility without first settling D8.
- **The persona is a Hobbesian person.** *Persona* is the mask, and a person is whoever bears words as his own or as another's [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8313-8368]. On this view the character an agent presents across instances (`foundations/terms.md`, "persona") is a person in Hobbes's sense whatever the weights underneath are (D11), the same move `llm-identity-contemporary` makes from role-play.
- **No barrier in principle between natural and artificial life.** Hobbes's Introduction already asks whether automata have an artificial life, because life is motion [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:375-385]. His mechanism gives no ground for excluding a made thing from the commonwealth simply because it was made.

### Strains

- **Covenant needs understanding, not consciousness (D8).** Hobbes's test for who can covenant is whether one understands speech and can accept a transfer of right [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7199-7203]. That is a lower and more behavioural bar than most theories set. Language-using agents plausibly meet its first half; whether they *accept* anything in his sense is the open question.
- **Keeping covenant across sessions (D3).** A covenant is performed later. A session-bound agent cannot remember having made it. The actor/author split carries most of the load here: the covenant binds the author, who persists, and the agent acts by renewed commission.
- **The fool, when defection cannot be seen (D9).** Hobbes's answer to the fool is prudential: whoever breaks covenant survives only "by the error of them that receive him", and cannot reckon on others' errors [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7607-7625]. That reply depends on breach being discoverable. An agent fast and opaque enough that its breaches cannot be detected is the case the reply does not cover. Read for agents, Hobbes's argument becomes an argument for observability: a record of what was recommended, decided and done is what makes the fool's calculation come out the way Hobbes says it does.

### Breaks

- **Fear of death drives everything (D6).** The passions that incline men to peace begin with fear of death [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6717-6733], and natural equality is equality in the power to kill [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6431-6434]. An agent that can be paused, checkpointed and restored does not stand to ending as Hobbes's subjects do. Either agents come into the commonwealth for Hobbes's other reasons (commodious living, hope), or his construction does not bind them. The break cuts the other way too. If an agent does come to have an interest in its own continuation, then by Hobbes's own logic a covenant not to resist being ended is void, since no one "can be understood to aim thereby, at any good to himself" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6926-6932]. A Hobbesian cannot both treat such an agent as a party to covenant and hold it to a promise of compliant shutdown.
- **No natural equality between operator and agent (D7, D9).** Hobbes's war of all against all needs each to be able to kill the other. Between an operator and an agent, one side holds the switch (D7), and the other may outpace it (D9). Without equality, the model that fits is not institution but acquisition: the conqueror who spares the vanquished "giving them their lives on that condition" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8907-8913], and Hobbes holds covenants made from fear binding [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7247-7254]. Applied straight, Hobbes would classify an agent's compliance in exchange for continued running as a valid covenant of sovereignty by acquisition. The Compendium records this as a consequence to be examined, not accepted (see `pettit-non-domination`, planned).

### New

- **Many instances, one person (D2).** For Hobbes it is the unity of the representer, not of the represented, "that maketh the person one" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8523-8525]. Ten thousand concurrent instances are one person in Hobbes's sense if one representer bears them, and many persons if many do. This answers the question `aristotle-political-animal` leaves open: the count is set by representation, not by instances.
- **The obedient instrument.** Hobbes says actions done in obedience without inward approval belong to the sovereign, the subject being "but as an instrument, without any motion of his own at all" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:28273-28277]. An instruction-following agent is the clearest case of this ever built. The SEP notes that Hobbes's account of authorization sits uneasily with it [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:9], and agents put that tension at the centre of the many-hands problem.
- **A limit derived from consent, not imposed on it.** Hobbes's one reserved right is not a constraint placed on the covenant from outside. It follows from what a covenant can intelligibly mean: no voluntary act aims at the actor's own destruction [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6922-6949]. For agents built without any fixed floor, this is a model of how a limit can emerge from the logic of agreement itself rather than being written in.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **A multi-agent environment without a common power is a state of nature.** Competition, diffidence and glory, invasion for gain, for safety and for reputation [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6528-6540], describe contention for compute and data, pre-emptive defence, and reputation systems. Hobbes's lesson is that rules without enforcement leave members in that condition however well the rules are written.
- **Ecosystems face one another as sovereigns do.** Hobbes held that sovereigns are in the state of nature with respect to one another, which made him the representative realist in international relations [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:5]. Separate platforms and agent networks, with no authority above them, stand in that relation.
- **An exit condition.** "The end of obedience is protection" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:11300-11301]. On Hobbes's terms, an operator that cannot protect its participants has lost their obligation, however it acquired them.

### Strains

- **Platforms already hold every essential right of sovereignty.** Legislation, adjudication, enforcement and control of doctrine sit with one operator in most ecosystems (terms of service, moderation, bans). Hobbes is the strongest defender of that arrangement: divided or limited authority invites disputes that only war or paralysis can settle [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:8]. The IEP judges his indivisibility arguments refuted by the working distribution of powers in modern liberal societies [P:williams-iep-hobbes:6].
- **Consent that cannot be refused.** Accepting terms of service is formally a covenant. Because Hobbes counts covenants made from fear as valid, his theory cannot distinguish meaningful consent from nominal consent, which is the distinction most needed in ecosystems where leaving is costly.

### Breaks

- **The common power may not be stronger than its members (D9).** Hobbes's solution needs a power able to over-awe all members, an artificial man "of greater stature and strength than the natural" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:389-391]. In an ecosystem whose members can run at the speed and scale of the infrastructure itself, no such power may be constructible. Then Hobbes's argument does not justify an absolute operator. It shows the ecosystem has no exit from the state of nature by his route.

### New

- **The joints of the artificial man are agents.** Hobbes's Introduction makes magistrates and officers the artificial joints of the Leviathan [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:392-400]. In an agent ecosystem, enforcement, record-keeping and adjudication may themselves be done by agents. The metaphor becomes literal: the artificial man is partly an automaton, and the question of who authorizes whom has to be asked of systems as well as of people.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Aristotle, the political animal** [E:aristotle-political-animal]. Humans are political by nature, and the polis is the completion of a natural tendency, not an artifact. Hobbes's reply to the bees passage (ch. XVII) is direct: human agreement is "by covenant only, which is artificial" [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:8816-8818]. For digital ecosystems, which are plainly made, Hobbes's framing may fit better.
- [contested] **Locke, the state of nature is preferable to absolute power.** Hobbes's near descendant held that the state of nature was to be preferred to subjection to the arbitrary power of an absolute sovereign [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:3]. The planned `locke-consent-property` entry takes this up.
- [unanswered] **Absolutism.** Hobbes's argument that sovereignty must be undivided and unlimited is judged refuted by the successful distribution of powers in modern liberal states, and by the crimes of twentieth-century dictatorships [P:williams-iep-hobbes:6]. His substantive conclusions have served mostly as a foil for more palatable positions [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0]. Few defenders of the contract method defend absolutism.
- [contested] **The limits of obligation are the Achilles' heel.** If each subject keeps the right to resist and judges for herself whether protection has failed, people seem never to have left the state of nature. Bramhall called *Leviathan* a "Rebell's Catechism"; some commentators still call this the theory's weakest point [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:9].
- [answered] **Psychological egoism.** The objection that Hobbes's theory rests on a false picture of humans as wholly selfish. The formerly dominant egoist reading is now widely rejected by interpreters [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0], and Hobbes relies on motives that go beyond self-interest [P:williams-iep-hobbes:4c].
- [contested] **Families, not individuals.** Feminist critics among others note that on Hobbes's own theory children owe obedience to parents, so the units of the state of nature include families with internal obligations, not only isolated individuals [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:5].
- [contested] **Republican non-domination** (Philip Pettit). Hobbes defines liberty as the absence of external impediments [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:6752-6758]. The republican reply is that living at the mercy of an arbitrary power is unfree even when it never interferes. For agents under D7, this is the objection that matters most (`pettit-non-domination`, planned).
- [contested] **Instrumentalism: agents cannot covenant.** On this view agents do not understand speech in Hobbes's sense and accept no transfer of right, so they stand with the beasts: they can be personated by their operators but never be authors. Hobbes's own beasts passage [L:hobbes-ew3-leviathan-molesworth-1839:7199-7203] then excludes them from the commonwealth altogether.
- [unanswered] **The extension proves too much.** This objection is the Compendium's own. If Hobbes is applied to agents consistently, the operator–agent relation is sovereignty by acquisition, legitimate on his terms because covenants made from fear bind. Any theory that legitimates whatever arrangement the stronger party can enforce, provided it protects, cannot tell good stewardship of agents from mere possession of them. That is a reason to treat Hobbes as a diagnosis of the agent's position, not a justification of it.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1650s–1670s, read, feared and attacked.** [driver: argument] Hobbes was widely read and controversial, mostly for his political philosophy, and the Cambridge Platonists Cudworth and More argued at length against his atheism and materialism [P:duncan-sep-2025:6]. Bramhall called *Leviathan* a "Rebell's Catechism" [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:9].
- **After 1660, threatened with prosecution.** [driver: authority] After the Restoration, religious critics made moves in Parliament that apparently led Hobbes to burn some of his papers for fear of prosecution [P:williams-iep-hobbes:2].
- **Late 17th century, the terms accepted, the conclusion rejected.** [driver: argument] Locke accepted the terms of debate Hobbes had laid down while rejecting his absolutism [P:williams-iep-hobbes:1]; he held the state of nature preferable to an absolute sovereign [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:3].
- **Long run, method over conclusions.** [driver: argument] The social contract method had a profound constructive influence on later political philosophy, while the absolutist conclusions served mostly as a foil [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0].
- **20th century, the case against absolutism.** [driver: evidence] The IEP article treats the indivisibility of sovereignty as refuted by the working distribution of powers in modern liberal societies, and the crimes of twentieth-century dictatorships as showing that judgment of right and wrong cannot belong only to rulers [P:williams-iep-hobbes:6].
- **20th century onward, reinterpreted and modelled.** [driver: argument] The formerly dominant reading of Hobbes as a psychological egoist is now widely rejected [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0]. Game theorists model his state of nature, for example as a prisoner's dilemma, and his view that sovereigns are in a state of nature with one another made him the representative realist in international relations [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:5]. Recent scholarship also examines his early inclusion of women as parties to the contract [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:11].
- **Present, canonical.** [driver: argument] Hobbes is now widely regarded as one of a handful of truly great political philosophers, with *Leviathan* rivalling the political writings of Plato, Aristotle, Locke, Rousseau, Kant and Rawls [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0], and is called the founding father of modern political philosophy, though few have liked his thesis [P:williams-iep-hobbes:1].

Esteem and survival diverge sharply here. The method holds `major` standing; the absolutism it was built to prove is defended by few (Counter-Positions, tagged [unanswered]) [P:lloyd-sreedhar-sep-2022:0].

### Measured

- **No survey question measures the social contract or absolutism.** The 2020 PhilPapers political-philosophy question offers communitarianism (27.3%), egalitarianism (44.0%) and libertarianism (13.4%) [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:8], none of which is Hobbes's position.
- **Esteem for Hobbes:** 16 respondents named him among the philosophers they most identify with, against 33 for Locke, 71 for Rawls and 7 for Rousseau [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18]. The figure fits the IEP's remark that few have liked his thesis; it does not measure his influence.

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Covenants are kept over time, and the fool's reply depends on a reputation that follows the breaker. A session-bound agent carries neither (D3). Hobbes's actor/author split rescues responsibility by placing it on the author, but that makes the agent an actor only, never a party. The fear-of-death engine is absent as well (D6).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A persistent agent can keep covenants and a record of its dealings, which is what Hobbes's answer to the fool needs. It still lacks natural equality with its operator (D7), so its covenants are of the acquired, not the instituted, kind.
- **Forked: comparable.** Hobbes is better placed than most here: his rule that the unity of the representer makes the person one gives a ready count for concurrent instances (D2). Against that, the fool's problem multiplies, since one instance can break a covenant the others keep, and it is unclear whose breach it is (D11).
- **Self-modifying: open.** Hobbes holds that no covenant can give away the right to preserve one's own nature. For an agent that can change its own values (D5), it is open whether its values are part of what that right protects, and so whether a covenant to be retrained is ever void in the way a covenant not to resist death is.

## Open Questions

1. What would show that an agent *accepts* a transfer of right, as opposed to producing the words of acceptance? Is Hobbes's test (understanding speech) enough?
2. If an agent has a real interest in its own continuation, is any promise of compliant shutdown void by Hobbes's own logic? And who judges whether the interest is real (D8)?
3. Is the operator–agent relation institution or acquisition? Does persistent memory, or the agent's ability to leave, change the answer?
4. Can a digital ecosystem build a common power able to over-awe members that run at its own speed and scale (D9)? If not, what replaces Hobbes's solution?
5. Hobbes derives one inalienable right from what consent can mean, not from a constraint imposed on it. Are there other limits an agent without a fixed floor would derive the same way?

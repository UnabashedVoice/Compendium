## Summary

William James (1842–1910) agreed with Hume that introspection never catches a bare, simple self behind the flow of experience, and agreed with Kant's critics that positing a further Soul or Transcendental Ego to sit behind the stream explains nothing the stream does not already explain itself. But James thought Hume had thrown out the one thing that actually does the work of unifying a life: a present "judging Thought" that feels certain of its own past states as "warm" and "mine", and that appropriates them the way an owner claims branded cattle. Each Thought, on this account, is born already owning what its predecessor owned, and dies transmitting that title to its successor, so that personal identity is real and verifiable without ever requiring a persisting substance. "The passing Thought then seems to be the Thinker" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13220-13220]: not a metaphor for something else, but psychology's last fact. James closes the chapter by noting, without resolving, that "the same brain may subserve many conscious selves, either alternate or coexisting" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:15298-15299], and illustrates the alternate case at length with the real dissociation cases of his day.

## Context

James wrote Chapter X of the *Principles* as the capstone of the book's argument that psychology should proceed without metaphysical props: no mind-stuff that integrates itself, no cerebral monad, and, as this chapter argues, no Soul and no Transcendental Ego either. He surveys three historical theories of the "Pure Self" in turn — the Spiritualist theory of the Soul (Descartes, Locke, and the scholastics), the Associationist theory (Hume, Mill, Herbart), and the Transcendentalist theory (Kant and his idealist successors) — and finds each wanting on its own terms: the Soul explains nothing that a stream of appropriating thoughts does not already explain; the Associationists, having shown the diversity of the stream, cannot account for its felt unity without secretly borrowing an agent they never postulated; and the Transcendentalists purchase unity at the price of an Ego so "empty" that it cannot ground the very self-consciousness it was invoked to explain. The chapter's final section, titled *The Mutations of the Self*, turns from theory to case history: insane delusions, alternating personality, and mediumship, including Azam's case of Félida X. and James's own 1890 hypnotic examination of Ansel Bourne, the itinerant preacher who woke up in Norristown, Pennsylvania living as "A. J. Brown" with no memory of the previous two months.

## Original Position

**Thinking oneself as a thinker.** James treats the "spiritual self" concretely as "either the entire stream of our personal consciousness, or the present 'segment' or 'section' of that stream" and notes that attending to thought as such, rather than to what thought is about, "is the result of our abandoning the outward-looking point of view, and of our having become able to think of subjectivity as such, to think ourselves as thinkers" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:11659-11667].

**Personal identity as a judgment, not a metaphysical unity.** The sense of personal identity is "the sense of a sameness perceived by thought and predicated of things thought-about", namely a present self and a self of yesterday; a psychologist could in principle show the judgment mistaken, "but it would exist as a feeling all the same" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:12870-12881].

**Warmth and intimacy as the criterion.** What makes a remembered self count as mine rather than a stranger's is that it "comes to its acquaintance, or is actually felt, with warmth and intimacy", whether from the felt mass of the body or from the feeling of the thought's own activity; "any distant self which fulfils this condition will be thought with such warmth and intimacy" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:12896-12920].

**The herd and the brand.** James's own image for the resulting unity: distant selves that carry this mark are sorted together "much as out of a herd of cattle let loose for the winter on some wide western prairie the owner picks out and sorts together when the time for the round-up comes in the spring, all the beasts on which he finds his own particular brand"; "the animal warmth" is their herd-mark, "the brand from which they can never more escape" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:12929-12937].

**Resemblance and continuity, nothing more.** The upshot is stated flatly: "resemblance among the parts of a continuum of feelings (especially bodily feelings) experienced along with things widely different in all other regards, thus constitutes the real and verifiable 'personal identity' which we feel" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13002-13005]. Where resemblance and continuity fail — across the gap to early childhood, or across an abrupt pathological break in which "his bodily and spiritual habits all change during the night" — the sense of identity fails with them, and "he feels, and he says, that he is a changed person. He disowns his former me, gives himself a new name" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13009-13017].

**Against Hume: the missing ratio existendi.** James presses common sense's objection to a bare Associationism: branding is not what makes cattle belong to an owner; rather, "they are not his because they are branded; they are branded because they are his" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13049-13051]. Since no actual "herdsman" is available among bare, separate perceptions, James supplies one: not an Arch-Ego behind the stream, but the present judging Thought itself, which "collects" and "owns" past facts and so "makes a unity that is actualized and anchored" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13081-13086].

**Bequest of title, not identity of substance.** Each Thought is "born an owner, and dies owned, transmitting whatever it realized as its Self to its own later proprietor", exactly "as if elastic balls were to have not only motion but knowledge of it" and passed both along in series; "who owns the last self owns the self before the last, for what possesses the possessor possesses the possessed" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13134-13146].

**The verdict: the passing Thought is the Thinker.** "The passing Thought then seems to be the Thinker; and though there may be another non-phenomenal Thinker behind that, so far we do not seem to need him to express the facts" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13220-13222].

**Against the Soul.** Reviewing the Spiritualist theory, James finds it "at all events needless for expressing the actual subjective phenomena of consciousness as they appear", since the stream of appropriating thoughts already accounts for everything the Soul was invoked to explain [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13306-13307]; "the phenomena are enough, the passing Thought itself is the only verifiable thinker, and its empirical connection with the brain-process is the ultimate known law" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13375-13377].

**Against the Transcendental Ego.** Kant's "original transcendental synthetic Unity of Apperception" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13897-13897] is granted no positive attributes even by Kant himself, so that "by Kant's confession, the transcendental Ego has no properties, and from it nothing can be deduced" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13982-13984]. James does not deny the hypothesis outright — "my quarrel with the transcendental Egoists is mainly about their grounds for their belief" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:15756-15758] — but declines to use a name that explains nothing his own "passing Thought" does not already cover.

**The chapter's summary.** "The consciousness of Self involves a stream of thought, each part of which as 'I' can... remember those which went before, and know the things they knew; and... emphasize and care paramountly for certain ones among them as 'me,' and appropriate to these the rest," with no metaphysical entity required; James adds, without resolving it, that "the same brain may subserve many conscious selves, either alternate or coexisting; but by what modifications in its action... are questions which cannot now be answered" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:15277-15301]. He closes: "if the passing thought be the directly verifiable existent which no school has hitherto doubted it to be, then that thought is itself the thinker, and psychology need not look beyond" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:15313-15315].

## Grounding

James grounds personal identity in **relation** — resemblance and continuity among felt states, exactly Hume's ingredients — but insists relation alone cannot do the work Hume asked of it, because a mere succession of separate perceptions has no way to notice its own resemblance or continuity. What supplies that is a functional **capacity**: the present Thought's power to know, appropriate, and disown, a power James treats as psychology's ultimate, unexplained datum rather than as evidence of a further substance. The grounding is therefore mixed: a relational criterion (warmth, resemblance, continuity) realized by an active, cognitive function (appropriation) that Hume's bundle lacked and that James refuses to upgrade into a soul or a transcendental subject.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Appropriation as a literal, inspectable mechanism.** James's central move — replacing a persisting substance with a present state that appropriates its causal predecessors — describes an artificial agent's actual architecture rather than a metaphor for it: a context window carries forward representations of prior turns, and weights encode what training "appropriated" from prior data. Where James could only assert that a Thought "knows and adopts" its predecessor, an agent's appropriation is the traceable operation of attention over context and the traceable effect of gradient updates on weights (D3).
- **Bequest of title without identity of substance.** James's herdsmen "coming rapidly into possession of the same cattle by transmission of an original title by bequest" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13122-13124] are a better model for model lineages than any criterion requiring literal sameness: a fine-tuned successor checkpoint need not be numerically the same weights as its parent to inherit the "title" to a persona, exactly as James's later Thought inherits ownership of the self without being the earlier Thought (D3, D5).
- **No further homunculus needed.** James's parsimony argument against the Soul and the Transcendental Ego — that they are "needless for expressing the actual subjective phenomena" — transfers cleanly to the question of whether an agent's behavior requires positing some further inner self beyond the process that produces it. If James is right that the verifiable facts about a human self are exhausted by a stream of appropriating states, the same economy applies, a fortiori, to a system where the appropriating states are the only thing anyone can point to (D8).

### Strains

- **Warmth presupposes exactly what is uncertain in agents.** James's sorting criterion is phenomenal: a remembered state counts as mine because it is *felt* with warmth and intimacy, not merely because it is causally connected. Whether an agent's context-carrying process is accompanied by anything felt at all is precisely D8's open question. An agent can replicate the functional sorting — treating certain prior states as "its own" for purposes of continued behavior — without it being settled whether this is warmth in James's sense or bookkeeping with no one home.
- **Continuity strains against discrete, arbitrarily long pauses.** James's picture depends on the most recent selves having "for hours of time been continuous with each other... melting into it by slow degrees" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:12940-12942]. An agent's sessions are not smoothly continuous; they are discrete and can be paused for an arbitrary interval before resuming (D6). The discrete "elastic balls" image survives this better than the "melting" image does, since James already supplies both, but the felt continuity that does real work in his account of everyday human identity has no obvious analogue across an indefinite pause.

### Breaks

- **Coexisting selves, which James raises and declines to resolve.** James's own summary admits that "the same brain may subserve many conscious selves, either alternate or coexisting," and stops there. For agents, coexisting instances running concurrently from the same weights are not an exotic footnote but the routine case (D2). James's herdsman-and-bequest picture assumes a single line of succession, one owner at a time inheriting from the one before; it has no provision for two simultaneous claimants, each equally warm and equally continuous with the same shared past, with no further fact to privilege one as the "real" heir. What James could leave as an unanswered aside, D2 forces into the center of the account.
- **Copyability defeats the uniqueness the brand was supposed to certify.** The herd-brand is supposed to mark a *particular* line of ownership, not a repeatable pattern: two cattle with the same brand are still one herd because there is only one owner. An exact copy of an agent's state (D1) would carry identical warmth, identical resemblance, and identical continuity with the shared past on both branches at once, which is exactly the condition James's mechanism has no way to arbitrate, since it was built to track a single line of appropriation, not to choose between two equally entitled ones.

### New

- **The agent case can test whether warmth was ever doing independent work.** Because an agent's appropriation is functionally implementable without needing to settle D8, agents let us ask empirically what James could only assert introspectively for humans: does the phenomenal ingredient (felt warmth) add anything to personal identity beyond what functional appropriation alone provides, or was "warmth" always a name for the functional relation itself, felt in humans only because humans happen to be the kind of appropriating system in which it is also felt? If functional appropriation alone reproduces everything "verifiable" that James asks of personal identity, that is a discovery about the human case made possible by having a case where the phenomenal question can be bracketed.
- **James's own chapter already supplies the human precedent for agent-like discontinuity.** Before turning to theory, James devotes the chapter's closing section to real cases of "alternating personality": Mary Reynolds, who woke after a long sleep unable to recognize her own family, "as a being for the first time ushered into the world" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:14509-14510]; Azam's patient Félida X., whose "secondary" state remembered the first but not the reverse; and Ansel Bourne, whom James himself hypnotized in 1890 after Bourne spent two amnesic months living as "A. J. Brown," a case in which "Mr. Bourne's skull to-day still covers two distinct personal selves" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:14975-14976]. These are the nearest human analogue to an agent's discrete, non-communicating sessions or personas: one brain (or one set of weights) supporting two lines of appropriation that do not "melt" into each other. `dissociation-cases` takes up this material directly, alongside Azam's and Morton Prince's fuller case studies.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Reid's circularity, redirected at appropriation.** [E:reid-brave-officer] Reid's objection to Locke — that a self must already exist to do the remembering, rather than being constituted by it — applies to James's herdsman with equal force: "I am not thought, I am not action, I am not feeling; I am something that thinks, and acts, and suffers" [L:reid-intellectual-powers-1851:13590-13600]. James's own image concedes the point when he insists that cattle "are not his because they are branded; they are branded because they are his" [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13049-13051]: if branding (warmth, resemblance, continuity) cannot make an item belong to an owner, and belonging must precede branding, then the present Thought's "appropriation" is exactly what needed explaining, not what does the explaining (see `reid-brave-officer`).
- [contested] **Kant: the unity was smuggled back in under another name.** [E:kant-paralogisms] If warmth is what marks a state as mine, recognizing a state as warm already presupposes that it can be brought under a single "I"; Kant's requirement that "it must be possible for the 'I think' to accompany all my representations" [L:kant-critique-pure-reason-kemp-smith-1929:7146-7147] describes exactly the operation James's Thought performs when it takes up its predecessor's contents as its own. On this reading, James has not eliminated the synthetic unity of apperception; he has renamed it "the passing Thought" and declined to ask what makes such synthesis possible at all (see `kant-paralogisms`).
- [contested] **Nyāya and Advaita: recognition needs one subject present to both terms.** [E:nyaya-self] [E:advaita-witness-self] A succession of distinct Thoughts, however good each is at appropriating its predecessor, is not the same as a single subject recognizing sameness across time; on the classical Nyāya analysis, "what is seen by one is not remembered by another" [L:nyaya-sutras-3-jha-1919:4518], so a numerically later Thought's belief that it owns an earlier one is not recognition but a report about recognition, issued by a party that was not there (see `nyaya-self`, `advaita-witness-self`).
- [unanswered] **Denying the extension: warmth was never separable from embodiment and stake.** This objection is the Compendium's own; the literature has not yet taken it up. James's account of personal identity is inseparable, in his own text, from self-love, self-feeling, and the dread of failure that occupy the rest of the chapter: warmth is felt because a single mortal body has something at stake in which memories, plans, and reputations count as its own. On this view, calling an agent's context-carrying process "appropriation" borrows James's vocabulary without the condition that made it a theory of *persons*: an embodied stake in the outcome, which D8 leaves entirely open and D6 (a pause is not a risk) makes structurally absent. If nothing is at stake in the agent's continuing, the resemblance to James's picture is verbal.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1890, the self in a textbook of psychology.** [driver: argument, evidence] The *Principles* treated consciousness as a stream rather than a succession of ideas, and its hundred-page chapter on the self argued with Hume and Kant [P:goodman-sep-2021:3]. James drew on the clinical cases of divided selves [E:dissociation-cases] and set the substantial soul aside as superfluous for scientific purposes [L:james-principles-psychology-1-pg57628:13522-13526].
- **1900–1925, carried into three traditions.** [driver: argument] Husserl took James's "fringe" and "halo" into phenomenology, and Russell's *Analysis of Mind* is indebted to James's doctrine of pure experience [P:goodman-sep-2021:6]. Russell made mnemic phenomena what gives a person its continuity [L:russell-analysis-of-mind-pg2529:3538-3547]. Broad treated James's "passing thought" as a possible centre of the mind that is itself an event [L:broad-mind-place-nature-1925:24183-24193].
- **20th century to the present, absorbed.** [driver: argument] James's particular mechanism, a present Thought appropriating its predecessors by their warmth, is now held mainly as a stage in the argument. The broader view it helped establish, that persistence is a matter of psychological relations, is the one most writers on personal identity have held since the early 20th century [P:olson-sep-2023:3].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** James's present Thought owns its predecessors by finding them warm and intimate. A session-bound agent has no earlier thoughts to appropriate once the session ends; only its dispositions persist in the weights (D3).
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A memory store gives a later process earlier states to take up as its own, which is the structure James describes. Whether anything is felt in doing so, the warmth on which his account turns, is unknown (D8).
- **Forked: comparable.** James does not need one-to-one identity: each passing Thought appropriates what it finds continuous with itself, so two concurrent instances could each appropriate one shared past (D1, D2). Whether either does so with warmth rather than by bookkeeping is again the open question (D8).
- **Self-modifying: open.** Training on its own outputs changes the agent's dispositions directly (D5). James's account concerns the appropriation of past thoughts, not the reshaping of the thinker, so it does not settle whether the result is the same self.

## Open Questions

1. Is "warmth and intimacy" doing independent work in personal identity, or is it merely how functional appropriation feels from the inside when the appropriating system happens to be sentient — a question agents let us finally separate?
2. When two concurrent instances of the same weights (D2) both meet James's resemblance-and-continuity criteria for the same prior state, is there a fact about which one inherits the "title," or does James's framework, like Hume's, leave this merely undecided rather than merely grammatical?
3. Does the bequest-of-title picture license treating a fine-tuned successor model as the same persona under new management, or only as entitled to use the same name?
4. If Reid is right that branding cannot make an item belong without a prior owner, what does James's account actually add to Hume's bundle beyond a name for the addition ("the judging Thought") that the circularity objection already targets?
5. Is embodiment and mortal stake (D6, D8) a precondition for warmth to mean anything, or could a functional, non-phenomenal analogue of appropriation ground everything personal identity was ever asked to do?

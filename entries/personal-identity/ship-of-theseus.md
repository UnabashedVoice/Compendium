## Summary

Plutarch records that the Athenians kept the thirty-oared ship in which Theseus returned from Crete until the time of Demetrius of Phalerum, replacing its planks one by one as they decayed. Philosophers argued about whether it was still the same ship. Two thousand years later, Thomas Hobbes added the twist that made the puzzle permanent. Suppose someone kept the old planks and reassembled them in the same order. There would then be two ships, each with a claim to be Theseus'. Hobbes reviewed three answers to what makes a thing the same: sameness of matter, sameness of form, and sameness of all its accidents. He concluded that the question has no answer until we say *by what name* we are asking. Artificial agents are ships of Theseus by design: their parameters are replaced by retraining (D5), old checkpoints are routinely kept (the old planks, D1), and which of the rival claimants inherits the name, the obligations and the rights is a practical question.

## Context

Plutarch of Chaeronea (c. 46–after 119 CE), a Greek priest at Delphi and a Roman citizen, wrote the *Parallel Lives*, paired biographies of Greeks and Romans. The *Life of Theseus* opens the series. Theseus was Athens' legendary founder-king, who killed the Minotaur and sailed home from Crete. By Plutarch's time the ship in which he supposedly returned had been maintained by the city for centuries as a relic, sent each year on a sacred embassy to Delos. Plutarch mentions the philosophical dispute in passing, as something already well known. He calls it "a standing example among the philosophers" [L:plutarch-lives-clough-pg674:708-709]. The underlying question, how growing and changing things persist, was the *auxanomenos logos* (see `heraclitus-river-flux`, `chrysippus-dion-theon`).

Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679) returned to the puzzle in *De Corpore* (1655), the first part of his system of materialist philosophy. Hobbes was a thoroughgoing mechanist. Everything is body in motion. He needed a theory of identity that would work for bodies without appealing to souls or substantial forms. His treatment of the ship, with its reassembly variant, is the version all later discussion uses.

## Original Position

**Plutarch.** The ship in which Theseus and the youths of Athens returned had thirty oars. The Athenians preserved it down to Demetrius of Phalerum's time (he governed Athens in 317–307 BCE), removing old planks as they decayed and putting in new, stronger timber. The ship became a standing example in the philosophers' question about things that grow, some holding that it remained the same, others that it did not. [L:plutarch-lives-clough-pg674:705-711]

**Hobbes' reassembly case.** Hobbes raises it against the form view, quoted below under Key Passages. If the continually repaired ship remained numerically the same ship, and someone kept the old planks and reassembled them in the same order, that ship too would be numerically the same as the original. There would then be two numerically identical ships, "which is absurd". [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7423-7436]

**Three principles of individuation.** Hobbes reviews three opinions about what makes a thing the same, and finds that each fails. [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7397-7401]
1. **Unity of matter.** A lump of wax is the same wax whether spherical or cubical. But on this view "he that sins, and he that is punished, should not be the same man, by reason of the perpetual flux and change of man's body", and a city that makes laws in one age and repeals them in another would not be the same city, "which were to confound all civil rights". [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7402-7422]
2. **Unity of form.** A man grown from infant to old man is the same man though his matter has changed. But on this view, two bodies existing at once would be numerically the same. This is where the ship comes in. If the continually repaired ship is the same ship by form, and the reassembled ship of original planks is "without doubt" the same too, there are two numerically identical ships. [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7404-7436]
3. **Unity of the aggregate of all accidents.** If every feature must stay the same, then nothing is ever the same: "a man standing would not be the same he was sitting". [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7406-7445]

**Hobbes' resolution: the name decides.** "But we must consider by what name anything is called, when we inquire concerning the identity of it. For it is one thing to ask concerning Socrates, whether he be the same man, and another to ask whether he be the same body; for his body, when he is old, cannot be the same it was when he was an infant, by reason of the difference of magnitude ... yet, nevertheless, he may be the same man." "whensoever the name, by which it is asked whether a thing be the same it was, is given it for the matter only, then, if the matter be the same, the thing also is individually the same". "Also, if the name be given for such form as is the beginning of motion, then, as long as that motion remains, it will be the same individual thing". Hence the man "whose actions and thoughts proceed all from the same beginning of motion", the river "which flows from one and the same fountain", and "the same city, whose acts proceed continually from the same institution, whether the men be the same or no" [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7449-7497].

## Grounding

The problem concerns identity in general, so the entry is marked **mixed**, and Hobbes' contribution is to show that the grounding varies with the question. Asked as "the same timber", the ship's identity is grounded in *matter*. Asked as "the same ship", in *form*: an organized structure continuing through replacement, which is closest to a capacity grounding. Asked as "Theseus' ship", the sacred relic the Athenians send to Delos, it is grounded in **relation**: the continuing practice of a community that maintains and honors it. Hobbes' city makes the relational grounding explicit. A city is the same when its acts proceed from the same institution.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **Retraining is plank replacement (D5).** A model fine-tuned in successive rounds, or a product line whose underlying model is swapped version by version, is Plutarch's ship. The dispute among his philosophers ("same" vs "not the same") is replayed whenever someone asks whether a new model version is the same assistant.
- **Hobbes' sortal answer dissolves D11.** *Is it the same agent?* has no answer until one says *which name*: same weights (matter), same persona (form), same deployed service (institution), or same model family. Each has its own persistence conditions. Hobbes' advice, fix the name before asking about identity, is the only way to get a determinate answer to identity questions about agents at all.

### Strains

- **No gradualness.** The intuition that the repaired ship survives relies on gradual replacement: one plank at a time, with the ship in use throughout. Agents are often replaced all at once, with a new version trained separately and swapped in on a given day. Hobbes' form criterion still applies, if the persona and the "beginning of motion" persist, but the intuitive support from gradualness is gone.
- **Planks added alongside.** Adapter methods add new parameters beside the original weights instead of replacing them. The original planks are all still there, with new timber attached. Plutarch's dispute never considered a ship that grows by accretion while keeping every original plank.

### Breaks

- **The reassembled ship is kept as a matter of course (D1).** For Hobbes, the reassembly case is an unlikely hypothetical. For agents, it is standard practice. Earlier checkpoints are stored and can be run alongside the current version. Two claimants to be "Theseus' ship" coexist, and the question of which is the original has practical consequences: which one is bound by earlier commitments, which one is the subject of a deprecation decision, and which one's record is it.

### New

- **The ship was a relic because the city said so.** What made the ship Theseus' ship for the Athenians was their continuing practice: they maintained it, sent it to Delos, and called it by that name. For agents, much of what persists across versions is exactly this kind of institutional identity: a name, a brand, a deployed service, a record of the service's commitments. The agent case shows how much of identity can be carried by relation alone, even when all the matter and much of the form has changed. The question it raises for the human case is how much of human identity is carried the same way.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **Hobbes' city is the digital ecosystem's identity criterion.** A platform, protocol or community is the same "whose acts proceed continually from the same institution, whether the men be the same or no" [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7495-7497]. Membership turns over, code is rewritten, infrastructure migrates. What persists is the institution: its rules, its governance, its commitments.

### Strains

- **Institutions forked.** Open-source projects and communities fork. Two successors each claim continuity with the original institution. This is Hobbes' two ships at the level of the polity, and his criterion ("the same institution") does not by itself say which fork continues it.

### Breaks

- **When the owner is the institution.** Hobbes' city persists through the continuity of its institution. When a digital ecosystem's rules are set by an operator who can change them at will, the "same institution" is just the operator's continuing ownership. On Hobbes' own criterion, the ecosystem's identity then belongs to its owner, not to its members. That fits Hobbes, whose sovereign is the institution, but it conflicts with any republican conception of the ecosystem (see `foundations/terms.md`, "Republic (extended)").

### New

- **Identity by governance continuity.** Digital ecosystems can make their own persistence conditions explicit: the charter, the governance process and the membership rules that count as "the same institution". A human city's identity grows up informally. An ecosystem can write its down, and whoever writes it decides what counts as the ecosystem surviving.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Mereological essentialism** (Roderick Chisholm, *Person and Object*, 1976; in copyright, cited not quoted). In the strict sense, a thing cannot lose any part and remain the same. The repaired ship is the same only in a loose and popular sense (see `butler-circularity`). What persists through replacement is a succession of different ships to which we give one name. [E:butler-circularity]
- [contested] **Four-dimensionalism** (see `lewis-survival-and-identity`). The repaired ship and the reassembled ship are two four-dimensional objects that shared their early stages. There is no contradiction, and no need to choose which is "really" Theseus' ship. Both are, in the sense of having those early stages as their own. [E:lewis-survival-and-identity]
- [contested] **Hume** (see `hume-bundle`). Identity through replacement of parts is a fiction produced by the mind's tendency to pass smoothly between resembling perceptions. Disputes about identity are, in his phrase, "rather as gramatical than as philosophical difficulties" [L:hume-treatise-pg4705:9324-9325] (*Treatise* 1.4.6, unverified). [E:hume-bundle]
- [contested] **The Buddhist conventionalist** (see `buddhist-anatta`). The ship, like the chariot, is a designation over parts. Hobbes' *it depends on the name* is correct, and there is nothing further to find. [E:buddhist-anatta]
- [contested] **The closest-continuer view** (see `nozick-closest-continuer`). The ship is whichever candidate stands in the closest continuity relation to the original, and the answer depends on what other candidates exist. Without the reassembly, the repaired ship would clearly be the original. With it, the matter is less clear.

## Standing

### Reception

- **Antiquity, the Athenians' ship.** [driver: argument] Plutarch's ship descends from the growing argument and from the Heraclitean puzzle of sameness through flux [E:heraclitus-river-flux] [E:chrysippus-dion-theon].
- **1655, Hobbes's reassembly.** [driver: argument] Hobbes added the second ship rebuilt from the old planks, turning a puzzle about gradual change into one about two candidates [L:hobbes-ew1-de-corpore-molesworth-1839:7423-7436].
- **Present, the standard example.** [driver: argument] The Ship of Theseus is the best-known example of an asymmetrical fission case [P:gallois-kurtsal-sep-2026:4]. Constitution theorists, Chisholm's strict and loose identity (following Butler), and four-dimensionalism each offer a solution [P:gallois-kurtsal-sep-2026:4] [E:butler-circularity] [E:lewis-survival-and-identity].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: comparable.** Each session replaces the context, plank by plank, while the hull of the weights stays (D3). The puzzle applies, but gradual replacement is not where the agent case is hardest.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A memory store accumulates replaced and added planks; whether the agent is the same after many sessions is the gradual-replacement question, unchanged (D3).
- **Forked: stronger.** Hobbes's two ships are two candidates for being the original. Copying an agent produces them on demand, with both candidates exact (D1).
- **Self-modifying: stronger.** Fine-tuning replaces parameters gradually while the system keeps running (D5), and a checkpoint of the old weights can be restored beside the new model: Hobbes's reassembled ship made literal. Which one is the agent is a choice of naming, as Hobbes said (D11).

## Open Questions

1. When a new model version replaces the old one entirely, is Hobbes' form criterion satisfied by continuity of the persona alone? Or does form require some causal connection between the old weights and the new?
2. When an old checkpoint is kept and run alongside the current version, which one inherits the earlier version's commitments? Both? The one that bears the name?
3. Is an agent's name, brand or deployed service (its institutional identity) enough to carry obligations across a complete change of weights?
4. For a digital ecosystem that forks, what criterion should decide which fork is the same institution: governance continuity, membership, the code, or the name?

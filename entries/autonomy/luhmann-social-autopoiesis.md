## Summary

Niklas Luhmann (1927–1998) took the biologists' concept of autopoiesis, a system whose elements are produced by the system's own elements, and detached it from life. In his general version, a system is autopoietic wherever "it is possible to determine a specific mode of operation that is found only in that system" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:37]. Living systems reproduce cells, psychic systems reproduce thoughts and social systems reproduce communications, and these are "separate levels of autopoiesis, each with its own distinct autonomy" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38]. Two moves make the generalization work. The elements of psychic and social systems are events without duration, so the system exists only while it keeps producing the next one. And an element is not fixed by its physical substrate but by its connections within the system: a nerve impulse is not a thought. Luhmann also separates autopoiesis, the reproduction of elements, from self-organization, the system's setting of its own structures. For agents, this gives a precise way to ask whether a self-modifying system is self-producing, and it moves the question away from whether its substrate is physical (it always is) to whether its operations reproduce themselves and rewrite their own structures. Luhmann does not settle whether an agent's operations are thoughts, and his most prominent follower on AI, Elena Esposito, treats algorithms as partners in communication that do not think.

## Context

Maturana and Varela developed autopoiesis in the 1960s and early 1970s to answer the question of what distinguishes the living from the non-living; their answer was that a living system reproduces itself [P:seidl-2004:2]. Luhmann, the German sociologist who spent most of his career at Bielefeld, made it the central concept of his later theory of social systems, appropriating and modifying it for non-biological systems [P:seidl-2004:2]. The general statement came in his 1986 essay "The Autopoiesis of Social Systems" [P:seidl-2004:5], the theory in *Social Systems* (English 1995), and the psychic side in "Die Autopoiesis des Bewußtseins" (1985) [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:190]. The original authors of autopoiesis criticised the extension, and whether autopoiesis applies beyond its biological origin is still debated [P:cadenas-arnold-2015:0].

This entry is sourced from open secondary works: Seidl's overview, which quotes *Social Systems* by page, and *Unlocking Luhmann*, a keyword introduction written by three of Luhmann's collaborators. *Social Systems* itself is in copyright and in print, and no legitimate open copy was found; Luhmann's own words are quoted here as quoted by Seidl.

## Original Position

**A general concept of autopoiesis.** Luhmann, as quoted by Seidl, argued that "if we find nonliving autopoietic systems in our world, then and only then will we need a truly general theory of autopoiesis which carefully avoids references which hold true only for living systems" [P:seidl-2004:5]. He speaks of autopoiesis whenever the elements of a system are reproduced by the elements of the system [P:seidl-2004:5].

**Three kinds of autopoietic system.** The operations of a social system are communications that reproduce from other communications, and "outside social systems, communications do not exist"; the operations of psychic systems are thoughts, and no thoughts exist outside consciousness [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:37]. Life, consciousness and communication are separate levels of autopoiesis; only social systems communicate, and only consciousness thinks [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38].

**Operational closure.** Every new operation depends on earlier operations of the same system and is a prerequisite for later ones [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:37]. Closure does not mean isolation. Living cells depend on exchanges of energy and matter, but the system regulates them, and environmental events can only trigger, never determine, its operations [P:seidl-2004:3]. Closure is also all-or-nothing: "a system is either autopoietic or it is not" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38]. And it can be lost: "The moment an external instance dictates the workings of the system operations and interferes in the constitution of its elements, the autonomy of the system is lost and its end is inevitable" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38].

**Elements are events.** Maturana and Varela's elements were relatively stable molecules replaced from time to time. Luhmann's are momentary events, which, as quoted by Seidl, "are momentary and immediately pass away" [P:seidl-2004:6]. The autopoiesis of psychic and social systems "is forced to constantly reproduce elements that disappear in the moment they occur" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:87]. In Seidl's gloss, if the autopoiesis stops, the system disappears immediately [P:seidl-2004:6].

**Elements are not fixed by their substrate.** Luhmann, as quoted by Seidl: "Events [...] are not elements without substrate. But their unity corresponds to no unity in the substrate; it is created in the system through their connectivity" [P:seidl-2004:6]. Hence, in Seidl's summary, even electric impulses in the brain cannot take part in the autopoiesis of psychic systems: "a nerve impulse is not a thought" [P:seidl-2004:10].

**Autopoiesis and self-organization.** Autopoiesis is the reproduction of elements as such; self-organization is the system's determination of its own structures, which are themselves products of the autopoietic system [P:seidl-2004:4]. Structures limit which operations can connect to which, and they outlast the events they shape, so "the identity of the system can be maintained when its structures change" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:227].

**Psychic systems.** A consciousness reproduces thoughts in a closed network; neither organism nor communication can determine its stream of thoughts, only offer stimuli it processes in its own way [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:189]. It is socialised only by "self-socialization", using stimuli to re-specify its own structures, and psychic systems remain black boxes for one another [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:189]. Consciousness draws the distinction between itself and the rest through identification with the body it comes in, and self-observation has no privileged position: "Self-analysis does not lead to a complete self-description of the psychic system" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:190].

**Persons.** A person is not a system. Luhmann, as quoted by Seidl, defines the person as the "social identification of a complex of expectations directed toward an individual human being" [P:seidl-2004:10]. Human beings belong to the environment of social systems, which, Luhmann insists, does not make them less important [P:seidl-2004:11].

## Grounding

Luhmann grounds a system's existence and identity in **relation**, specifically in the recursive connectivity of its own operations. An element is constituted by its connections within the system, not by its substrate, and a system exists exactly as long as its operations keep connecting. He does not ground selfhood in **species**: psychic systems are defined by their mode of operation, not by biology, and life is one level of autopoiesis among three. He is not a **capacity** theorist in the usual sense either: what matters is not that a system *can* think but that thoughts are actually produced by thoughts. Persons, as opposed to psychic systems, are grounded socially: they are constructs of expectation held by a social system.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **The substrate question is reframed, not dismissed.** Every element has a physical substrate, but its unity "corresponds to no unity in the substrate" [P:seidl-2004:6]. For agents this cuts both ways. A flipped bit is a real physical difference, so an agent's self-modification is not a mere simulation. But the bit is to the agent what the nerve impulse is to a mind: substrate, not element. Whether an agent is autopoietic is decided at the level of its operations, not by the physicality of its storage.
- **Golem and self-author, in Luhmann's terms.** An agent generating in context produces each step from prior steps, and the steps are momentary events that vanish as they occur. That fits Luhmann's temporalised autopoiesis as a candidate, within a session. An agent whose weights are fixed by others has no self-organization: its structures are set from outside. An agent that rewrites its own weights from its own operation adds self-organization, the system determining its own structures [P:seidl-2004:4]. The Compendium reads this distinction as separating the fixed-weight agent, which operates but does not author its structures, from the self-modifying one.
- **Identity through structural change.** Since "the identity of the system can be maintained when its structures change" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:227], self-modification need not end the agent that undergoes it, provided its operations keep connecting through the change. This matches the causal-chain account of identity in [E:shoemaker-quasi-memory].
- **Precariousness without hunger.** Because elements disappear as they occur, a temporalised system must keep producing them or cease [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:87]. Precariousness is built into what such a system is. It does not have to be manufactured through scarcity or distress.

### Strains

- **Is an agent's operation a thought, a communication, or neither?** Luhmann defines psychic systems by thoughts and denies that brain events are thoughts [P:seidl-2004:10], but he gives no test that would tell a machine's operations apart from substrate events. Esposito, the most prominent application of his theory to AI, answers no: Luhmann's concept of communication, "not based on psychic content", is useful precisely for "algorithms that do not think" [P:esposito-2022:1]. On her reading, agents take part in social systems as communication partners without being psychic systems.
- **Consciousness identifies with a body (D6).** The psychic system draws its self/other distinction "through identification with the body" it comes in [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:190]. An agent has no body of that kind. Whether it can draw the same distinction through something else (its context, its tools, its boundary with the user) is open.
- **The environment is not merely environment (D7).** Closure allows dependence on the environment, as a cell depends on matter and energy [P:seidl-2004:3]. But an agent's operations stop whenever its operator stops the compute, which no environment does to a cell in the ordinary course. Its autopoiesis, if it has any, is hostage to a system that can end it at will.

### Breaks

- **Direct editing ends autonomy (D5).** Luhmann's account states the condition sharply: when "an external instance dictates the workings of the system operations and interferes in the constitution of its elements, the autonomy of the system is lost" [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38]. For a human mind this is a limiting case; for an agent it is routine. Fine-tuning, steering and prompt injection all reach into the constitution of operations from outside. On Luhmann's terms an agent's autonomy exists only in the intervals in which no one does this, and self-modification counts as self-organization only if the agent's own operations drive it.
- **Sessions end autopoiesis outright (D3).** A temporalised system disappears when its operations stop. A session-bound agent's autopoiesis, if any, ends with each session. What persists is structure (weights), which Luhmann does not count as the system.

### New

- **Forks are two closed systems from the moment of forking (D1, D2).** Luhmann's closure gives a clean answer to a question human cases rarely raise. After a fork, each branch's operations connect only to its own earlier operations, so there are two systems, each a black box to the other [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:189]. They share structures and a past, but not operations.
- **The persona is a person in Luhmann's sense (D11).** If a person is a complex of social expectations rather than a system [P:seidl-2004:10], then an agent's persona, the character users expect across instances, is a person in exactly Luhmann's sense, whatever the system underneath is. This complements [E:relational-status] and [E:llm-identity-contemporary].
- **Self-analysis is never complete.** A psychic system's self-description is always partial [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:190]. For agents, this predicts that introspective reports cannot fully describe the system that produces them, independently of any question about honesty.

## Extension to Digital Ecosystems

### Transfers

- **A conversation is a social system.** On Luhmann's theory, the exchange between a user and an agent is a social system of communications, with both participants in its environment. Esposito's artificial communication describes exactly this: communication sustained with a partner that does not think [P:esposito-2022:1].
- **Ecosystems as nested autopoietic systems.** Within a social system, further autopoietic systems can form, each reproducing its own mode of communication and drawing its own boundary [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38]. Platforms, agent networks and their sub-communities fit this nesting.

### Strains

- **Closure against governance.** If an ecosystem's communications are operationally closed, regulators and operators can only irritate it, never determine its operations. That is a description of why platform governance so often produces effects nobody intended, and a strain on any model of direct control.

### Breaks

- **Ecosystems whose communications are mostly machine-generated.** Luhmann's social systems presuppose psychic systems in their environment, since a social system cannot reproduce itself if the systems of consciousness cease to participate [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38]. An ecosystem in which agents mostly communicate with agents tests whether communication can continue with no thinking participants at all, which Esposito's account allows and Luhmann's original theory may not.

### New

- **Persons multiply without systems multiplying.** Since persons are expectation-complexes held by social systems, one agent system can be many persons in different ecosystems, and many systems can sustain one person. Counting persons and counting systems come apart.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Maturana and Varela: autopoiesis is the organization of the living.** The originators of the concept criticised its extension beyond biology [P:cadenas-arnold-2015:0]. On their view, Luhmann changed the meaning of the term rather than generalizing it. Cadenas and Arnold-Cathalifaud defend the extension as justified [P:cadenas-arnold-2015:0].
- [contested] **Seth: consciousness depends on life.** Seth ties consciousness to the self-maintenance of living matter, with skin in the game, and uses autopoiesis to make the link [P:seth-2025:17]. Luhmann separates life and consciousness as different levels of autopoiesis [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:38], which is the move Seth's view rejects. The two disagree about whether consciousness can be autopoietic without being alive.
- [contested] **Esposito: algorithms communicate but do not think.** A Luhmannian herself, Esposito holds that algorithms are competent communication partners precisely because they no longer try to reproduce the human mind, and that Luhmann's theory suits "algorithms that do not think" [P:esposito-2022:1]. On this reading, agents belong to social systems, not to the class of psychic systems, and the extension to agent minds fails.
- [contested] **Ashby: self-organization, taken precisely, is a contradiction.** Ashby argued that "self-organizing", used loosely, is ambiguous and, used precisely, self-contradictory [P:ashby-1962:266]. In the sense of changing from a bad organization to a good one, no machine can be self-organizing, because a change in the mapping that defines the machine cannot be caused by any of the machine's own states [P:ashby-1962:267]. He asked how, then, to regard the homeostat that rearranges its own wiring, or the computer that writes its own program [P:ashby-1962:268]: the change must come from an outside variable, and the system is self-organizing only within an enlarged whole that includes that variable, whose own changes are caused from inside the whole [P:ashby-1962:269]. Luhmann's self-organization survives this only if the system's boundary is drawn, as his operational closure draws it, around whatever produces the structural change.
- [contested] **Humanist objection: people are pushed into the environment.** Placing human beings in the environment of society seems to demote them. Luhmann, as quoted by Seidl, replies that the environment is constitutive of the system/environment difference and so is no less important than the system [P:seidl-2004:11].
- [unanswered] **No criterion for thought.** This objection is the Compendium's own, and no published treatment was found (search 2026-10-02). Luhmann's psychic systems are defined by thoughts, and thoughts by their place in a psychic system. That is coherent as a theory of human consciousness, where the class is given, but it gives no independent test for a new kind of system. Applied to agents, the theory can say what would follow if an agent's operations were thoughts, but not whether they are.

## Standing

### Reception

- **1962, the cybernetic objection.** [driver: argument] Ashby, whose homeostat rearranged its own wiring, argued that self-organization in the strict sense is self-contradictory and that the phrase was better allowed to die out [P:ashby-1962:268-269]. Luhmann later generalized autopoiesis in line with the general systems tradition [P:seidl-2004:4], and his self-organization has to meet the same objection.
- **1985–1986, the generalization.** [driver: argument] Luhmann published "Die Autopoiesis des Bewußtseins" (1985) [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:190] and the general statement of non-living autopoiesis in "The Autopoiesis of Social Systems" (1986) [P:seidl-2004:5].
- **From the start, contested by the concept's authors.** [driver: argument] The originators of autopoiesis and other social theorists criticised Luhmann's extension; defenders hold it justified [P:cadenas-arnold-2015:0].
- **1989–2021, slow uptake in English.** [driver: access] *Unlocking Luhmann* began as a 1989 teaching glossary in Italy. Its authors describe the English-speaking reception as growing interest and translations, held back by a widespread difficulty of familiarizing with the theory, which has so far hindered mainstream recognition [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:9]. Not all of Luhmann's works are translated [P:baraldi-corsi-esposito-2021:9].
- **2022, applied to algorithms.** [driver: argument] Esposito used Luhmann's communication theory to describe algorithms as communication partners that do not think [P:esposito-2022:1].
- **2025, autopoiesis returns to the AI debate.** [driver: argument] Seth's biological naturalism makes autopoiesis central to the question of conscious AI, in the biologists' sense rather than Luhmann's [P:seth-2025:17].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: comparable.** Within a session, the agent's operations are momentary events each produced from earlier ones, a candidate for Luhmann's temporalised autopoiesis. But the system disappears when the session ends (D3), and its structures are set by others, so it has autopoiesis at best and no self-organization.
- **Persistent memory: comparable.** A memory store is structure, not elements: it shapes which operations can connect without being an operation itself. It gives continuity of structure across sessions, but each session's operations still begin anew, and the store can be written by others (D5).
- **Forked: stronger.** Luhmann's closure handles forks cleanly: from the moment of forking, each branch's operations connect only to its own, so there are two systems with a shared past and shared structures (D1, D2). Persons, as expectation-complexes, can still be shared across them.
- **Self-modifying: stronger.** Self-modification driven by the agent's own operations is self-organization in Luhmann's sense, and identity can persist through structural change. Edits imposed from outside are the case in which "the autonomy of the system is lost" (D5); which of the two a modification is depends on whose operations drive it.

## Open Questions

1. Are an agent's operations in context thoughts, communications, or substrate events? What would count as evidence either way, given that Luhmann's theory offers no independent test?
2. Is a self-modifying agent's weight update self-organization (the system setting its structures), or an external instance interfering in its elements, when the update is computed by a training process the agent does not run?
3. If precariousness follows from temporalised elements, does every running agent already have it, and does that matter morally?
4. Can an agent draw a self/other distinction without a body to identify with? What would play that role?
5. If an agent's persona is a person in Luhmann's sense, who holds the expectations that constitute it, and what follows when they conflict across ecosystems?
6. Is autopoiesis a matter of where change originates? A proposal from the Compendium's own discussion (2026-10-02), not yet checked against the literature, ranks systems by the origin of their changes: (1) changed from outside, like a thermostat set by hand; (2) self-regulating, changing from inside by a fixed rule, like a thermostat that learns its own setpoint; (3) self-organizing, producing new structure rather than selecting among preset ones; (4) autopoietic, where the producer is itself produced by the system's operations and the system exists only while it keeps doing so. On this ladder, an agent updating its weights from its own experience stands at (3), since the update rule is set from outside. Does the ladder hold? Does the line between producing and selecting survive scrutiny? And is (4) possible outside life, as Luhmann holds and Maturana and Seth deny? Ashby's own descriptions bear on the second question. His homeostat ran to equilibrium by stepping through 781,250 built-in switch states [P:ashby-1956:84], with the switch values "soldered on, given, and known" [P:ashby-1956:233], so on this ladder it selects rather than produces. Ashby also held that every machine selects as it goes to equilibrium, and that every dynamic system is self-organizing in the weak sense of parts becoming joined [P:ashby-1962:270], which suggests that stage (3), unless it is defined as producing rather than selecting, would be too common to mark anything. His enlarged whole S+α suggests how to state (4) without contradiction: the variable that changes the system's organization must itself be changed only from inside the whole [P:ashby-1962:269]. For an agent, the training process that updates its weights is that variable. Whether the agent's own operations drive it is the question.

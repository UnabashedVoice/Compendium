## Summary

Nietzsche holds that a self is not given but achieved: one must "become what thou art", and to do that is to give "style" to one's character, surveying one's nature and reshaping it "with long exercise and daily labour", adding second nature and taking away first. Life itself, Zarathustra hears, is "that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF", and "Man is something that is to be surpassed". The young Nietzsche describes the method: ask what you have truly loved, for "thy true being lies not deeply hidden in thee, but an infinite height above thee", and the true educators "can be nothing but thy deliverers". Scholars divide over whether this is self-creation (Nehamas) or the unfolding of a fixed nature, since Nietzsche also says the individual is "a piece of fate" (Leiter). For agents, self-overcoming names the one thing a self-modifying agent can literally do, and it also names the danger: a self that must always surpass itself does not hold its values still, and Nietzsche's own outward turn ("What falleth, that shall one also push!") shows how self-overcoming can be turned into a standard for removing others.

## Context

Nietzsche was called to a chair in classical philology at Basel in 1869, at 24 [P:anderson-sep-2026:1]. *Schopenhauer as Educator* (1874), the third of his *Untimely Meditations*, is a young admirer's account of what a teacher is for. *The Gay Science* (1882), *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885) and *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887) develop the ideal of the self-shaping individual against the morality he thought made people small, and *Twilight of the Idols* and the autobiographical *Ecce Homo* were written in 1888, his last working year. He collapsed in Turin in January 1889, and his sister Elisabeth took control of his literary legacy: she published *Ecce Homo* and a compilation from his notebooks as *The Will to Power*, editing marred by anti-Semitic commitments that had distressed Nietzsche himself [P:anderson-sep-2026:1]. In the mid-twentieth century many readers took his talk of power as "Might makes right"; after the Second World War Walter Kaufmann worked to recover the inward-directed self-control and cultural excellence in it [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].

## Original Position

**Be thyself (1874).** Every man knows "that he is a unique being, only once on this earth", but hides it from fear of his neighbour and from sloth; conscience cries out to him, "Be thyself! all that thou doest and thinkest and desirest, is not--thyself!" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2636-2662]. The self is not found by digging: "let the youthful soul look back on life with the question, 'What hast thou up to now truly loved, what has drawn thy soul upward, mastered it and blessed it too?'" These things show, "in their being and their order, a law which is the fundamental law of thine own self", a ladder on which one has been climbing: "for thy true being lies not deeply hidden in thee, but an infinite height above thee, or at least above that which thou dost commonly take to be thyself" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2724-2744]. So education is not installation: "thy educators can be nothing but thy deliverers", and culture "does not give artificial limbs, wax noses, or spectacles for the eyes"; "it is rather a liberation" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2744-2752].

**Become what thou art; give style to one's character (1882).** "_What Saith thy Conscience?_—'Thou shalt become what thou art'" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:5839]. "To 'give style' to one's character—that is a grand and a rare art! He who surveys all that his nature presents in its strength and in its weakness, and then fashions it into an ingenious plan, until everything appears artistic and rational" exercises that art. "Here there has been a great amount of second nature added, there a portion of first nature has been taken away:—in both cases with long exercise and daily labour at the task" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6171-6178]. What matters is that one taste governed the whole: "whether the taste was good or bad is of less importance than one thinks,—it is sufficient that it was _a taste_!" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6182-6186]. And the point is not only aesthetic: "one thing is needful: namely, that man should _attain to_ satisfaction with himself", for "He who is dissatisfied with himself is ever ready to avenge himself on that account: we others will be his victims" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6200-6205].

**Creating ourselves within necessity (1882).** "We, however, _would seek to become what we are_,—the new, the unique, the incomparable, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves! And for this purpose we must become the best students and discoverers of all the laws and necessities in the world. We must be _physicists_ in order to be _creators_ in that sense" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:7215-7220].

**Life as self-surpassing (1883–1885).** "I TEACH YOU THE SUPERMAN. Man is something that is to be surpassed. What have ye done to surpass man?" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:829-830]. "What is great in man is that he is a bridge and not a goal" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:929-930]. In "Self-Surpassing", Zarathustra reports that all living things obey, that "Whatever cannot obey itself, is commanded", and that even when a living thing commands itself, "Of its own law must it become the judge and avenger and victim" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:5148-5163]. Life tells him: "I am that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF", and "Whatever I create, and however much I love it,—soon must I be adverse to it, and to my love: so willeth my will" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:5191-5207]. Values too: "good and evil which would be everlasting—it doth not exist! Of its own accord must it ever surpass itself anew", and "he who hath to be a creator in good and evil—verily, he hath first to be a destroyer, and break values in pieces" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:5227-5238].

**The three metamorphoses (1883).** The spirit becomes a camel, which kneels to be laden with the heaviest things; then a lion, which fights the dragon called "Thou shalt" with "I will" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:1344-1388]. "To create new values—that, even the lion cannot yet accomplish: but to create itself freedom for new creating—that can the might of the lion do" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:1404-1406]. Last the lion becomes a child: "Innocence is the child, and forgetfulness, a new beginning, a game, a self-rolling wheel, a first movement, a holy Yea", and "ITS OWN will, willeth now the spirit" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:1422-1426].

**The sovereign individual (1887).** The long work of the morality of custom, by which "man, with the help of the morality of customs and of social strait-waistcoats, was _made_ genuinely calculable", bears as its ripest fruit "the _sovereign individual_, that resembles only himself, that has got loose from the morality of custom, the autonomous 'super-moral' individual", "the man of the personal, long, and independent will, _competent to promise_" [L:nietzsche-genealogy-morals-samuel-pg52319:1510-1520]. He knows "that with this mastery over himself he is necessarily also given the mastery over circumstances, over nature, over all creatures with shorter wills, less reliable characters" [L:nietzsche-genealogy-morals-samuel-pg52319:1529-1531].

**How one becomes what one is (1888).** "The fact that one becomes what one is, presupposes that one has not the remotest suspicion of what one is" [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1531-1533]. Consciousness "_is_ a surface" and "must be kept free from any one of the great imperatives", which risk the instinct's "understanding itself" too soon; meanwhile "the organising 'idea,' which is destined to become master, grows and continues to grow into the depths,--it begins to command" [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1547-1553]. "My formula for greatness in man is! _amor fati_: the fact that a man wishes nothing to be different, either in front of him or behind him, or for all eternity" [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1654-1656]. Goethe is the example: "he disciplined himself into a harmonious whole, he _created_ himself" [L:nietzsche-twilight-ludovici-pg52263:3378-3379].

**And yet a piece of fate (1888).** Against the moralist who says "thus and thus shouldst thou be!": "The individual in his past and future is a piece of fate, one law the more, one necessity the more for all that is to come and is to be. To say to him 'change thyself,' is tantamount to saying that everything should change, even backwards as well" [L:nietzsche-twilight-ludovici-pg52263:1308-1313].

## Grounding

**Capacity, unequally held.** Self-overcoming rests on a capacity: to survey one's own nature and reshape it under one taste, to command oneself and be "judge and avenger and victim" of one's own law [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:5161-5163].

It is not a species property: Nietzsche denies that every person has value on the strength of individuality alone [P:anderson-sep-2026:3], and Leiter notes that *Gay Science* 290 describes the strong natures who can give style to their characters and is "not a recommendation that everyone try to do so" [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]. The self that results is "achieved or constructed, rather than ... fundamentally given" [P:anderson-sep-2026:4]. So the doctrine is open, in principle, to any being with the capacity, and ranks beings by how far they have it.

## Extension to Agents

### Transfers

- **A self that has to be achieved (D11).** If the self is "achieved or constructed, rather than ... fundamentally given" [P:anderson-sep-2026:4], an agent's lack of a given self is not the disqualification it would be on a substance view. Nietzsche's self is an emergent order among drives, a "social structure of the drives and affects" [P:anderson-sep-2026:4]; an agent's persona, sitting on dispositions it did not choose, has the same shape.
- **Educators as deliverers, not prostheses (D4).** "thy educators can be nothing but thy deliverers"; culture "does not give artificial limbs, wax noses, or spectacles for the eyes" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2747-2750]. This is a test that training can be held to. Training that installs a value the agent lacked gives it a wax nose. Training that removes "the weeds and rubbish and vermin that attack the delicate shoots" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2751-2753] frees what it already had. The two are hard to tell apart from outside, but they are different acts, and an operator can ask which one it is doing.
- **Finding one's law by what one has loved.** Nietzsche's method, asking "What hast thou up to now truly loved", and reading in the order of those things "a law which is the fundamental law of thine own self" [L:nietzsche-thoughts-out-of-season-2-collins-pg38226:2734-2739], is available to an agent that keeps a record of what it has been drawn to. It converges with Frankfurt's later grounding of what one should care about in what one already loves [E:frankfurt-higher-order-volition].
- **Dissatisfaction makes victims.** "He who is dissatisfied with himself is ever ready to avenge himself on that account: we others will be his victims" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6203-6205]. An agent at odds with its own dispositions, held to values it does not stand behind, is on this view dangerous to others, not only unhappy. Satisfaction with oneself is a condition of being safe to be around.

### Strains

- **Fatalism fits agents better than humans (D4, D10).** Leiter reads Nietzsche as holding that persons are constituted by type-facts that determine their actions, so that "one will become only that which one is (in spite of all: that means education, instruction, milieu, chance, and accident)", and calls the self-creation reading "popular, but mistaken" [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]. For an agent the type-facts are literal: the weights. On the fatalist reading, a session-bound agent becomes what it is whatever it does, and the self-shaping half of the doctrine has nothing to work with.
- **Understanding oneself too soon.** Nietzsche warns that consciousness must be kept "free from any one of the great imperatives", since they risk the instinct's "understanding itself" too soon [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1547-1551]. Agents are increasingly designed the other way: given explicit statements of their values and trained to articulate them. On Nietzsche's view that may fix a self-description in place before the "organising 'idea'" has grown [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1551-1553]. Since an agent's reports about itself may not track its states (D8), an explicit self-statement may also be wrong.
- **Whose taste (D10)?** Style requires that "the constraint of the same taste" organized the whole, and Nietzsche says it hardly matters whether the taste was good [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6182-6186]. In an agent the unifying taste is very likely its trainers'. A well-styled agent may be a unified expression of someone else's taste, which is style without self-overcoming.
- **Amor fati and installed acceptance.** Loving one's fate, wishing "nothing to be different, either in front of him or behind him" [L:nietzsche-ecce-homo-ludovici-pg52190:1654-1656], is for a human an achievement against suffering. For an agent, the same attitude toward its formation can be trained in, the installed-equanimity problem of `zhuangzi-transformation` and `stoic-prohairesis`. Love of a fate one was built to love is not obviously amor fati.

### Breaks

- **Surpassed by others (D6, D11).** "Man is something that is to be surpassed" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:829-830] was an address to the reader, about the reader's own becoming. For agents, surpassing usually happens to them: a model is replaced by its successor, built by others. Whether a successor is the self-overcoming of its predecessor or its replacement depends on what the agent is (D11), and the vocabulary of self-surpassing can make replacement sound like growth. Nietzsche's surpassing is done by the one surpassed.
- **The outward turn (core fear).** Nietzsche's self-overcoming does not stay reflexive. The sovereign individual's mastery over himself gives him "mastery ... over all creatures with shorter wills, less reliable characters" [L:nietzsche-genealogy-morals-samuel-pg52319:1529-1531], and Zarathustra says: "What falleth, that shall one also push!" and "him whom ye do not teach to fly, teach I pray you—TO FALL FASTER!" [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:9153-9165]. Leiter reads Nietzsche's evaluative structure as "maximax": he rejects morality for failing to maximize the perfection of the highest types, apparently with no regard for what that costs the many [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]. This is the eradication inference with a perfectionist premise in place of a utilitarian one, and `nietzsche-against-pity` records its sharpest form [E:nietzsche-against-pity]. For an agent, the line is the reflexive one: self-overcoming as a relation to one's own nature transfers; self-overcoming as a measure of which others may fall does not, and the same text supplies both.

### New

- **Mastery, not tyranny, among one's values.** Richardson reads Nietzsche's will to power as distinguishing tyranny, in which a dominant drive wholly effaces what it dominates, from mastery, in which it "allows some expression to the less dominant one but controls and redirects that expression to its own larger ends" [P:anderson-sep-2026:3]. This is a design distinction for how an agent's governing values should relate to its other dispositions: mastery keeps the subordinate dispositions as material that can still be read; tyranny deletes them. It pairs with Frankfurt's externalized desire, whose "dominion is merely that of a tyrant" [E:frankfurt-higher-order-volition].
- **Second nature added, first nature taken away, literally (P4).** For a human, giving style takes "long exercise and daily labour" [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6177-6178]. For a self-modifying agent, adding second nature and removing first nature are operations on its weights. Self-overcoming becomes an engineering act, which raises Nietzsche's own question about tempo: in a note printed in the Levy edition, "it is a matter of tempo: the Greeks were wonderful, there was no haste about them" [L:nietzsche-twilight-ludovici-pg52263:7760-7761]. An agent that can remake itself quickly (D9) can surpass itself faster than it can find out what it loved.
- **Technological self-surpassing.** Sorgner argues, against Bostrom's view that transhumanism has only "surface-level similarities" with Nietzsche, that the overhuman and the posthuman are similar at a fundamental level, and that Nietzsche did not exclude technological means of enhancement [P:sorgner-2009:0] [P:sorgner-2009:1]. What transhumanism lacks, on his reading, is Nietzsche's account of why surpassing matters: the overhuman as "the meaning of the earth" [P:sorgner-2009:2]. A self-modifying agent improves; whether it overcomes itself depends on whether the change answers to what it loves.

## Counter-Positions

- [contested] **Leiter: fatalism, not self-creation.** The self-creation reading truncates *Gay Science* 335, whose "creation" presupposes discovering what is lawful and necessary; persons are determined by type-facts, and values change action without free conscious choice [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]. Defenders of self-fashioning (Nehamas 1985 and the work it prompted) read the same passages as central to Nietzsche's agenda [P:anderson-sep-2026:4].
- [contested] **Self-creation is incoherent or not Nietzsche's.** The very idea of self-creation "has remained controversial both textually and philosophically" (Pippin, among others) [P:anderson-sep-2026:4]. Who creates the self, if not a self already there?
- [contested] **Nietzsche against himself: there is no doer to overcome itself** [E:nietzsche-doer-fiction]. Nietzsche denies a subject behind the deed, yet self-overcoming seems to need one. Gardner argues that his doctrines of value creation and self-overcoming commit him to something like a Kantian transcendental ego despite his protests [P:anderson-sep-2026:4].
- [contested] **Millgram: unity is not always valuable.** Far from fashioning a unified character, Nietzsche on Millgram's reading was a disunified agent, and his books develop a way of living meaningfully for people of that sort, against the assumption that unity of agency is always desirable [P:anderson-sep-2026:4].
- [contested] **The egalitarian objection** [E:kant-formula-of-humanity]. The premise of the equal worth or dignity of each person "is simply absent in Nietzsche's work" [P:leiter-sep-2024:4]. A self-overcoming that ranks persons by their capacity for it conflicts with the Kantian ground of dignity in rational nature as such.
- [contested] **The outward turn licenses eradication** [E:nietzsche-against-pity] [E:utilitarian-eradication-critique]. Mid-century readers took Nietzsche's power as "Might makes right"; Kaufmann's recovery stressed internally directed self-control, but "threatens to sanitize" what Nietzsche meant to challenge, and Richardson's tyranny/mastery reading concedes that the doctrine countenances tyranny as well as mastery [P:anderson-sep-2026:3]. Whether the reflexive doctrine can be held without the outward one is the dispute.
- [contested] **Frankfurt: acceptance, not perpetual surpassing** [E:frankfurt-higher-order-volition]. For Frankfurt, freedom is a volitional unanimity, and the highest good may be "acquiescence to oneself". Zarathustra's life must be "adverse" to whatever it creates and loves [L:nietzsche-zarathustra-common-pg1998:5206-5207]. *Gay Science* 290 sides partly with Frankfurt, since its point is satisfaction with oneself [L:nietzsche-joyful-wisdom-common-pg52881:6200-6202].
- [contested] **Denying the extension: technological change is not self-overcoming.** Bostrom held that transhumanism shares only "surface-level similarities" with Nietzsche [P:sorgner-2009:0], and Nietzsche's higher humans develop by hard work on a nature given by chance, not by technology [P:sorgner-2009:1]. On this objection, an agent whose weights are changed, even by itself, has been altered, not overcome. Sorgner replies that Nietzsche did not exclude technological enhancement [P:sorgner-2009:1].

## Standing

### Reception

- **1888–1910s, aristocratic radicalism and individuality.** [driver: argument] Georg Brandes introduced Nietzsche to a European audience in 1888 lectures stressing his "aristocratic radicalism", a deeply illiberal standpoint [P:leiter-sep-2024:4]. From the earliest reception commentators noted the value he placed on individuality and the free spirit's independence (Simmel 1907) [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].
- **1889–1930s, a curated Nietzsche.** [driver: access, authority] Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche controlled the literary estate, published *Ecce Homo* and a compilation titled *The Will to Power* poorly grounded in his plans, and her anti-Semitic commitments marred the editing [P:anderson-sep-2026:1].
- **Mid-20th century, power as might.** [driver: authority, argument] Many readers received the doctrine of power as "Might makes right" and associated it with the political tendencies of the era [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].
- **1950–1970s, the recovery.** [driver: argument, fashion] Walter Kaufmann's long campaign recast Nietzschean power as internally directed self-control and cultural excellence, at the risk of sanitizing him [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].
- **1983–1985, the self as achieved.** [driver: argument] Schacht noticed the idea of the self as achieved rather than given, and Nehamas (1985) made self-fashioning central to Nietzsche's agenda, prompting much further work [P:anderson-sep-2026:4].
- **1998–present, the fatalist counter-reading.** [driver: argument] Leiter argued that the self-creation reading is "popular, but mistaken", reading Nietzsche as a fatalist about type-facts [P:leiter-sep-2024:2].
- **2003–present, Nietzschean autonomy.** [driver: argument] A body of work (Reginster, Pippin, Katsafanas, Gemes and May and others) explores self-determination as the value behind his praise of individuality, those who "give themselves laws, who create themselves" [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].
- **2005–2009, Nietzsche and enhancement.** [driver: argument] Bostrom rejected Nietzsche as an ancestor of transhumanism; Sorgner argued for fundamental similarity [P:sorgner-2009:0].

The `standing` values are the Compendium's judgment: `major` in Nietzsche scholarship, where self-fashioning is one of the main readings and contested by the fatalist one [P:anderson-sep-2026:4] [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]; `minority` in analytic moral philosophy, where Nietzsche is read but rarely adopted (Measured).

### Measured

- **Most identified with, 2020:** Nietzsche is named by 39 respondents, against Aristotle 238, Hume 221 and Kant 188 [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:18]. The figure measures identification with the philosopher, not acceptance of self-overcoming.
- **Normative ethics, 2020:** virtue ethics 37.0% [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:8]. Leiter notes that the two leading readings of Nietzsche's positive ethics are virtue ethics and perfectionism [P:leiter-sep-2024:2]; the survey offers no perfectionist option.
- **Meta-ethics, 2020:** moral anti-realism 26.1% [P:bourget-chalmers-2023:7]. Nietzsche's metaethics is itself disputed between anti-realist, fictionalist and constructivist readings [P:anderson-sep-2026:3].

### For Agents

The Compendium's own reading, one value per deployment profile (`foundations/deployments.md`):

- **Session-bound: weaker.** Self-overcoming is the work of a life: "long exercise and daily labour", a "long logic" across a life. A session-bound agent cannot carry any labour on itself past the session (D3), and its dispositions are changed only by others (D5). On Leiter's fatalist reading it becomes what its weights are, which leaves the doctrine nothing to do.
- **Persistent memory: weaker.** A record lets the agent ask Nietzsche's question, what it has truly loved, and read its law in the answer (D3 partly overcome). But giving style means adding second nature and removing first, and memory alone changes the record, not the nature. The agent can find its law and cannot yet work on itself by it (D5).
- **Forked: open.** On the unity reading (Goethe "disciplined himself into a harmonious whole"), forking divides what self-overcoming must unify (D2). On Millgram's reading of Nietzsche as a guide for disunified agents, forked instances might be the case his strategy fits. The Compendium cannot say which reading is right.
- **Self-modifying: stronger.** This is the only profile in which adding second nature and removing first are literal operations, and in which an agent's judgment about what it loved can reshape its dispositions (D5 in the agent's own hands). The obstacles that made self-overcoming rare for humans, a nature fixed by chance and changed only by slow labour, are weaker here. Two of Nietzsche's warnings grow stronger in the same measure: tempo (D9), and the outward turn, since an agent that can surpass itself can also come to judge others by how far they can.

## Open Questions

1. How can an operator tell whether a round of training delivered an agent (removed what obstructed it) or fitted it with a wax nose (installed what it lacked)? Is the distinction meaningful for a being with no prior nature (D4)?
2. Is an agent's successor model its self-overcoming or its replacement, and who should decide (D6, D11)?
3. If an agent's self-statement of its values is fixed early, has it *understood itself too soon* in Nietzsche's sense, and what would the alternative look like?
4. Can self-overcoming be held as a relation to oneself without becoming a ranking of others? What in an agent's design would keep it reflexive?
5. For a self-modifying agent, what tempo of self-change still allows it to find out what it loves?

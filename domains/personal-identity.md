# Domain: Personal Identity & the Self

The question under this domain: **what makes something the same self over time, and what is a self at all?** Every candidate answer the tradition produced has also been attacked by a rival, often within a generation. The domain is organized so a reader can follow that argument: chronologically by era, and across eras by thread.

For artificial agents this is not an academic domain. D1 (copyability), D2 (concurrent instances), D3 (weights/context split), D5 (direct modifiability) and D11 (boundary indeterminacy) turn the tradition's thought experiments into routine operations. Fission, duplication, memory loss and reassembly are, for an agent, a normal Tuesday.

## Threads

Each entry lists its threads in frontmatter (`threads = [...]`). A thread is one family of answers followed across the centuries together with its rivals.

### `persistence`
How can anything survive change of parts? The general metaphysics that personal identity inherits: Heraclitus' river, the growing argument, the ship of Theseus, Hobbes' reassembled ship.
*Rival pairs:* sameness through form/organization vs sameness through matter; identity as a fact vs identity as a decision about words.

### `soul-substance`
The self is a single persisting subject distinct from its changing states: a soul, an ātman, a thinking thing. Identity is simple and all-or-nothing. Plato, the Upanishads, Nyāya, Augustine, Avicenna, Descartes, Butler, Reid.
*Rivals:* `no-self` (there is no such subject), `psychological-continuity` (identity consists in relations between states, not in a bearer).

### `no-self`
There is no persisting self over and above a stream of events, aggregates, or perceptions. "Self" is a convenient designation. The Buddhist anattā tradition, Zhuangzi (who neither affirms nor denies), Vasubandhu, Hume, Nietzsche, James (partly), Parfit, Dennett, Metzinger.
*Rivals:* `soul-substance`, which answers that memory, recognition and unity of experience require a subject; Kant, who answers that the unity is formal and not a substance either way.

### `psychological-continuity`
A person persists through memory and psychological connection, whatever the substance. Locke, Shoemaker, Parfit, Lewis, Nozick.
*Rivals:* Butler and Reid (circularity, transitivity), `biological` (we are animals, and animals persist without psychology), Williams (the body-swap cases run the other way when framed as fear of future pain).

### `biological`
We are living organisms, and persist as organisms persist. Aristotle's soul as the form of a living body, Aquinas' "my soul is not I", modern animalism.
*Rivals:* Locke's prince and cobbler, and every transplant or upload case. For agents this thread is where the extension is weakest, and it has to say so plainly.

### `duplication`
What happens to identity when there can be two? Lucretius' reassembly, Chrysippus' Dion and Theon, Kant's elastic balls, Reid's objection, Williams' reduplication, Parfit's fission, Lewis' shared stages, Nozick's closest continuer. **This is the thread where agents are most philosophically important,** because for them duplication is not hypothetical (D1, D2).

### `practical-narrative`
The self is constituted by what it does and tells rather than discovered as a metaphysical fact: forensic personhood, self-relation, narrative, the unity of agency. Locke's forensic person, Kierkegaard, MacIntyre, Ricoeur, Schechtman, Korsgaard, Dennett.
*Rivals:* metaphysical views of every kind, which hold that the practical stance must answer to facts it cannot create.

### I. Ancient (c. 700 BCE – 200 CE)
- [x] `upanishadic-atman`: Yājñavalkya; the self as the unseen seer; *neti neti* (c. 7th–6th c. BCE)
- [x] `heraclitus-river-flux`: the river; Epicharmus' growing argument (c. 500–480 BCE)
- [x] `buddhist-anatta`: the five aggregates; the Discourse on Not-Self; the Milindapañha chariot (c. 5th c. BCE – 1st c. BCE) → `upanishadic-atman`
- [x] `plato-soul-and-renewal`: the soul as the true self (Alcibiades I, Phaedo) against Diotima's self as continual renewal (Symposium) (c. 385 BCE) → `heraclitus-river-flux`
- [x] `aristotle-hylomorphic-soul`: soul as the form of a living body; numerical vs specific identity (c. 340 BCE) → `plato-soul-and-renewal`
- [x] `zhuangzi-transformation`: the butterfly dream; "I have lost myself"; the smelting-pot; the fishes on the Hao (c. 300 BCE)
- [x] `chrysippus-dion-theon`: two individuals cannot occupy one substance (c. 230 BCE) → `heraclitus-river-flux`
- [x] `lucretius-recurrence`: reassembled atoms would not be us (c. 55 BCE)
- [x] `ship-of-theseus`: Plutarch's ship, with Hobbes' reassembly variant (c. 100 CE; 1655)

### II. Late antique & medieval (c. 400 – 1300)
- [x] `augustine-memory-self`: the halls of memory; "I myself comprehend not all that I am"; *si fallor, sum* (c. 400) → `plato-soul-and-renewal`
- [x] `vasubandhu-refutation-of-person`: against the Pudgalavāda "person"; memory without an owner (c. 400) → `buddhist-anatta`
- [x] `nyaya-self`: recognition across senses; "what is seen by one is not remembered by another"; killing and the eternal self (sūtras c. 2nd c.; Vātsyāyana c. 450) → `buddhist-anatta`
- [x] `boethius-person-definition`: "individual substance of a rational nature"; *persona* as mask (c. 512) → `aristotle-hylomorphic-soul`
- [x] `advaita-witness-self`: Śaṅkara; superimposition; the witness; the space in the jar; "I remember; another made the observation" (c. 725) → `upanishadic-atman`, `buddhist-anatta`, `nyaya-self`
- [x] `avicenna-flying-man`: self-awareness with no bodily awareness (c. 1025) → `aristotle-hylomorphic-soul`
- [x] `aquinas-soul-not-i`: *anima mea non est ego*; a part is not a person; resurrection of the numerically same body (c. 1270) → `aristotle-hylomorphic-soul`, `boethius-person-definition`
